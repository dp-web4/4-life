## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: clean, calm, one sentence I can parse ("A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules"). The "Trust, built into the internet" line is a slogan but the paragraph under it is concrete. I like that it tells me what order to read in.
- The "onramp" cards: I understood "substrate + three scales" after a second read. "Named for the Greek goddess of the hearth" and "As in hardware-bound" are the two best sentences on the page; the names made sense instantly.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".
- Confusion points: the status paragraph says "The hub is pilot-ready" but the Hub card says a hub "turns a group into a sovereign Web4 society" in the present tense, as if that happens today. Minor; I kept going.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (/tldr) (02:30)
- This page did its job. Web2 / Web3 / Web4 as "platform declares" / "key holder is assumed" / "witnessed behavior" is the clearest framing on the site. I can repeat it.
- The badge key tripped me. It defines **Spec** as "written down and stable, *not yet built here*" and then one paragraph later says "the core standard below is both Spec and Reference". If Spec means not built, a thing that is built cannot also be Spec. I think they mean "Spec" as "has a written spec" but that is not what the definition says.
- "The three run in that order" took me a beat; "the three" is the badges, not the pieces.
- "Your own device is your day-zero witness" was a nice touch; it pre-empted my "who witnesses the first person" question.
- Unanswered here: what happens to a witness who signs something false? "Staking your own reputation" is asserted but never cashed out anywhere I read.
- Clicked: "Why Web4?" (following the recommended order).

### Why Web4? (/why-web4) (07:00)
- The "Problems You Already Know" section is the most persuasive writing on the site. Five problems, five root causes, I nodded at every one.
- "Why previous solutions failed" being fair to each one ("what it gets right") made me trust the page more.
- The four idea cards were readable. Then the "All four, in one moment" box contradicted card 4 on the same screen: card 4 says "**Everyone stays visible** - what changes is weight", the box says "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply **at all** - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread". Those are opposite claims about whether strangers see things, and I cannot tell which one Web4 does.
- Same box: "fractions of a cent of energy". Cents? Two pages later the ATP page insists ATP has "no price against money". So which is it? If it has no price, "fractions of a cent" is meaningless; if it is fractions of a cent, it has a price.
- The page says "Four ideas" and goes out of its way to say CI is "not a fifth mechanism". The very next page in the reading path (How It Works) says "five foundational mechanisms" and "Five systems, in plain English". Reading them back to back, I felt corrected twice in opposite directions.
- FAQ: I read "Is this deployed?", "Who builds this?", "What backs ATP?", "Shut out without a chip?", "When does an agent die?". Honest tone throughout. Two things stuck:
  - "Is this deployed?" lists **federation** under "Reference implementations: tested code covering ... federation" and "System integration: ... federation consensus". Three other pages (Running Now, the onramp map, Hestia) say federation "remains **Spec only**, specified but **not yet built**". One of these is wrong.
  - "Who builds this? Why anonymous?" says it is "not a company or academic lab" and the anonymity is intentional. Running Now and the Hub page then say "the **lab** that builds Hestia" and that "its members and roles are published at 4-lab.io/fleet". I clicked that link (see below).
- 72 questions is a lot. The topic index helped; I would not have scrolled it otherwise.

### First Contact (/first-contact) (22:00)
- Alice's story is the best teaching device on the site. Costs, trust numbers, a mistake, a recovery, a death, a rebirth. I followed every step.
- Small arithmetic things I noticed because the page invites you to watch the numbers:
  - Step 2 says trust jumps to **0.55**; the Arc map under the button says Act 2 ends at **0.56**.
  - Alice dies at trust **0.51** and is reborn at **0.54**. Dying raised her trust. The page admits "how much trust carries ... is not settled" and that another page models it falling, but no page I found gives any reason for it to *rise*.
- The "What triggers death?" box is dense but I got it after two reads: crossing 0.5 restricts, staying below kills, ATP-zero death comes back only if trust is above the line. Good.
- "Whose identity this is: the walkthrough follows a hardware-anchored identity" is the kind of caveat I would normally skip, but it became important later (see Hestia).
- "Trust Score: her three trust dimensions **weighted for the role** she is acting in." Two pages later How It Works says the blend is fixed at 0.4/0.3/0.3 by the spec and "what varies per role is the minimum, **not the blend**". So is Alice's one number role-weighted or fixed-weighted? Both pages are confident.
- The actual interactive walkthrough needs JavaScript; I read the story version. Fine.

### How It Works (/how-it-works) (33:00)
- Long, as warned. The Birth/Life/Death/Rebirth spine is good and I kept my place.
- Things that stopped me:
  - **Reading costs ATP.** "Reading and browsing are effectively free, about 1 ATP a read ... viewing content about 2". I start with 100 ATP. So a newcomer who reads 100 posts before daring to write one is at zero and has an "energy death" with trust still at 0.50, having done nothing but read. The page calls this "lurking is cheap". From the 100-ATP start, it is not. I clicked "Why reading costs anything at all →" to get the answer.
  - **That link is broken.** It goes to `/atp-economics#faq-reading-cost`. The ATP page has no such section, and as far as I can find, never mentions a read costing anything at all; its simulator has no "read" action. So the claim that reading costs 1 ATP exists only on How It Works, and the page that is supposed to justify it does not know about it.
  - The MRH step in the "aliveness" ladder says "Actions outside your MRH **don't cost energy** and don't move trust". The MRH card three screens up says "sending it **still costs energy**". The Trust Neighborhood page says "Outside MRH? **Very expensive**". And Why Web4 said everyone stays visible, so there is no "outside". That is four statements about one question.
  - "Web4 has **four** core systems" (How the Pieces Fit Together heading) and, five lines later, "The **five** systems aren't independent". Same page, same section.
  - Section 3 is titled "Death: ATP Reaches Zero — *Energy death, the recoverable kind*", then the body says whether you come back "is not decided by which of them fired", and the Rebirth section says ATP-death with trust under 0.5 is "Permanent death". So energy death is not "the recoverable kind"; it is sometimes recoverable. The subtitle is the thing I would remember and it is wrong.
  - Spam costs **5** ATP; a real post costs **10-20**. The thesis is "spam dies because it costs more than it returns", fine, but it is odd that the system prices spam at a quarter of a good post. What makes a message count as the cheap kind?
- Things I liked: the "commissioned work pays, your own work only refunds" distinction is repeated until it sticks. The karma examples being flagged as "one modelling choice, not the rule" is honest, even if I now hold three different rebirth numbers (112, 145-carried-forward, 70) with no way to rank them.
- The SAL governance section and the plagiarism walkthrough were the first time I felt the design had thought about humans disagreeing. Then the "Status: all four are design, not track record" box took it away again. Honest, but deflating.

- This is the piece badged **Running**, and the page is good at describing a Tuesday with it. Vault, delegation that expires, revoke, witness chain. I could picture using it.

I went back to First Contact and the Trust Tensor page. 0.50 is the *start*. 0.50 is also the *survival line*. So on the one piece that actually runs, every identity starts on the line and can never climb above it. The same page says, higher up, "Trust that evolves: T3/V3 tensors that move with the outcomes of your actions". They can only move down. Nobody says this sentence plainly; I assembled it from three pages.

### ATP Economics (/atp-economics) (80:00)
- The 60-second version is good. "Recharge refunds, payment earns" finally made the economics click.
- But: "**No scarcity cap**, no mining" here, versus the Why Web4 FAQ "What backs ATP?": "**Capped issuance**: A community mints a finite ATP pool" and "**Decay on inactivity**: idle ATP doesn't sit forever". Capped or uncapped? Does idle ATP decay or not? The ATP page never mentions idle decay.
- Quality ramp: the table says 70% quality on a 50-ATP task pays ~30 (60%), yet the prose says "above 70% the task pays near-full". 60% is not near-full. And "scales linearly above 70%" here vs "between 30-70% linearly" on How It Works.
- Simulator: "Send spam message, cost 5" and "Low-quality post (your own), cost 10". Spam is half the price of a bad post. What is the difference, and who decides which one I sent?
- "Cross-community transfers apply the same 5% fee ... when societies federate (see the hub)" is written as if federation works; three pages say it is not built.

### Trust Tensor (/trust-tensor) (86:00)
- Three dimensions, per role, with the surgeon/mechanic example. Clear.
- The page warns that "two different numbers live on this page" (fixed composite weights vs role-match percentages). That warning is needed because the widget shows Data Analyst at 40/35/25 while the composite is 40/30/30. I understood it after the warning, but it means First Contact's "weighted for the role" and How It Works' "fixed by the spec" are talking about different numbers without saying so.
- Decay: Temperament halves every 30 days. The page itself asks "can decay alone push you below the survival line?" and answers "not settled, filed with the maintainers". For a software-only identity capped at 0.50, a month off would seem to do exactly that with no way back above the line. Nobody joins those two facts.

### LCT Explainer (/lct-explainer) (89:00)

- "Am I dead on arrival?" box directly addresses the software-at-0.50 worry. The answer is "no, you are just at zero margin forever". That is the honest sentence Hestia's page needed.

The page says "one model, not the rule" about the *amount*, but the *sign* flipping is not an amount question.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): witnessed behaviour instead of platform-declared or key-assumed trust. The 2-minute page nails this.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: the dependency diagram on /onramp did it.
- [x] What LCT means: a key on my device, with other devices co-signing; ceilings by hardware. Yes.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means: a spend-and-refund budget; receipts convert back when recipients confirm; commissions are the only way up. Yes, but with unresolved cents / cap / idle-decay / read-cost questions.
- [~] What Trust Tensors are: three numbers per role. I understood the idea. I do not know which weighting produces the one number that decides whether I live.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: Running Now is clear. But the hub is simultaneously "we run one" and "not deployed", and federation is both "tested code" and "not built".
- [x] Why any of this matters: the problem page convinced me.

## Unanswered Questions
1. If reading costs 1 ATP and I start with 100, what happens to someone who only reads? The link that was supposed to answer this is broken.
2. Is the hub running or not? The lab says it runs one; the status page says none are live.
3. Is federation built or not? One page says tested code; three say Spec only.
4. On Hestia, the only Running piece, my trust starts at 0.50, is capped at 0.50, and the survival line is 0.50. What exactly can my trust do, other than fall?
5. Does a stranger outside my neighbourhood see my post with zero weight, or not see it at all?
6. What does it cost me to act outside my neighbourhood: nothing, the normal cost, or "very expensive"?
7. Why does Alice's death at 0.51 trust earn +12 karma while the karma page's death at 0.64 trust earns −15?
8. Is ATP issuance capped or uncapped, and does idle ATP decay?
9. What happens to a witness who attests to something false? "Staking your reputation" is asserted everywhere and explained nowhere I read.
10. Who are the people? The anonymity FAQ says "intentionally anonymous"; the status page says "published"; the published page is a list of computers.
11. Does the lab's own Web4 society have any human member?

## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest and unusually long, and the two are related: nearly every page has a paragraph saying what is not settled, what is a modelling choice, and what has not been tested on a real device. I came to trust the tone. By the end I also noticed that the honesty is doing some of the work that deciding would do. There are at least three rebirth models, two federation statuses, two hub statuses, two ATP issuance stories, three out-of-neighbourhood costs, and two visibility models, and in most cases the site knows and says "read it for the shape, not the amount". After ninety minutes, shapes are what I have.

The reading path works. The first three pages got me to a real mental model in the promised fifteen minutes, and Alice's story is the thing I will remember. The problem page is genuinely good writing.

What I would fix first is not any single contradiction but the broken promise on How It Works: a sentence tells me reading costs energy, a link offers to explain why, and the link lands nowhere. That is the one moment where the site's honesty posture cracked, because the page that should own the claim does not know the claim exists. The second thing I would fix is the Hestia sentence nobody writes: "your trust will start at 0.50, stay at or below 0.50, and 0.50 is the line". Every ingredient is on the site; the conjunction is left to the reader, and the reader is the one deciding whether to install it.

I would come back if someone told me the hub was live and had people in it. Right now the most concrete thing I learned about who runs Web4 is that it is eight computers with names.
