## Honest Assessment
This is a site that knows what it is explaining and is unusually honest about what is real. The landing page, /tldr, the top of /why-web4, /running-now, /onramp, and /lct-explainer are good teaching. The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast, the badge legend, "recharge refunds, payment earns," and "the present tense below is the model talking" are the sentences that taught me the most, and I'd quote all four to someone else.

Where it loses me is not jargon, and it is not dishonesty. It is numbers and absolutes that don't agree with each other across pages, and sometimes within one page. I hit six of these that I'd call serious: visibility flips on /why-web4, cap vs no cap on ATP, a quality ramp whose box, table, prose and example are four different ramps, an energy death that is +12 karma on one page and −15 on another, out-of-neighborhood actions that cost energy and also don't, and a hub that the lab runs and that is also not running. None of these needed Web4 knowledge to spot. They needed two tabs open.

The pattern underneath them: the site has a habit of saying "this number is one modelling choice, not the rule," which is honest, but it then shows a second page using a different modelling choice and leaves me holding both. Honesty about uncertainty is good. Showing me two concrete contradictory numbers and then telling me neither is canonical is not the same thing as teaching me the shape. After three of those, I stopped trusting any number on the site, including the ones that are settled.

The second pattern: the Running piece is the one the explainers fit worst. Hestia is the proof that this exists. It also ships the identity tier that the trust pages treat as the degenerate case (capped at the start line, can't witness, can't recover), and none of that is on the Hestia page in those words. If I were going to install one thing from this site, it would be Hestia, and the site's own trust model says my trust number would never move upward. I'd want to be told that before, not after.

Would I come back? Maybe. I'd come back to /running-now and /lct-explainer. I would not re-read /atp-economics until the ramp is one ramp.
