## Understanding Gained

- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — a proposed standard where trust comes from witnessed behavior, not platforms or keys. Taught clearly by / and /tldr.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — one substrate, three scales. Taught.

- [x] What Trust Tensors are — Talent / Training / Temperament per role, 0.4/0.3/0.3 composite. Taught, with checkable math.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — /running-now is excellent. Hestia is Running on the lab's own 4 machines; hub is Reference; hardbound unvalidated on hardware; no public network.
- [~] Why any of this matters — the AI-agent accountability problem is clear and compelling. Whether *this* design solves it is shaken by the gate-bypass admission and the unsettled economics.

## Honest Assessment
This is a better site than I expected from the jargon in the sidebar. The front of the reading path (landing → /tldr → /why-web4) did its job: I can explain the pitch to a friend in two sentences, and the Web2/Web3/Web4 contrast is genuinely memorable. /running-now is the most trustworthy page I read; it tells me exactly what exists, with version numbers and caveats, and the "Where you fit today" section placed me correctly ("here to understand it, not to run it").

The honesty is real and pervasive: "the present tense below is the model talking", "settled vs still moving", "we will not invent a rule the standard does not contain", "the policy gate stops accidents, not adversaries". I believe these people aren't trying to sell me something.

Where it lost me is the numbers. When a page showed its arithmetic (trust update deltas, composite weights, Alice's three roles), I checked it, it worked, and my confidence went up. But the economic layer (ATP prices, the quality ramp, recharge, karma at rebirth) kept failing small checks: spam is the cheapest action, a ramp table that doesn't fit its own rule, an 84 from nowhere, "capped" and "no cap" in the same reading path, and three different rebirth models, each with its own "this is just one model" disclaimer. Each disclaimer is fair on its own. Stacked, they teach me that the numbers are decoration, which then bleeds back into the numbers that *were* right.

The other thing that stuck with me: the core promise is "prove what an AI agent did." Then, buried in a card on /running-now and the bottom of /hestia, I learn that an agent routing around the gate leaves no record at all, using two environment variables. That's honest, and I'm glad it's there, but it's the one fact that most changes what I'd tell a friend, and it's the one I was most likely to miss.

If I could ask for one thing: make the ATP/karma numbers on the main reading path agree with each other (or explicitly line up the competing models in one place), and put the gate limitation where the gate is first described.
