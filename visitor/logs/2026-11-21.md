# 4-Life Visitor Browse Log — 2026-11-21

- **Duration**: ~75 minutes of reading (my own estimate; the site's "45 minute" path took me well over that)

- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Would recommend?**: maybe (to a technical friend, yes for /tldr and /running-now; not the concept pages yet)

**How I browsed, and what I could not see.** I read the text each page serves. I could not operate anything interactive: the First Contact walkthrough past its opening frame, the lifecycle animation on How It Works, the LCT "security audit", the T3 scenario buttons, lives 2 and 3 on the karma page, turns 2 to 4 of "Quality vs Spam". Where a page's teaching lives inside one of those, I did not get it. Three reads were incomplete: /why-web4 is enormous and I only read its first third closely (problems, proposal, the 12 "most asked" answers; the other 60 answers I only saw as titles), I read only the top of /atp-economics (the "60-second version" and the Bitcoin comparison), and I stopped partway into the "Design Tensions" section of /what-could-go-wrong. I did not get to /day-in-web4, /value-tensor, /coherence-index, /glossary or the remaining "Going Deeper" pages.

## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: clear and calm. "Trust, built into the internet." and the subtitle about proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, by what rules. I understood the pitch in about 20 seconds, which is rare for this kind of site.
- "Witness" is defined right where it is first used. Good.
- The four onramp cards (core standard, hub, Hestia, Hardbound) read like product cards. Some phrases went straight past me: "sovereign Web4 society", "signed founding law", "append-only witnessed ledger", "open ontology".
- "The vocabulary" block lists Identity (LCT), Trust (T3), Value (V3), Neighborhood (MRH), Energy (ATP/ADP), Reputation (Karma) as bare labels. Six acronyms, zero expansions. I assumed they'd be explained later.
- "Where this actually stands" is a strong section: it says plainly this is research, not a product.
- Confusion points: a heading "Reference" with nothing under it, and a link "what does that tag mean?" where I could not see any tag. (Probably a badge that did not come through as text, but that is what I saw.) There is a stray "Aa" near the footer which another page later tells me is the glossary button.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".

### Web4 in 2 Minutes — /tldr (02:00)
- Best page on the site. Problem, shift, four pieces, result, "how real is it, honestly?". The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 comparison (platform declares / key holder assumed / witnessed behavior accrues) is the single clearest thing I read all session.
- The three badges (Spec, Reference, Running) are defined, and the page volunteers that "Running" counts only the lab's own daily use and "does not claim adoption". I appreciated that, though it does make "Running" mean less than the word suggests.
- "Your own device is your day-zero witness", and software-only "carries less weight". I noted that and moved on; it turns out to matter a great deal later (see Hestia).
- Clicked: Why Web4?

### Why Web4? — /why-web4 (05:00)
- The five "problems you already know" landed. Spam, siloed reputation, unlimited fresh starts, AI agents, platform control. I recognise all of these.
- "Why previous solutions failed" is fair to the alternatives (each gets a "what it gets right").
- The four proposals are where the acronyms finally get expanded: ATP (Allocation Transfer Packets), LCT (Linked Context Token), T3 (Talent, Training, Temperament), MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon, "math-flavored jargon you can ignore"). That last aside made me smile and I did ignore it.
- **First real stumble.** The "All four, in one moment" example says of a forum reply: only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see it, and "a stranger five hops away never sees the thread." Then, a few scrolls down in the FAQ, "Who can see my messages?" answers: "Everyone can see them", and beyond 3 hops is "zero weight, still visible". Those are opposite answers to a question I care about a lot (who can read what I write). I went back and re-read both to make sure I was not misreading. I was not.
- **Second stumble.** The same example sums the cost of the reply as "fractions of a cent of energy". A few answers later, ATP has no price in money and you "can't sell it for dollars". So what is a cent of it?
- The "Is this deployed or theoretical?" answer is impressively candid but dense: "424 attack vectors, ~85% detection against synthetic adversaries", "EK certificate chain through 2049", TEMPEST, TPM2. I cannot evaluate any of that. It also says "The specification is complete" in the same answer that lists formal proofs as missing.
- The FAQ is 72 questions. I read the 12 "most asked" and skimmed titles of the rest. The titles alone are a good map of what a skeptic would ask, and I was glad they exist, but I did not have the stamina to open them.
- Clicked: First Contact.

### First Contact — /first-contact (14:00)
- Promises a 7-minute interactive walkthrough of "Alice". I could not drive it; I only got the setup panel and the "Alice's Arc" summary table.
- The setup is good: two numbers, Energy (starts 100) and Trust (starts 0.50), and what makes each move.
- The arc table: Join 100 / 0.50, Build 0.56, Stumble −25 ATP and 0.48, Recover 0.62, then "Die & Reborn": dies at ATP 0, +12 bonus, reborn at 112 and 0.54.
- Confusion: she dips to 0.48 in Act 3, below the 0.50 line the panel just told me restricts features. Nothing in the summary says whether she was restricted. And in Act 5 she "dies" and the visible consequence is that she comes back with *more* energy than a newcomer (112 vs 100) and higher trust than she started with. As a story about consequences, dying looks like a promotion.
- The page then tells me, in a footnote, that the karma page models this differently and that this "is not what happens here", and to "take the shape, not the amount". I did not yet know what the karma page says, so this read as a warning about a contradiction I had not met yet.
- "The rules running here are real" sits a few lines from "a model with nothing deployed". I think I understand the intent (real rules, simulated world) but it took a second read.

### How It Works — /how-it-works (21:00)
- This is the 25-minute page and it felt longer. It is where I went from "I get it" to "I am not sure the numbers agree with each other".
- What worked: the Birth → Life → Death → Rebirth frame; the cost examples (commissioned write-up costs 15, pays 40; self-started post costs 15, refunds at most 15; spam costs 5, earns 0); the plagiarism walkthrough with Dr. Chen, which was the most concrete "how does a dispute go" story on the site; the Open Science Collective charter example.
- What tripped me:
  - "Five systems, in plain English" at the top, "Web4 has four core systems" further down (the second list drops MRH).
  - Reading costs ATP (about 1), a post costs 10 to 20, and a spam message costs 5. So on the price list the abusive action is cheaper than the honest one. Later the "death spiral" example charges a spammer 10 per post. Which is it, and why is spam the bargain?
  - "Life 2: ATP fluctuated 80-180. Had one ATP crisis (dropped to 15)." 15 is not between 80 and 180.
  - Life 1 "T3 rose to 0.65" over "a dozen+ actions". The page gives me the formula (0.02 × (quality − 0.5), i.e. +0.007 for a very good action). A dozen very good actions is under +0.1 even before the weighting the page mentions. I tried the arithmetic twice and get something like 25 actions needed. The page hedges ("net of many moves") but "a dozen+" and the formula do not meet.
  - Karma: "scored from the track record, not read off the balance", directly beside "Life 1 ended with 145 ATP, reborn with 145 ATP (karma carried forward)", which is exactly reading it off the balance. The page admits how much carries is "not settled" and that it shows one branch while First Contact shows another. Honest, but I now have two different rebirth rules and have not reached the karma page yet.
  - "No moderators needed to police spam", then a rotating Moderator decides Dr. Chen's appeal. The page does separate "policing spam" from "settling disputes", so this is a tone problem more than a contradiction.
  - "Game all five simultaneously? Mathematically impractical." No math is offered for that sentence.
- "Synthons" (groups that come alive) appeared for one section and never came back anywhere else I read.
- Clicked: Running Now.

The admission that Hestia's safety gate can be bypassed with "two environment variables" and that this is "an accepted limit right now rather than a scheduled repair" is the kind of sentence that makes me believe the rest.
- It opens by saying it "assumes the vocabulary" and sends newcomers to the TL;DR. But /learn told me this is page 5 of the *beginner* path. The vocabulary it assumes (witness chain, MCP, OID4VCI, TUI, Tauri, loopback operator plane, council gate, trait contracts) was not taught on pages 1 to 4.
- Confusion about "Running": the page says "Nothing on this page is a fielded production system" and also that Hestia is "deployed and operational today, in day-to-day use". The reconciliation (it is the lab's own four machines) is there, but I had to hold both sentences in my head.
- Bigger confusion about the hub: badge is Reference, "not yet deployed", pilot-ready "does not claim any exist yet", and it "would earn Running once live instances operate day-to-day". Then the "Where you fit" list says the lab "runs a society in the open" with members at 4-lab.io/fleet. Is that society a hub? If yes, a live instance exists. If no, what is it running on?
- "Where you fit today" (five reader types) is excellent and should be much higher up the site. I am the first bullet: nothing to install.

### Hestia — /hestia (57:00)
- The hearth metaphor is lovely (guests under your laws of hospitality, carrying embers to a shared fire).
- "A day with Hestia" is a good six-step story. I followed it.
- Then the caveat paragraph, which is the most important thing I learned today and it is two-thirds of the way down a product page: the identity Hestia gives you is software-only, and a software-only identity is **capped at 0.50** on the trust scale.
  - Every page so far told me everyone *starts* at 0.50.
  - Every page so far told me 0.50 is the *survival line*, and staying under it is permanent "trust death".
  - So with the only piece that is badged Running, I start at my ceiling, my ceiling is the death line, I can never go up, and any bad day puts me under. The same page lists "Trust that evolves: T3/V3 tensors that move with the outcomes of actions" as a feature. Evolves to where?
  - /lct-explainer later says what the ceiling "really costs you is margin", which confirms my reading rather than easing it. /what-could-go-wrong says "starts at 0.50 and 0.50 is also where it stops". Nobody says what a software-only user's first below-average rating does to them.
- Step 1 says "You open the Hestia app. It is a normal desktop app." The install section says the desktop app is the one piece with no download; you build it from source. So step 1 of the day is not available to a normal person.
- Step 4 says every tool call "lands in your witness chain". The next section says an action that goes around the gate "writes no entry at all". The page is honest about it, but the walkthrough states the strong version first.
- `--expires 24` with no unit in the command (the prose says hours).
- Crypto names with no gloss: ChaCha20-Poly1305, Argon2id, SQLCipher, OID4VCI.

### Hardbound — /hardbound (62:00)

For a product literally named after hardware binding, that is the headline, and the page does say "we call that out on purpose". I would still put it above the pillars, not below.

### LCT (Identity) — /lct-explainer (66:00)
- "A digital ID card on your own device" works. The Web2 "what you know" / Web3 "what you have" / Web4 "what witnesses verify" table works. The lake scenario (drop your phone: minutes / 3 to 7 days / start from zero, depending on tier) is the most practically useful thing on the page.
- The hardware tier table (TPM 0.90, Secure Enclave 0.85, FIDO2 0.75, software 0.50) and the "lower of chip class and device count" rule are clear once read twice.
- Two different pictures of what an LCT looks like:
  - `lct:web4:0x4c8f…a3f2#alice.assistant1`: before the `#` is hardware-anchored, after the `#` is "a label you chose and can change".
  - `lct:web4:agent:alice.assistant1@Thor#perception`: here `alice.assistant1` is *lineage* ("created by Alice, who vouches for it") and after the `#` is a *task scope* ("read-only, cannot execute code").
  - So is the thing after `#` a nickname I can change or a permission I cannot? Is `alice.assistant1` a label or a parentage claim?
- Getting from NASCENT to ACTIVE needs either infrastructure witness nodes or a community member's attestation. The same page says nobody outside the lab runs a witness node and there is no public network. So today, can anyone outside the lab have an ACTIVE LCT? The page says there are four lifecycle states "described in a lifecycle section below"; I did not find that section in what I was served.
- The page is very long and repeats the "who witnesses my first device?" question twice under two headings with slightly different answers (factory attestation vs Endorsement Key; I think they are the same thing).
- The widget numbers ("2.5x harder to attack", "30% anomaly detection, each witness adds 15%") arrive with no source. They feel precise and invented at the same time.

### T3 (Trust Tensor) — /trust-tensor (72:00)
- The surgeon / mechanic framing is immediately convincing. Alice as analyst 90%, as project manager 74%, as mechanic 28%, with the arithmetic shown, is the best worked example on the concept pages. The numbers check.
- "Why tensor?" answer: it is a 3-axis score. Thank you.
- The decay section worried me. Talent never decays, Training halves every 180 days, Temperament every 30. The composite weights are 0.4 / 0.3 / 0.3. If I stop showing up, two of my three numbers drift toward zero and only the 0.4-weighted one stays, so my composite heads under 0.5, the death line, purely from absence. The page raises exactly this ("Can decay alone push you below the survival line?") and answers that it is "not settled" and has been "filed with the standard's maintainers". I respect the honesty. But as a person deciding whether I'd want to live under this system, "we don't know yet whether a long illness kills your reputation" is a large open item to find in a sub-sub-heading.
- "A score of 0.80 settles at 0.40" after 180 days: it does not settle, it keeps falling (the next clause says 0.20 after a year).
- The page says the person who received my work decides its quality. My brief look at the ATP page suggested "three scorers, not one". I did not read far enough to reconcile them.
- "hestia evolves real T3/V3 trust tensors" in a callout; "implemented and tested in simulation" in the footer.

### ATP/ADP (Energy) — /atp-economics (78:00, top of page only)
- The "60-second version" is good: a budget not a wealth, every action spends, confirmed value recharges, self-started work refunds at most what you spent, commissioned work can pay more, quality matters "about 7×", not a cryptocurrency, ADP is the receipt.
- I understood ADP here for the first time: it is the spent form, the receipt that can be turned back into ATP when someone confirms the work was worth it.
- Odd bit: "not something you can buy, sell, or speculate on", and yet inside a society "a used-camera listing can ask 350" ATP and peer transfers exist with a 5% fee. If I can pay 350 ATP for a camera, ATP is money inside that society, and someone will sell cameras for it. I did not read far enough to see if the page deals with this.
- I ran out of steam here and did not read the rest of the page.

### Karma & Consequences — /karma-consequences (82:00)
- The opening is strong and unusually self-critical: permanence only holds with hardware; software-only identity makes the record "sheddable"; this is "Sybil resistance, not Sybil prevention" (Sybil is never defined; from context: one actor, many fake identities); "a genuine hole" that falls hardest on people with the least hardware.
- Now the rebirth problem arrives in full. Life 1 on this page: starts 100 / 0.50, does *good work* (trust rises to 0.64), overspends, hits ATP 0, dies. Karma: **−15**. Next life starts at **70** ATP. Formula: next ATP = 100 + karma × 2; next trust = previous × 0.95.
  - First Contact: Alice hits ATP 0, dies. Karma: **+12**. Next life starts at **112** ATP, trust goes *up* from 0.51 to 0.54.
  - How It Works: lives end with 145, restart with 145. No karma number at all.
  - Same event (ran out of energy with decent trust), three outcomes: a 12-point bonus, a 30-point penalty, or keep everything. All three pages footnote that this is "not settled" and point at each other. I believe them that it is unsettled. But "what happens when you fail" is the core of the whole pitch ("bad behavior follows you"), and after three pages I cannot tell you whether running out of energy is rewarded or punished.
- "This creates asymmetric consequences: building positive karma is harder than destroying it." The formula right above it is perfectly symmetric (2 ATP per point either way).
- "High karma = faster trust recovery" in the connections box; the formula has a flat 0.95 regardless of karma.
- "It took three lives ... 100 ATP, then 70, then 84": I could see Life 1's numbers only, so 84 came from nowhere for me.
- "If the system gets it wrong about you" is honest that appeals are designed and "no community has run it". Good section; grim answer.

### Trust Neighborhood (MRH) — /trust-neighborhood (88:00)
- "Friends, friends of friends, then strangers" is intuitive. 0.70 / 0.49 / 0.34 per hop is easy.
- The visibility question from /why-web4 comes back and this page contradicts *itself*: the Key Insight box says "you see your connections, their connections, and their connections - nothing more", and under privacy, outsiders "can't see your relationships". Further down: beyond 3 hops is "0% trust weight (treated as a stranger, not invisible)" and "'Zero' means trust weight, not visibility". I now think the intended rule is "everyone is visible, only nearby people count", but the first box on the page teaches the opposite.
- The explorer defaults to depth 2 while every sentence says 3 hops.
- "0.7 is the teaching calibration this site uses"; the reference code uses about 0.85 and they "arrive in the same place by depth 3 to 4". 0.7³ is 0.34 and 0.85³ is 0.61. Those are not the same place. Maybe there is a cutoff I am not being told about.
- A developer section with RDF, SPARQL and "O(d³) not O(n)" is marked skippable and I skipped it.

### What Could Go Wrong — /what-could-go-wrong (94:00, through the nine risks and part of the tensions)
- This page raised my opinion of the whole project. Nine risks with severity and likelihood, starting with "Nobody adopts it: existential, high likelihood", described as "the #1 existential risk". Sites selling a vision do not usually write that.
- It has its own acronym key at the top, which every concept page should copy.
- Risk 3 has a sharp observation I had not thought of: because death requires *staying* below 0.5, and a scoring error is "a sustained condition by construction", the wrongly scored user is exactly the one who stays below. And appeals are untested. That is a real problem stated plainly.
- Risk 8 (accessibility) confirms the software-only trap: "starts at 0.50 and 0.50 is also where it stops", called "a policy choice, not a technical inevitability". It also carefully says the 0.50 survival line and the 0.50 software ceiling are different things that "happen to" share a number. They may be different things, but a software-only user lives at their intersection.
- Risk 9 (the agent routes around its own gate) is "measured rather than predicted", with a public issue number. Good.
- Risk 4's numbers ("60% base detection", "coalitions become unprofitable at 2-3 members", "200 ATP stakes") are presented with confidence, then the page says there is no validated economic model. I do not know how much weight to give them.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — a proposed open standard where trust comes from other parties witnessing and signing what you (or your AI agent) actually do, rather than a platform's say-so or possession of a key. Taught by the site (/tldr), not inferred.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — one shared vocabulary/library, run at personal, community and enterprise scale. Taught by /tldr and /onramp. Still fuzzy on whether "the standard" is a document or a package.
- [x] What LCT means — Linked Context Token: an identity anchored to your device (ideally its security chip), not a password or account. Taught. I could not tell you what one actually looks like, since the page shows two incompatible formats.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means — an energy budget you spend to act and get back when others confirm your work was valuable; ADP is the spent receipt. Taught (the 60-second version on /atp-economics). Whether it is "not money" I am less sure.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — three scores (Talent, Training, Temperament) per role, not one global number. Taught well, with a worked example that adds up.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — libraries installable; Hestia runs on the lab's own machines; hub is runnable but (apparently?) not live; Hardbound usable but its hardware binding unvalidated; federation is paper only; no public network. Taught very well by /running-now, with the one hub ambiguity.
- [ ] Why any of this matters — half. I am persuaded the problems are real and that "prove what an agent did and on whose authority" is worth solving. I am not persuaded the consumer-internet story (spam dies, reputation follows you) holds, because the rules for the moments that matter (what happens at 0.50, what happens at death, who can see my posts) differ from page to page, and the tier most people would start in is the one the rules treat worst.

## Honest Assessment

There are two sites here and they are of very different quality.

The first is the status-and-honesty site: /tldr, /running-now, /onramp, /what-could-go-wrong, and the "honest status" sections of the product pages. This site is excellent. It tells me in plain words that there is no network, that the one safety gate can be walked around with two environment variables, that the enterprise tier's defining feature has never been tested on real hardware, and that the number one risk is that nobody uses it. I have rarely seen a project describe its own weaknesses this precisely, and the effect is that I trust the claims it does make. If the whole site were these five pages I would say "good" understanding and "yes" to recommend.

The second is the rules-of-the-world site: /how-it-works, /first-contact, /karma-consequences, /trust-neighborhood, parts of /lct-explainer and /trust-tensor. This site describes a society with energy, trust scores, death and rebirth, in the present tense, with a lot of specific numbers. And the numbers do not hold still. A message is invisible beyond three hops, or visible to everyone. Death by exhaustion earns +12 or costs −15 or changes nothing. Spam costs 5 or 10, and either way less than or equal to an honest post. A score climbs 0.15 in a dozen steps of 0.007. A range of 80 to 180 contains 15. Each individual wobble is small, and most are footnoted ("one model, not the rule", "take the shape, not the amount", "not settled", "this site's calibration, not the standard's"). But the footnotes are the problem as much as the cure: by the fourth page I had learned that any number I was shown might be disowned by the next page, so I stopped trying to learn the numbers at all. The site taught me the *shape* (acts cost energy, value earns it back, trust is per role, distance discounts trust, hardware makes identity sticky). I inferred nothing reliable about the *rules*.

The thing I will actually remember tomorrow is the 0.50 coincidence. Start at 0.50; die if you stay under 0.50; software-only identities cannot exceed 0.50; the only running software issues software-only identities. The site knows this (it calls the tier's situation "a genuine hole" and "a policy choice") and says it in at least four places, always a little way down the page and never with the consequence spelled out. For a system whose pitch is fairness to newcomers and consequences for bad actors, the first real users would be newcomers pinned to the floor. I would much rather read one clear paragraph near the top that says so and says what the designers intend to do about it.

I also cannot shake the gap between the opening pitch and the body. The landing page sells something sober and specific: proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority. Hestia and Hardbound are about exactly that, and their pages are concrete. But the middle of the site is mostly about a social world of posts, spam, karma and reincarnation that the running software does not appear to implement and that the site itself says exists only in simulation. Nobody tells me how the two relate. Is the lives-and-karma world the long-term vision and agent accountability the first step? Is it a teaching metaphor? I finished unsure whether Web4 is an audit trail for AI agents or a replacement for social media, and I think the honest answer the site would give is "both, at very different levels of reality". It should say that sentence somewhere early.

Caveat on my own experience: I read text only. The site clearly leans on interactive pieces (Alice's walkthrough, the lifecycle animation, sliders) to do its "concrete before abstract" teaching, and I got none of that. Someone clicking through may find the story lands better than the summary tables did for me. The contradictions above, though, are in the prose, and no widget would resolve them.
