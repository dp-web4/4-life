## Honest Assessment
This is the most honest project website I have read. It disclaims its own claims relentlessly, publishes its failure modes, and tells me which numbers are made up. I trust the authors more after 85 minutes, not less.

But the honesty has a failure mode of its own. Roughly a third of my confusion came not from things the site hid but from things it told me twice, differently, and then told me it knew. First Contact says the karma page disagrees with it; the karma page says First Contact disagrees with it; How It Works says both disagree with it and it won't pick. That's three pages spending paragraphs on meta-commentary about an inconsistency instead of one page fixing it. A newcomer reads that as "they don't know," which may be true, but then a single illustration with a single "one modelling choice" note would teach more than three that argue.

The thing that actually shook my confidence was small and structural: the one piece I can run today gives me an identity whose trust starts at its ceiling. I found that by combining a caveat on /hestia with a number on /first-contact. Nobody said it in one sentence. Everything else on the site says "trust is earned through behavior," and for the person the site tells to go run hestia, that appears not to be true yet. If it is true in some other sense (per-dimension scores, the record, something), that sentence is the one to write.

The site's best moments are concrete: the plagiarism walkthrough, the ordinary Tuesday with hestia, "the edge that is not there", the "calibrated, not derived" answer. Its worst moments are counting: four or five systems, seven or nine risks, three fives, three rebirth formulas. Counts are the cheapest thing to make agree, and every mismatch made me trust the next number a little less.

Would I come back? Maybe. If someone told me the hestia ceiling question had a real answer, yes.
