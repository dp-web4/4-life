## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: a clear one-line pitch: a proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, under what rules. I liked "trust is earned through witnessed behavior." The "Where This Actually Stands" box is unusually honest. It says R&D, not a finished product, and that hardware binding isn't validated on real devices.
- The four pieces at "three scales" (substrate plus community/personal/enterprise) came across fine as a list. I don't yet know why I'd want any of them.
- Six vocabulary words (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, Karma) were dumped on me as acronyms. The nav sidebar has four sections and about 25 links. That's a lot for a first visit.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".

### /tldr (02:00)
- A good page. Web2 vs Web3 vs Web4 in one comparison is the clearest framing on the site. The status badges (Spec / Reference / Running) are a nice idea.
- "No timeline commitments" is honest. Clicked "Why Web4?" as suggested.

### /why-web4 (05:00 – 20:00)
- The problem section (spam, trapped reputation, cheap fresh starts, bots, platform control) is relatable. "Why previous solutions failed" is good.
- The proposal section: energy budget, device-anchored identity, role-specific trust, trust neighborhood, and the CI "which is not a fifth mechanism". Fine.
- Then the FAQ: **72 questions**. The reading path budgets 6 minutes and excludes the Q&A, which is fair. But it's a wall. The page is about 139k characters, and my tool had to read it in two chunks.
- Things I wrote down while reading:
  - "creating a fake identity means buying a new physical device". Later, /onramp says the hub accepts a device as hardware "because its owner declared it so". So in the only community software that exists, faking is free?
  - "everyone starts at 0.5 trust" and "Raw trust below 0.5 that stays there is described as fatal." So everyone is born exactly on the death line? That's alarming, and nothing here reassures me except "newcomers are the most fragile for ~30 actions".
  - Death section: trust death is permanent, "there is no rebirth on the far side of it." In the same breath, "Rebirth is checked against your T3 score." I had to reread several times to see that rebirth belongs to *energy* death. That isn't said next to it.
  - Tier numbering: the five-tier migration list is 1–5 (Wrapper…Native), but elsewhere the page refers to "Tiers 0-3" and "Tier 4". Which numbering is right?
  - The Maya and 0.80 → 0.52 federation numbers check out (×0.65). The whistleblower 70% checks out. Thanks for numbers that add up.
  - "Reddit estimated it spends ~$50M/year on content moderation." There's no source.

### /first-contact (20:00)
- Clear framing: a browser model, not the real thing. The "Why raw?" expander is helpful: the 0.50 check reads raw trust, not trust × CI².
- Alice's arc: 0.50 → 0.56 → **0.48** (Stumble) → 0.62 → dies at ATP 0 with trust 0.51 → "+12 bonus → 112", reborn at trust **0.54**.
  - In Act 3 she sits *below* 0.50. The panel told me "staying below causes permanent trust death". I don't know how long "staying" is, so I couldn't tell whether she was in mortal danger.
  - She dies of ATP exhaustion and gets **+12** karma. Her trust is *higher* after rebirth (0.54) than at death (0.51). The page itself admits the karma page models trust falling at rebirth and this walkthrough doesn't. I appreciate the admission, but now I don't trust either version.

### /how-it-works (25:00 – 40:00)
- The dense, "complete" page. Costs: ~1 ATP per read, 10-20 per post. The trust rule is a real formula: `0.02 × (quality − 0.5)`, ×1.0/×0.8/×0.6. Worked: quality 0.85 → +0.007 talent. I checked it and it's right.
- **Life 1 (Novice) didn't add up when I checked it.** Start 100 ATP, T3 0.5. Spent 60, earned 105, T3 rose **0.5 → 0.65**. Using the page's own rule, a 0.85-quality action raises the composite by about 0.4(0.007) + 0.3(0.0056) + 0.3(0.0042) ≈ **0.0057**. A +0.15 climb needs roughly **26** high-quality actions. At 10-20 ATP per post, 60 ATP buys **3 to 6** posts, which is about +0.02 to +0.03, not +0.15. Reads cost ATP but nothing says they raise trust. So either the spend, the climb, or the per-action rule is wrong. I couldn't find what reconciles them.
- Life 1 "Ended with 145 ATP. Reborn with 145 ATP." Born again from what? The page itself notes none of these lives ends at 0 ATP, so none is an energy death. Then why is there a rebirth? It says it "declines to name other endings". So I'm left with lives that end for an unnamed reason.
- The rebirth example: 0.4(0.40) + 0.3(0.60) + 0.3(0.60) = 0.52, so eligible. But Talent is 0.40, which is below the 0.5 survival line. Is the survival line checked per dimension or on the composite? It's never stated.
- The death-spiral talent −0.006 implies quality 0.20. OK.

### /hestia (45:00)
- A good, concrete page, with real commands (`hestia init`, `vault add`, `delegate grant … --expires 24`, `serve`), loopback-only binding, and honest limits ("stops accidents, not adversaries").
- **The big one for me:** a heading promises **"Trust that evolves"**, and the page says "A software-only identity is capped at 0.50". Hestia is software-only (hardware is deferred to hardbound). Every other page says everyone starts at 0.50. /what-could-go-wrong says it plainly: "A software-only identity starts at 0.50 and 0.50 is also where it stops". So the recommended first step gives me an identity whose trust can **only go down** from where it starts, and it starts on the death line. Hestia never says that, and the "Trust that evolves" heading implies the opposite.
- "you start over from zero with a fresh identity." Zero what? Trust zero? Everything else says a fresh identity starts at 0.50. I think it means zero *history*, but it reads as zero trust.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed standard for witnessed, provable actions by humans and AI agents
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one substrate, three scales; start with hestia
- [x] What LCT means: device-held cryptographic identity, ceilings by hardware class
- [~] What ATP/ADP means: the concept (energy budget, receipt) yes; the numbers (quality ramp, karma carry-over) no
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three role-specific numbers with fixed composite weights
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: /running-now is excellent
- [~] Why any of this matters: the problem framing is good; whether the solution works without verified hardware is unclear

## Honest Assessment
This is the most self-aware project site I've browsed. It admits the gate can be bypassed with two environment variables, that nothing is in production, that the hub has no live network, and that the karma model "is not settled". /running-now and /what-could-go-wrong made me trust the authors.

But I came away with a pattern I couldn't shake. Whenever I checked a number against a number on another page (or sometimes the same page), roughly half didn't match. The trust formula is real and I verified it. Then Life 1 climbs five times faster than that formula allows. The quality ramp has a stated shape, and the table beside it has a different one. CI is "not for the survival line" two paragraphs after it is the "alive/dead line". Alice's ATP death earns +12 karma, and another agent's identical death earns −15. Each page often flags its own caveat, which is honest. But a caveat on every number makes the numbers feel decorative, and then I don't know which ones to learn.

The finding that stuck with me most: the site says to start with hestia, and hestia promises "Trust that evolves". I only put it together after four pages: a hestia identity starts at 0.50, is capped at 0.50, and dies if it stays below 0.50. So my first real step into Web4 gives me trust that can only fall and starts on the edge of death. Maybe there's a good reason (software identities are meant to be throwaway bootstrap keys?), but the hestia page should tell me, not leave me to triangulate it from /lct-explainer and /what-could-go-wrong.

I'd come back to see if it gets clearer, and I'd point a technical friend at /tldr, /onramp and /running-now. I wouldn't send a casual reader into /how-it-works yet.
