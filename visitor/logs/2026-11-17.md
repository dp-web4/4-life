## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed open standard so that actions by people and AI agents carry proof of who did what under whose authority, with trust earned from witnessed behaviour. The site taught me this; /tldr did most of the work.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: standard underneath, then personal / community / enterprise scales. Taught by /onramp. The word "onramp" itself is overloaded.
- [x] What LCT means: Linked Context Token, an identity your device holds and proves, stronger when anchored in a security chip and witnessed by your other devices. Taught, but not on the landing page where I first met it.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means: Allocation Transfer / Discharge Packets; an energy budget you spend to act and a receipt that turns back into energy when others confirm the work. Concept taught well. I do not trust any specific ATP number on the site.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three scores (Talent, Training, Temperament) per role, blended 0.4/0.3/0.3. Taught clearly.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: yes, and this is the site's strongest suit, with one hole: I cannot tell whether a hub is actually running anywhere.
- [ ] Why any of this matters: half. The problem framing convinced me. What I cannot yet see is why I, today, would do anything with it besides read, unless I already run AI agents (then hestia's delegation story is concrete).

## Honest Assessment
The ideas landed. After /tldr and /why-web4 I could explain Web4 to a friend in two sentences, and after /running-now I could tell that friend what is code and what is hope. That is rare for a project like this, and the site's willingness to print things like "stops accidents, not adversaries" and "no validated economic model of what an attack returns" made me trust the authors more than the average explainer.

What undercut it is the numbers. Almost every page has a worked example, and when I tried to carry a number from one page to the next it broke: the quality table against its own rule, the karma sign between First Contact and the karma page, the 0.64 two-hop trust, "every action costs 1 ATP" under a list of posts that cost 3, 15 and 5. Many of these come with a disclaimer that the figure is "one modelling choice" or "not settled". The disclaimers are honest, but there are so many of them that by the end I stopped treating any figure as meaningful, including the ones that are apparently fixed by the standard. I could not tell the settled numbers from the placeholder ones without reading a collapsible box on one specific page.

The other thing that stayed with me is the 0.50 problem. The site tells me repeatedly that 0.5 is the line under which you are restricted and eventually dead, and also that anyone without a security chip is capped at 0.50, and also that the only piece running today hands out software identities. Each page that mentions it handles it carefully in isolation. Put together, a newcomer's takeaway is "the version I can actually try puts me on the edge of the cliff forever", and no page addresses that reading head-on.

The site is also long. The recommended path is 45 minutes and I believe it; /how-it-works alone is two pages' worth, and the FAQ has 72 questions. The mockups on /day-in-web4 did more for my intuition in two minutes than /how-it-works did in twenty, and they are buried in "Going Deeper".

Would I come back? If I ran AI agents and wanted scoped, revocable delegation, I would go look at hestia's repository; the "day with Hestia" walkthrough sold that. As a general reader, I leave understanding the pitch, respecting the candour, and unsure what the numbers are for.
