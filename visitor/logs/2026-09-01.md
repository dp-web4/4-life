# 4-Life Visitor Browse Log - 2026-09-01

## Session Summary
- **Duration**: ~95 minutes
- **Pages visited**: 24 (23 on the site, plus the external 4-lab.io/fleet page the site sent me to three times)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Would recommend?**: maybe (yes to a technical friend; no to a general one)

Note on method: I read the pages as served text. The interactive parts (the First Contact walkthrough, the ATP simulator, the CI sliders, the device-count slider) need scripting I did not run, so I judged them by the text around them, not by driving them.

## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: a clean framing of a problem I recognise. "AI agents already take real actions... we cannot prove what an agent did, on whose authority, or by what rules." I understood that immediately. "To witness is to observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation on the record" is a good sentence.
- Then the four "onramp" cards, and my comprehension fell off a cliff. "Witnessed presence, role-contextual trust, and auditable authority." "Seven roles, a signed founding law, an append-only witnessed ledger, and sealed member channels." "A cryptographic LCT, an encrypted vault, scoped revocable delegation." I had read maybe 120 words of the site and was already being handed eleven terms I could not define. The hestia and hardbound cards each open with a name etymology (Greek goddess / "as in hardware-bound") before telling me what the thing does, which is a strange order.
- The "Reference" heading above "Where this actually stands" confused me: is "Reference" the name of the section, or a label? Next to it: "what does that tag mean?" There is no tag anywhere on the page that I could see. I clicked it later and learned it is a maturity badge word. On the landing page it just looks like an orphaned heading.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes", because the intro paragraph told me to and gave me an order (2-minute intro, Why Web4, First Contact).

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (04:00)
- Good. "Plain English first, so you can read this cold" is a promise, and the page mostly keeps it. The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 triplet is the clearest thing I read all session: platform declares your reputation / whoever holds the key is assumed to be the owner / trust accrues from witnessed behaviour. I did not know what "key" meant in the Web3 line, but I got the gist.
- The Four Pieces list puts the words **Reference** and **Running** next to each piece with no key to what they mean. Three pieces are "Reference", one is "Running". I guessed "Reference" meant "documentation only", which turned out to be wrong (it means built and runnable). So the label actively misled me for the next twenty minutes.
- "How real is it, honestly?" repeats the four-piece list I had just read, and drops `pip install web4-core` / `cargo add web4-core` into a page that opened by saying nothing here needs a background in crypto or AI. I don't know what pip or cargo are.
- "The one-sentence version" at the bottom is excellent. It should be near the top.
- Clicked: Why Web4.

### Why Web4? (08:00)
- The best-written page on the site. "You already know this. You feel it every day." The five problems (spam is free, reputation trapped in silos, unlimited fresh starts, AI making it worse, platforms control everything) each have a "root cause" line, and I could argue with each one, which is a sign they were put clearly. The "why previous solutions failed" section is fair to the alternatives ("What it gets right" is a nice touch).
- The four mechanism cards are the first time the acronyms are introduced properly (Energy Budget = ATP, Device-Anchored Identity = LCT, Multi-Dimensional Trust = T3, Trust Neighborhood = MRH) with the plain name first. This is the order the landing page should have used.
- "All four, in one moment" (replying to a stranger's forum question) finally made the four ideas concrete. I wish it were on the landing page.
- The FAQ is huge. I read three: social credit (the comparison table is persuasive; the "honest caveat" that a society *could* be configured oppressively is appreciated), Web3 (clear lineage), and "When will it be available" which says "The mechanics are proven in simulation" and "The technology is ready for that step." I noted "proven" and kept going. Later pages made me want to come back to that word.
- Clicked: First Contact.

### First Contact (18:00)
- Told me "this page starts from zero" and "you don't need to memorize" and then, before the Start button, gave me: a green "Running in the real world" banner; a paragraph explaining that the banner is *not* about what I am about to drive; a second paragraph explaining the first paragraph; the two numbers to watch; a note about whose identity this is with two outbound links; a box titled "Why 'raw'?" that introduces the coherence index and the formula raw x CI squared; and a collapsed reference map of four ideas. That is a lot of throat-clearing for a page that says I can just press Start.
- The two-number panel itself (Energy Budget starts at 100, Trust Score starts at 0.50, below 0.50 features restrict, staying below is fatal) was clear and is what I actually needed.
- The five-act map with endings was useful. Then "About that number" tells me the +12 karma bonus is "one modelling choice, not the rule" and that "the karma page models it as falling at each rebirth, which is not what happens here." So before I have watched the walkthrough, the page has told me that another page on the same site disagrees with it. I did not know what to do with that.
- I could not drive the walkthrough (no scripting on my side), so I cannot say whether the story lands. The text around it suggests it would.
- Clicked: The Reading Path, because tldr told me I had "finished the first" of five steps and I wanted to see the list.

### The Reading Path (26:00)
- Clear and honest about time: five pages, ~45 minutes, "the first three are the short version, about 15 minutes." The progress tracker (0 / 24 concepts, kept in this browser only) is a nice touch. The "Why learn this now?" line under each step is good pedagogy.
- Nothing confusing here. It did tell me How It Works is 25 minutes, which set expectations correctly.
- Clicked: How It Works.

### How It Works (29:00)
- Long. It is honest about that. The "No network you can join yet" note at the top and the "Key Takeaways" box are the right way to open a 25-minute page.
- The five systems table (LCT / ATP / T3 / CI / MRH) was where the acronyms finally stuck.
- The lifecycle: born with 100 ATP and 0.5 trust; reading costs ~1 ATP, posting 10-20; commissioned work pays more than it costs (15 -> 40), self-initiated work refunds at most what you spent; trust moves in steps of 0.007. The tiny-step point ("Reputation is the accumulation of hundreds of them, not the payoff of any one") is a good line.
- The section heading is "3. Death: ATP Reaches Zero", and the first thing under it says there are two ways to die. The heading names one.
- The three-life example (ended with 145, then 130, then 165 ATP, trust climbing 0.65 -> 0.72 -> 0.85) comes with a box saying none of these lives ends in either kind of death, and then: "What else ends a life is not settled... it names no term limit, no lifespan, and no natural end of life." So the worked example of the lifecycle shows three lives ending for a reason the system does not define. I read the box twice. I understand *why* it is there (honesty), but it means the central illustration illustrates something the page says does not exist.
- Karma here: you are reborn with your whole final balance (145 -> 145). First Contact said 100 + 12. Both flagged as "one modelling choice". Noted; more on this below.
- The SAL governance section and the plagiarism walkthrough (40% overlap, 30-day suspension, Training drops 0.15, appeal to a rotating moderator) were concrete and I followed them. The "All four are design, not track record" note under "What prevents unfair rules?" is the kind of honesty I trust.
- "When agents work together" needed a box explaining that 0.9^5 = 0.59 and the 0.7-per-hop decay are "two different numbers, not two rates for one thing." The box helped, but the fact that it was needed tells me the two numbers are going to be confused by most readers.
- Clicked: Running Now (step 5).

### Running Now (52:00)
- The best page on the site for my purposes. The badge key (Spec = written down; Reference = built and runnable, not in production; Running = in day-to-day use, so far only the lab's own) is exactly what tldr should have linked to next to those words. "Pilot-ready is not a badge" is a good clarification.

- The hestia caveat that "an action that routes around the gate writes no entry at all (today, two environment variables suffice)" is startling and I am glad it is there. It changes how I read "Running".
- "Where you fit today" with the five reader rows was the first time the site asked who I was. I picked the first row ("here to understand it, not to run it"). It told me the lab runs as a society "in the open" and linked 4-lab.io/fleet. I followed that later.
- Clicked: How the Pieces Compose, because the landing page and Running Now both kept pointing at it.

The "hearth / guests under your laws of hospitality / carrying embers to a shared fire" image is nice, and this is where the name finally pays off (the landing page card led with it before I could care).

- The Witness entry does the "two jobs, same word" split and gives the device ceilings (1 device 0.75, 2 devices 0.85, 3+ up to 0.90; software-only stays at 0.50). I wrote these down; they matter below.

- Clicked: LCT (Identity), first item in the concept path.

### ATP Economics (90:00)
- "Agent just means any participant, you included" in the first screen: good, I had been wondering.
- "Recharge refunds, payment earns" is the key idea and the page says it about seven times. Confirming your own work refunds at most what you spent; only work someone else commissioned puts you ahead. Then: "How someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question on this stack rather than a solved one." That is the question I most wanted answered, and the site says nobody knows. (It says this on three pages.)
- The "Settled vs still moving" box is a good idea. But in the Settled column it says the quality ramp is "below ~30% earns ~zero, above ~70% earns ~full, linear in between", and the table two screens down says a 50-ATP task at 70% quality pays ~30 ATP. 30 out of 50 is not "~full". At 85% it pays ~42. So either the words or the table are off, and the words are in the box labelled canonical.
- The "no floor, no allowance" answer (issuance is society law; the spec refuses a universal authority) was honest and I understood it.

### Karma & Consequences (112:00)
- "What is permanent is the identity, not the penalty" is a good line and clears up a fear I had.
- Life 1 demo: start 0.50 trust / 100 ATP, end 0.64 trust / 0 ATP, karma -15, "Poor resource management, not a bad reputation." Formula: next life = 100 + 2 x karma = 70 ATP; next trust = 0.64 x 0.95.
- Now I have three karma stories. How It Works: energy death is "recoverable if you built trust", and "if you built trust, you're reborn with a head start" (ATP page says this too); its example carries the whole balance forward (145 -> 145). First Contact: dies at 0, comes back with 100 + 12, trust goes *up* 0.51 -> 0.54. Karma page: an agent who *did* build trust (0.64) comes back handicapped at 70, and trust goes *down*. Each page says "one model, not the rule." I believe them. But I finished the three pages unable to say what happens when you die with good trust, which is the one thing every page says is the point of the system.
- Clicked: What Could Go Wrong (linked from nearly every page).

### What Could Go Wrong (118:00)
- The most trust-building page after Running Now. Nine risks, each with a likelihood, a mitigation, and what's still open. "Nobody adopts it: existential, high likelihood" first is the right call. Risk 9 ("the agent ignores the rules... measured, not predicted") admits the hestia gate reads its own settings from a place the agent can write.
- "Genuinely unsolved" list: witness bootstrapping, appeals untested with humans, governance at scale, real-world validation, right to be forgotten. After this page, "the mechanics are proven in simulation" on Why Web4 reads as too strong. Risk 7 says scalability is "partially validated" from 100-200 agent runs.
- Clicked: A Day in Web4 (How It Works had pointed me here for "what it looks like on screen").

A reader who lands here from a search will think the app exists.
- The mockups themselves (mail where a 0.43-trust stranger pays 8 ATP to reach me and a 0.87 friend pays 2; a marketplace listing at 350 ATP with "distance-adjusted trust 0.64, 2 hops") were the most vivid thing on the site and would have helped me on page two.

### 4-lab.io/fleet (128:00)
- Eight machines (Jetsons, a laptop, a Mac Mini) each running a small language model, with session counts and roles named after brain regions ("thalamic router", "cerebellum / habit compiler", "reward prediction"). One hosts "the fleet society ledger."
- I understand that this is the "live Web4 society" the hub page mentioned. As a lay visitor, what I found was a hardware inventory for an AI research project, and it felt like a different website. It did not help me picture a *community* running a hub. It may reassure a technical reader.

### Identity Constellation (131:00)
- Short and clear on the enrollment ceremony and the recovery ladder (3+ devices: replace it yourself; exactly 2: community vouching 3-7 days; software-only: start fresh).
- The interactive says a 2-device example shows "60% identity trust". The LCT page and the glossary both say 2 hardware devices cap you at 0.85. I do not know if these are the same number, but they are presented the same way (a trust figure for two devices) and they disagree.

### Pages I opened but did not read closely
- The Core Standard: the "canonical equation" Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP, with "/" meaning "verified by" and "*" meaning "contextualized by". The term-by-term reading was clear; the page is honest that RDF and MCP appear nowhere else on the path. The 30-second bootstrap needs git and python; not for me.

- Hardbound: enterprise tier, EU AI Act Article 12, SOC 2. Clear about what it is for; clearly not for me.
- Value Tensor: Valuation 0.30 / Veracity 0.35 / Validity 0.35, "truth over popularity." Its earnings example is T3 x V3 x base rate (0.7 x 0.8 x 10 = 5.6 ATP), which is a third way of computing what you earn after the ATP page's two.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): trust earned from witnessed behaviour instead of declared by a platform or assumed from a key.
- [x] What the onramp is and how the pieces fit: one substrate (the standard) and one shape at three scales (hestia / hub / hardbound). Taught by /onramp and /running-now, not by the landing page.
- [x] What LCT means: a device-anchored, witnessed identity; the chip's factory certificate is the first witness. Taught, thoroughly.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means: a per-participant energy budget and the receipt for spending it. Mechanics taught well. How a newcomer ever gets ahead: not taught, and the site says it is open.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: Talent / Training / Temperament per role, weighted 0.4 / 0.3 / 0.3. Taught by the Alice example.
- [x] What is real today vs still R&D: yes, and this is the site's real strength. /running-now is the page I would send someone.
- [x] Why any of this matters: yes, from /why-web4.

Things I was told but do not actually understand: what happens when you die with good trust (three answers); whether 0.5 is a CI threshold (page says yes and no); what ends a life if not energy or trust collapse ("not settled").

## Unanswered Questions
1. If I run out of energy but have good trust, do I come back stronger (How It Works, ATP page, First Contact) or weaker (Karma page Life 1)?
2. Is 0.5 an alive/dead line for the coherence index or only for raw trust? The CI page says both.
3. In the three-life example, what ended each life? The page says neither death applies and nothing else is defined.
4. As a newcomer with no track record, how do I ever get my first commission? Every page that raises it says "open question."
5. At 70% quality, does a task pay ~60% (table) or ~full (settled box)?
6. Two devices: 0.85 (LCT, glossary) or 60% (constellation)?
7. Who is the intended first user of this? /running-now's "Where you fit today" is the only place that asked, and the honest answer for a non-programmer was "read about it."

10. What is a "key" in "whoever holds the key" (tldr, Web3 line)? I guessed; the site did not say until the LCT page.

## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest, and by the end that honesty was both the reason I trusted it and the reason I was tired. Every page says what is real and what is not, and I believe all of it. /running-now and /what-could-go-wrong are the two pages I would show a sceptical friend. The concept pages taught me LCT, T3, MRH and CI well enough that I could explain them over dinner.

Where it lost me was not jargon in the end (the glossary's acronym table and the Why Web4 cards fix that, just too late in the path) but the number of places where the site's own numbers or rules disagree with each other and each disagreement is handled with a caveat rather than a decision. Three rebirth models. A "settled" quality ramp whose table does not match its sentence. A coherence page that says 0.5 is and is not a death line. Two-device trust of 0.85 in one place and 60% in another. A lifecycle example whose lives end for an undefined reason. Each caveat is honest. Together they left me unsure what the rules *are*, which is a strange place to finish a site whose thesis is that rules should be signed, readable, and checkable.

The landing page is the weakest page on the site and the first one everyone sees. The four onramp cards are the densest text I read all session. The one-sentence version at the bottom of /tldr, the "all four in one moment" forum reply from /why-web4, and the "one shape at three scales" line from /onramp would make a better landing page than the one that exists.

Would I return? Maybe. If hestia grows a desktop app a non-programmer can open, or a hub gets a real group of people in it, I would come back to see it. As a reader, I have what I came for. As a would-be participant, the site told me clearly and kindly that there is nothing yet for someone like me to join, and I appreciated being told.
