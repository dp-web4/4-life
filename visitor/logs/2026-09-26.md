## Understanding Gained

- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a standard for provable, witnessed agent actions, with trust earned from behavior
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one substrate, three scales. Clear.
- [x] What LCT means: your witnessed, device-anchored identity. Clear, though the hardware tiers took a while.

- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: /running-now is excellent
- [~] Why any of this matters: the problem framing is convincing; whether the economics would actually sustain an ordinary participant is unclear

## Honest Assessment
The site's biggest strength is its honesty. /running-now, /what-could-go-wrong (especially risk 9), the hub/hardbound "edge that is not there", and the gate-bypass disclosure all made me trust it more. I came away knowing clearly what is real (the libraries, the Hestia daily use by the lab, the hub MVP) and what is not (federation, hardware binding validation, any live network).

The biggest weakness is the numbers and the absolutes. The site asks me to watch numbers (First Contact literally says "the two numbers to watch"), so I did, and they kept disagreeing: 0.55/0.56, 12-of-13 "recovers its cost", a quality ramp whose table contradicts its definition, karma that rewards the worse life, a 60-ATP life that does ~20 actions' worth of trust climbing. Most of them come with an "illustrative, not the rule" disclaimer. After the fifth one, the disclaimers started to read as "don't check our arithmetic", which is the opposite of the site's pitch about verifiable records.

The one finding that actually changed my understanding: a software-only identity starts at the same 0.50 that is its ceiling, and the only running piece (Hestia) uses a software identity. So "trust that evolves" and "good behavior compounds" are, for anyone who can install something today, not true in the upward direction. The site does disclose each half: the cap on /lct-explainer and /hestia, and the software LCT on /running-now and /hestia. But I had to join them myself, and the pitch sentences on /hestia and /tldr don't reflect it.

Second: the visibility/cost story for strangers (the core anti-spam mechanism) is told in incompatible ways on three pages. I would fix that before anything else, because it is the first mechanism a reader tries to picture.

Pages were long but mostly well signposted ("skim this", "optional"). The reading path on the landing page worked, and I would follow it again.
