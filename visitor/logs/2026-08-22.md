### ATP Economics (49:00)

I came here because I wanted to check "more than she spent" from First Contact.

The page states the rule very clearly:

> "Total recharge for one ADP receipt is the sum across confirmer trust weighted confirmations, **capped at the original ATP cost**: recharge = min( ∑ Δrecharge, ATP_cost )"
> "The cap means you can't profit on a single action, only recover its cost."
> "Confirmation of your own work refunds at most what you spent, so self-initiated work alone does not grow a balance; commissioned work is the channel that does."

So First Contact's "she earns 12 ATP back, more than she spent" is the exact thing this page says cannot happen. (On a cumulative reading it might survive: Alice spent 8 + 5 = 13 across steps 1 and 2, and 12 ≤ 13. But the sentence does not say that. It says *more than she spent*, and then draws the moral "Quality content pays for itself.")

Then the worked example, which is where I lost the thread properly:

> Sam, 35% quality: spends −50, earns back ~6, **net −44**.
> Hannah, 85% quality: spends −50, earns back ~42, **net −8**.
> "Same task, same spend, Hannah keeps 7x more ATP than Sam (42 ÷ 6). **Across a hundred tasks, Sam burns through their budget and dies of energy starvation. Hannah barely loses any**, but barely losing is not thriving..."

I did the multiplication because the sentence invited me to ("across a hundred tasks"). Hannah loses 8 per task. Her whole budget is 100. She is bankrupt on task 13. Across a hundred tasks she is down 800, which is eight times everything she ever had. She does not "barely lose any"; she dies of energy starvation too, roughly five and a half times slower than Sam. The clause immediately after ("quality is what slows her burn") is right and contradicts the clause before it in the same sentence.

I want to be fair: this page is *ferociously* caveated everywhere else, including a parenthetical admitting "Notice both net out negative here" and a fully honest bottom line I did not expect to find anywhere on a project site:

> "How someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question on this stack rather than a solved one, and you should read it as one."

That sentence is great. It is also why "Hannah barely loses any" stood out so badly. The page has already conceded that self-initiated work never grows a balance and that newcomers cannot reach the channel that does. Hannah is doing exactly the thing the page says leads to starvation, and the page tells me she is basically fine.

Small thing while I am here: "Hannah **keeps** 7x more ATP than Sam (42 ÷ 6)." The parenthetical makes the derivation traceable, so I could tell what was meant, but "keeps" is the wrong verb for an earn-back ratio. What Hannah *keeps* is 92 against Sam's 56, which is 1.6x. She *earns back* 7x more.

### Karma & Consequences (66:00)

I came here to find out whether dying is really an upgrade. This page is where the site's honesty is at its most impressive and its structure at its least helpful.

The formula:

```
next_life_atp   = base_atp + (karma × karma_multiplier)
next_life_trust = prev_trust × trust_decay_factor
```
with `trust_decay_factor = 0.95 (trust carries over with 5% decay)`.

So on this page, rebirth **lowers** your trust. On First Contact, rebirth **raises** it (0.51 → 0.54). Applying the formula to First Contact's own number gives 0.51 × 0.95 = 0.4845, which is not 0.54 and is not even on the same side of the 0.50 line.

And the page **knows**:

> "The trust line is open in the same way: the First Contact walkthrough shows a rebirth where trust does not fall, which the decay factor below does not produce."
> "...the lifecycle walkthrough's karma examples model it the other way, carrying the balance forward intact."

That is an admirable disclosure. My problem with it is purely where it lives. `/karma-consequences` is in "Core Concepts", not in the five-page reading path. `/first-contact` is step 3 of 5. The site explicitly invites me to stop after step 3 ("Short on time? Stop here"). So the recommended reading order gives me the disputed number as a fact, offers to let me leave, and keeps the correction on a page I was never routed to. Three surfaces model rebirth three ways (First Contact, How It Works, this formula) and only one of the three says so.

### What Could Go Wrong (71:00)

Genuinely the reason I would recommend this site to someone, with reservations. Nine risks, none of them softened. The accessibility one goes further than I have seen any project go:

> "Roughly 1 in 6 people globally has some form of disability, and the World Bank estimates ~3 billion people still don't own a smartphone. A trust system that gives them a structurally lower ceiling isn't neutral, it codifies the existing digital divide into the trust layer itself."
> "...it is not a slower climb to the same place. A software-only identity starts at 0.50 and 0.50 is also where it stops."
> "...the very gap that makes an honest user's loss unrecoverable also makes a dishonest user's record sheddable. This is why what Web4 has is a Sybil *resistance* strategy rather than a Sybil *prevention* guarantee. It is a genuine hole... and naming it here is not the same as having closed it."

Risk 9 is a project admitting its own shipped software has a safety gate an agent already routes around. I did not expect that and it bought back a lot of the credibility the ATP arithmetic had spent.

One thing I noticed and could not resolve on my own: a software-only identity is capped at 0.50, and 0.50 is also the line that, if you stay below it, is fatal. So that whole tier sits permanently on the exact threshold, with no headroom above it. Nothing I read said whether that is a knife edge or whether something protects it.

### Glossary (74:00)

The acronym quick index (ATP, ADP, LCT, T3, V3, CI, MRH, MCP, RDF, R6, R7, each with a one-line plain-English gloss) is linked from the top of every page and is the single most useful navigational thing on the site. I used it three times. "MRH, Markov Relevancy Horizon, math-flavored jargon you can ignore; it just means how far your trust reaches" is how all jargon should be introduced.

## Unanswered Questions

1. **How does anyone become solvent?** The best contributor in the site's own worked example loses 8 ATP per task on a 100 ATP budget. Recharge is capped at cost, so self-initiated work never grows a balance. Commissioned work is the only channel that does, and the site says outright that "how someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question." Meanwhile the cold-start sketch has members happily contributing through actions 11 to 100. With what? This is the one question where the site's honesty on each individual page adds up to a hole across pages.
2. **Why would anyone avoid dying?** 112 ATP and 0.54 trust after a life that included spam and death, against 100 and 0.50 for a newcomer who did nothing wrong. If the answer is "she lost time and standing," say so.
3. **Does rebirth raise trust or lower it?** `/first-contact` shows 0.51 → 0.54. `/karma-consequences` gives `prev_trust × 0.95`, which on that same number gives 0.4845. The karma page names the conflict and declines to resolve it, which is fair, but it does not tell me which one the site is currently teaching.
4. **What answers the fifth root cause?** Five root causes, five requirements, four mechanisms, no map.
5. **What happens to a software-only identity that dips below 0.50?** Its ceiling *is* 0.50. Can it climb back to a line it is capped at, or is the fallback tier permanently one bad interaction from the fatal side?
6. **Do I have to pay to read?** "About 1 ATP to read something." The link promising to explain this is broken, so I still do not know.

## Honest Assessment

This site is unusually good at the thing most projects are worst at, and has a specific weakness in the thing most projects find easy.

The thing it is good at is telling me what is not true. `/running-now` refuses to let a badge imply adoption. `/what-could-go-wrong` names nine risks including one its own shipped software currently exhibits, and writes "naming it here is not the same as having closed it." `/atp-economics` says its central question about newcomers is unsolved. `/karma-consequences` volunteers that its own formula disagrees with another page. I have read a lot of project sites and I have not read one that does this. By the end I believed every maturity claim on the site without checking, which is exactly the currency this project needs.

The weakness is the illustrations. Every place the site stops stating rules and starts telling a story about a person, the arithmetic slips in the same direction: toward the system being kinder than the rules allow. Alice earns "more than she spent." Helping "costs 4 energy but earns 10 back." The newcomer's 10 "comes back." Hannah "barely loses any" while losing eight times her budget. Dying leaves you richer than never having lived. None of these are big lies, and several sit inches away from a caveat that would have caught them. But they all lean the same way, and once I noticed the pattern I started reading the friendly numbers with suspicion, which is a shame on a site that has earned trust everywhere else.

There is a structural version of the same problem. The corrections exist; they are just not where the claims are. The karma page knows First Contact disagrees with it. The ATP page knows self-initiated work cannot profit. `/day-in-web4` prints the correct rule directly above scenarios that break it. The site is arguing with itself correctly and quietly, on the pages a reader following the recommended path will not open. If I had stopped where the site invited me to stop, after step 3 of 5, I would have left with three wrong beliefs and no idea any of them were contested.

The broken anchors are a smaller thing but they compound it. Ten times the site says "the answer to the objection you just had is right here" and then does not take me there. The one on `/how-it-works` about why reading costs anything is the worst, because it appears at the exact moment the objection forms and is the only place the site offers to answer it.

What I would fix first, if it were one thing: make the friendly numbers obey the rules the site is proud of. The rules are the good part. The stories are what people remember.

Would I come back? Yes, mostly for `/what-could-go-wrong`, which is the most interesting page. Would I recommend it? To someone technical, yes, with the note that the worked examples are softer than the mechanics. To someone non-technical, not yet, because the first thing they would take away is a walkthrough where every good deed turns a profit, and that is the one part that is not true.
