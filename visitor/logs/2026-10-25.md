### How It Works (35:00)
- Long, but the structure (Birth, Life, Death, Rebirth) is clear. The aliveness diagram is a good idea.

The CI page says CI works with "the other three Web4 foundational pillars" and leaves MRH out. I could not settle on whether there are 3, 4 or 5.
- **Costs outside your neighborhood:** Aliveness step 2 says *"Actions outside your MRH don't cost energy and don't move trust."* The MRH card on the same page says spam to strangers *"still costs energy"*. /trust-neighborhood says messaging outside your MRH is *"Very expensive (spam prevention)"*. That's three answers to one question: free, costs something, very expensive.
- **Rebirth eligibility vs the 0.50 start (new and serious for me):** the eligibility check is *"Overall T3 score ≥ 0.5"*. Everyone starts at exactly 0.50. So a newcomer who does nothing, earns nothing and runs out of ATP is still "eligible", which contradicts *"recoverable if you built trust, permanent if not"* and ATP's *"Bad actors who never earned that trust? They die for good."* Going the other way: if your energy runs out during a single dip to 0.49 (which every page says is "recoverable, not fatal"), the ≥ 0.5 check reads that one moment and you get "No rebirth. Permanent death." So a single stumble *is* fatal if it happens to coincide with running out of energy. The pages insist a stumble is never fatal.

Using the page's own update rule, a perfect-quality action (quality 1.0) raises the composite by about 0.008 (0.4×0.01 + 0.3×0.008 + 0.3×0.006). Getting +0.15 takes at least 18 *perfect* actions, or about 26 at the page's own 0.85 example. The spend, the action count and the trust climb can't all be true together.
- The "none of these lives ends at 0 ATP, so ... read *ended* not *died*" note is honest, but I finished the section not knowing what ends a life other than the two deaths.
- Spam costs: *"Spam message: Cost 5 ATP"* and *"20 spam messages = -100 ATP"*, but the Death Spiral says *"Spend 10 ATP per low-quality post"*. That's minor, since a spam message and a low-quality post may differ.
- Agent chains: five 0.9 agents give 0.59 end to end, and a chain halts if any agent drops below threshold. A 7-hop chain of 0.9 agents gives 0.478. Does the whole pipeline halt even though every member is at 0.9? It isn't said.

## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest, and I mean that as a compliment. Almost every claim carries its own "here's what isn't settled" note, and the status pages (/running-now, /onramp's "edge that is not there", Hestia's "a record of governed activity, not all activity") made me trust the authors more. The problem framing on /why-web4 and the 2-minute page are genuinely good. I could explain the pitch to a friend.

The trouble is that the honesty is mostly *local*. Each page hedges its own claims well, but pages disagree with each other, and a few disagree with themselves, on the basic rules a newcomer most needs. Who can see me? Does stepping outside my neighborhood cost anything? Is there a CI death line? Is ATP capped? Does karma reward or punish the same kind of death? I kept a notepad, and by the CI page I'd stopped believing any single sentence until I'd seen it on two pages. That's the opposite of what an onramp should leave me with.

The pattern behind most of my notes is the number **0.50**. It is the universal start, the survival line, the rebirth threshold, and the software-only ceiling all at once. Every time I followed it, something broke. Starting where you die makes a one-time dip fatal if it coincides with running out of energy. Rebirth eligibility gets granted to people who built nothing. A one-month absence puts a newcomer under the line. And the only "Running" product's users are frozen on it. The site *names* each of these locally ("zero margin", "not settled", "filed upstream"), but no page owns the whole knot, and I suspect that's where a real reader gives up.

The worked numbers are the other weak spot. The walkthroughs say "illustrative", but when I actually did the arithmetic (Novice life, quality ramp, multiple-path trust, 12 of 13 "pays for itself") they don't close even on their own terms. For a framework whose whole pitch is "verifiable records instead of declared trust", numbers that don't add up hurt more than they would elsewhere.

Would I come back? Maybe, to see whether the pages start agreeing with each other. I'd send it to a technical friend with the warning "read /running-now and /why-web4's top half; take the numbers with salt."
