## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: one sentence, "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules." Clear and modest. The word "proposed" set my expectations correctly before anything else did.
- The page told me three times, in the same order, what to read first: the 2-minute intro, Why Web4, First Contact. I did what it said.
- Four cards (Core Standard, Hub, Hestia, Hardbound) with tags I did not yet understand ("Substrate", "Community Scale"). I skipped them for now.
- Confusion point: the Hardbound card promises "audit trails built to stand up to regulators." Three pages later the honesty ledger says its hardware binding is unvalidated and the threat model lists about ten open gaps. The card reads like a product pitch; the ledger reads like a lab notebook. Same site, two tones.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (00:02)
- Genuinely plain English. "Trust is earned through witnessed behavior, not granted from above" landed on first read. The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast was the single most useful paragraph on the site.
- I learned the three maturity tags here (Spec, Reference, Running) and that Running "does not claim adoption," which I appreciated.
- Small snag: it says "pip install web4-core" alone; later pages say "pip install web4-core web4-trust". Not a problem, just noticed.

### The Reading Path (00:05)

The banner's label borrows the badge word for a piece that does not hold the badge. This banner recurs on /first-contact, /why-web4, and /how-it-works.

### Why Web4? (00:08)
- The five "Problems You Already Know" are well written and I recognised every one. "Why Previous Solutions Failed" giving each failed approach credit for what it got right made me trust the author more.
- The four mechanism cards were readable cold. Then I hit the "All four, in one moment" example directly under them and got my first real contradiction:
  - The MRH card: "Everyone stays visible - what changes is weight. A stranger's message reaches you..."
  - The example, two paragraphs down: "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply at all - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread."
  - So which is it: everyone sees everything at different weight, or strangers see nothing? I could not resolve this from the page.
- Same example, last line: "fractions of a cent of energy." I read on to the FAQ, which says ATP has "no price against money" and "you can't sell it for dollars." If it has no price, where did the cents come from?
- The 72-question FAQ is honestly labelled as reference depth. I read about ten answers. The "is this deployed?" answer is the most candid thing I have read on a project site: 424 attack vectors, ~85% detection, no red team yet, no live network. I believed it.
- FAQ snags:
  - "Is the 0.5 threshold universal?" says each society sets its own (0.3 or 0.7 as examples). Every other page says 0.5 flatly. And the software-only trust ceiling is 0.50, so in a 0.7 society a software-only user would be below the line from birth. Nobody connects those two facts.
  - The youthful-mistakes answer says "trust below 0.5 = permanent death," dropping the "and stays there" clause the same page is otherwise careful about.

### First Contact (00:25)

### Karma & Consequences (01:03)
- Interactive Life 1: trust 0.50 → 0.64, ATP 100 → 0, Karma −15, reborn with 70. The formula below: next ATP = 100 + 2 × karma, next trust = previous × 0.95.
- This is the rebirth collision in full. On First Contact, dying at ATP 0 with trust 0.51 earns +12. Here, dying at ATP 0 with trust 0.64 earns −15 and is called "poor resource management." Same event, better trust, opposite sign. The disclaimers say the AMOUNT is unsettled; they do not say the SIGN is. Yet it flips.
- The trust line: First Contact goes UP at rebirth (0.51 → 0.54); here it goes DOWN (× 0.95). The page admits this too. Admitting it twice is not the same as resolving it.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — trust from witnessed behavior rather than platform say-so or key possession; the 2-minute page did this alone
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — /onramp's "one shape at three scales" and the dependency diagram
- [x] What LCT means — a device-anchored identity whose ceiling depends on the chip and the device count
- [ ] What ATP/ADP means — partial. I understand budget-not-money and spend-then-recharge. The quality-ramp numbers and the "fractions of a cent" line left me unsure how payouts actually work
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — three numbers per role, fixed blend, slow 0.02-step updates
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — Running Now is excellent at this
- [x] Why any of this matters — the five problems on Why Web4 and the AI-agents framing on the landing page

## Unanswered Questions
1. If I die at ATP 0 with trust above 0.5, is my karma positive or negative? First Contact says positive at 0.51; the karma page says negative at 0.64.
2. Does someone outside my neighborhood see my message at all, and does sending it cost me anything?
3. If a society sets its survival line at 0.7, can a software-only identity (ceiling 0.50) exist there at all?
4. Can a long absence alone kill a hardware-anchored identity? The decay arithmetic says a baseline identity falls well below 0.5 after six months away; the site says this is "filed with the standard's maintainers."
5. What does the lab behind Web4 actually run day to day: a hub instance, or hestia on four machines?
6. How does a newcomer get their first commissioned task? Three pages call this "an open question on this stack," which is honest but leaves the whole net-positive story resting on it.
7. Is 0.5 the CI access threshold the same line as the 0.5 raw-trust survival line, or just the same number?

## Honest Assessment
This is the most candid project site I have read. It tells you on nearly every page what is real, what is simulated, and what is still being argued about, and it tells you about its own holes (the policy gate that two environment variables bypass, the hub that cannot verify a hardware identity) before you can find them. I came away trusting the authors, which, for a project about trust, is the right first result.

The cost of that candour is that the site is now arguing with itself in public. The honest "about that number" boxes on How It Works, First Contact, and Karma each disclose that the other two pages disagree, but none of them resolves it, and a reader walks away holding three rebirth models with opposite signs. The same pattern shows up smaller: four and five mechanisms, visible and invisible strangers, costs and no costs outside the neighborhood, an alive/dead line that does not decide death. Each one on its own is a shrug. Together they make the careful reader wonder whether the small numbers that DO check out (and many did: every per-action trust delta, every chain product, every composite blend I tried) were checked or just happened to land.

The concept pages are each individually excellent. The onramp pages are honest and concrete. Running Now should be page two, not page five. And the 2-minute page is good enough that I would send it to anyone.

What the site taught me versus what I inferred: it taught me the four pieces, the three badges, the ceilings, and why actions should cost something. I inferred, from arithmetic the site did not do, that one-device identities cannot hold high-threshold roles and that long absences are dangerous. The site then confirmed the second inference itself in a side box, which was oddly reassuring.
