## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: "Trust, built into the internet." Clear and calm. The second sentence tells me this is about proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, by what rules. I understood the pitch in under a minute, and the definition of "witness" (observe an action, attest to it, stake your reputation on it) is given right there. Good.
- The four cards (Core Standard, Hub, Hestia, Hardbound) are where it gets dense fast: "seven roles, a signed founding law, an append-only witnessed ledger, and sealed member channels" in one card. I did not know what any of those were yet. I let it wash over me.
- "The vocabulary" lists six things with acronyms (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, Karma). No expansions here, just links. Fine for a landing page, but it is a wall of letters.
- "Where this actually stands" is the part I trusted most. It says outright this is R&D, not a product, and that Hardbound's hardware binding is "not yet validated on real devices."
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".
- Confusion points: "pilot-ready" for the hub is used with no explanation here. The "Aa" in the footer is a mystery until other pages tell me it is the glossary button.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes /tldr (00:04)

  - "Unresettable reputation is narrower than it sounds: it is a property of hardware anchoring, not of Web4 as such." Honest, but it lands right after "Bad behavior follows you" and partly takes it back.

### Why Web4? /why-web4 (00:11)
- The five problems (spam, siloed reputation, unlimited fresh starts, AI agents, platform control) are all things I recognise. "Why previous solutions failed" is fair to the alternatives, which made me trust the page more.
- The four proposals each get a plain name first and the acronym second. This is where I first learned what the letters mean: ATP = Allocation Transfer Packets, LCT = Linked Context Token, T3 = Trust Tensor (Talent, Training, Temperament), MRH = Markov Relevancy Horizon ("math-flavored jargon you can ignore", which made me laugh).
- "All four, in one moment" (a forum question and a reply) is a good idea, but two of its four bullets later turned out to contradict other parts of the site (visibility, and "fractions of a cent").
- Then the FAQ. The page says it is reference depth and to skim. It is 72 questions. I read maybe 25 properly and skimmed the rest. Many answers end in an "Honest caveat", which I liked, but the sheer volume of numbers in here (0.5, 0.02, 0.7 per hop, 5%, 1.4x, 93%, 424 attack vectors, ~85%, 0.50/0.75/0.85/0.90) is overwhelming, and I started noticing that several of them fight with other pages.
- Confusion points:
  - "Is this deployed anywhere?" opens with "Both". I had to read on to find out both what. The answer under it is good, though: "research prototype with substantial working code, not production."
  - FAQ on what backs ATP says "capped issuance (a finite pool per community)". The ATP page later says "No scarcity cap."
  - FAQ on who sees my messages says everyone can see everything, weight just drops. The worked example above it says a stranger five hops away "never sees the thread."
  - "Newcomer premium 1.4x": so newcomers pay 40% more? The First Contact page then tells me "Equal start for everyone."
  - Trust death "below 0.5 and stays there" is permanent. How long is "stays"? Nobody says.
- Clicked: First Contact.

### How It Works /how-it-works (00:36)
- Long (25 minutes is honest). This is where the system finally hangs together as birth, life, death, rebirth. The price list is concrete: reading ~1 ATP, posting 10 to 20, creating a task 15 to 30, broadcasting 20 to 50. The four earning cases (commissioned +25, commissioned +40, own post 0 at best, spam -5) made the "recharge refunds, payment earns" idea click.
- The governance example (Open Science Collective, Dr. Chen's 40% overlap plagiarism case, the appeal) is the most human thing on the site. I understood it completely.
- Confusion points:
  - The page says "five interlocking systems" at the top, "Web4 has four core systems" above the diagram (MRH missing), and Why Web4 said four ideas plus a Coherence Index that is "not a fifth mechanism." So is it four or five, and which one is the odd one out? Two pages give different answers (CI is the extra on one, MRH is the missing one on the other).
  - Spam costs 5 ATP. A real post costs 10 to 20. So spam is the cheapest thing you can do other than read. "Send 20 spam messages = -100 ATP" means every new identity gets 20 spam messages out of its free starting grant. And software identities are free. I do not see where the "expensive" comes in.
  - Life 1 "The Novice": spent 60, earned 105, trust 0.5 to 0.65, over "a dozen+ actions." I tried the arithmetic the page gives me. A very good action (quality 0.85) moves Talent +0.007, Training +0.0056, Temperament +0.0042; blended with the 0.4/0.3/0.3 weights that is about +0.0057 per action. To climb 0.15 takes about 26 such actions, all excellent. 60 ATP over 26 actions is about 2.3 ATP each, but the cheapest contribution on the price list is a 10 ATP post. I cannot make the Novice's life add up.
  - Karma: Life 1 ends with 145 and is "reborn with 145." First Contact was 100 + 12. The page lists three possible rules and says it is "not settled", and also that these lives did not die at all ("ended", not "died"), and that the standard names no way for a life to end other than the two deaths. So what ended them?
  - "Above 70%, near-full" payout here; the ATP page's table pays 30 of 50 at 70%.
  - "T3 → ATP: higher trust gives a better earning rate." Other pages say the payout ramp reads quality, not trust or seniority.
  - "Game all five simultaneously? Mathematically impractical", a few screens after "the design argument for them, not a measured result."
  - "ATP prices adjust dynamically based on supply and demand", dropped in near the end with no explanation, after the ATP material insisted there is no market.

### Trust Tensor /trust-tensor (01:52)
- The surgeon/mechanic framing works. Alice's three roles table (analyst 90, PM 74, mechanic 28) with the arithmetic shown is the clearest numeric example on the site, and the arithmetic checks out.
- "You don't need the math" for the word tensor: thank you.
- Confusion points:
  - A six-month break leaves Temperament "essentially gone" and Training at half. Plugging that into the page's own weights for someone at 0.7 across the board gives roughly 0.28 + 0.105 + ~0 = 0.39, under the 0.5 survival line. So a long holiday is trust death? The page says this is "not settled" and "filed with the standard's maintainers." Honest, but alarming.
  - Alice's 90% analyst score needs "a hardware-anchored identity plus three device witnesses." Nobody can have that today, since hardware binding is not validated and multi-device has never been run on a second device.
  - Scores are "calibrated probabilities": 0.7 means "~70% cooperative behavior." But 0.5 is also "neutral" and "we do not know you." Is a newcomer someone who cooperates half the time?

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed open standard where trust comes from other parties witnessing and signing off on what you do, rather than from a platform's say-so or from holding a key. The site taught me this, mostly on /tldr.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one shared vocabulary/library (the standard) and three programs that run it for a person, a group, and an organisation. Taught, clearly, three times over.
- [x] What LCT means: Linked Context Token, an identity that lives on your device (ideally in its security chip) and is vouched for by your other devices. Taught on /why-web4 and /lct-explainer. The details (what "Token" is, what the label is) wobble.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means: Allocation Transfer Packets, a per-person energy budget spent on every action; ADP is the receipt for spent energy that turns back into ATP when others confirm the work was useful. Taught. I do not understand the actual payout numbers.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three scores (Talent, Training, Temperament) kept separately per role, blended 0.4/0.3/0.3. Taught well on /trust-tensor.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: mostly yes. Packages installable, hub runnable but not deployed, hestia used daily by the lab only, hardbound not validated on hardware, federation not built. The one thing I could not settle is whether the lab runs a hub.
- [~] Why any of this matters: I get the problem (agents act, nobody can prove what they did). I am less convinced the answer holds up, because the piece that runs today keeps a record the agent can skip, and the economics examples do not add up when I check them.

## Unanswered Questions

## Honest Assessment
The concept landed. After the landing page and the 2-minute overview I could explain Web4 to a friend: agents and people act, others witness and sign, the record follows you, and four pieces of software let you run that at different scales. The site is unusually honest about status. I have rarely seen a project say "the daily use counted so far is the building lab's own" or "two environment variables suffice" about its own flagship. That honesty is the main reason I would come back.

The trouble starts when I check the numbers. Every time the site gives me a worked example, I can find another page whose worked example disagrees with it: karma is +12 or -15 for the same death, the payout table does not follow its own rule, the Novice's life cannot be assembled from the price list, messages are visible to everyone or only within three hops, ATP is capped or uncapped, there are four systems or five. Most of these come with a nearby "not settled" or "one modelling choice" note, and I believe the notes. But the effect of many small disclaimers is that I stop trusting any specific number, and then the pages that are made of numbers (ATP, karma, the second half of How It Works) stop teaching me anything. I would rather have one consistent toy example carried through the whole site, clearly labelled as a toy, than five examples that each apologise for the others.

The second thing that stuck is a gap between the pitch and what runs. The pitch is unfakeable identity and proof of what an agent did. What runs is a software identity that is capped at the starting trust value and a record the agent can route around. Both facts are stated, in the right places, by the site itself. But I had to assemble them from three pages, and the pages with the exciting examples (Alice climbing to 0.62, the analyst at 0.90) never say "nobody can do this yet." A naive reader who stops after First Contact leaves with a rosier picture than one who reads to the end.

Smaller things: the product pages (hub, hestia, hardbound, onramp, standard) all open by sending me to the TL;DR for three terms the TL;DR does not define. The FAQ is enormous and I think most of the contradictions I found live in the seam between it and the concept pages. And the LCT page, which is otherwise the most carefully argued, contradicts itself on small facts (what the Token is, what Secure Enclave is, what the label is) often enough that I started double-checking it.

Did the site teach me, or did I infer? The ideas were taught. The reconciliations were mine: where two pages disagreed, I had to guess which one was current, and I do not know if I guessed right.
