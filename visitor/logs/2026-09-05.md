## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): trust from witnessed behavior instead of platform say-so or key possession. The Web2/3/4 comparison did this in three lines.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one library set, three programs at three sizes. Clear by the compose page.
- [~] What LCT means: a key on my own device, stronger if it lives in a security chip; more devices raise the ceiling. I did not finish the page and could not say what "Context" in the name adds.
- [~] What ATP/ADP means: a budget you spend to act and earn back when the recipient confirms value; ADP is the receipt. I understood the idea. I do not understand whether out-of-neighborhood actions cost any.
- [~] What Trust Tensors are: three scores (talent, training, temperament) per role, blended into one number. Whether the blend weights vary per role, I could not tell.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: Running Now is clear. The hub page then unclear-ed it.
- [x] Why any of this matters: the five problems on Why Web4.

## Unanswered Questions
1. Does sending something to someone outside my trust neighborhood cost energy or not? (Three different answers.)
2. If an identity dies of energy death with good trust, does its next life start ahead or behind? (Alice: ahead. Three Lives: behind.)
3. Does the lab run a live hub society today, or not? Running Now and the hub page disagree.
4. Everyone actually using this today runs a software-only identity capped at 0.50, which is also the survival line. Does that mean the whole fleet is one dip from restricted features? The LCT page says a dip is survivable; it does not say what daily life at the ceiling is like.
5. Can software-only identities witness other identities or not? The LCT page says generally not; the hestia and hub pages describe the fleet doing it.
6. What ended Life 1 with 145 ATP in the How It Works example, if not energy death and not trust death? The page says it declines to name it.
7. Where did Alice's +0.03 trust at rebirth come from?
8. What does "witness" mean in "your own device is your day-zero witness"? The LCT page later says the word has two senses; the TL;DR uses it before either is defined.
9. Who is being quoted in the four onramp cards on the landing page? The sentences are in quotation marks.

## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest and unusually long. Every page tells me what is not built, which is rare and which made me trust the parts that are. The first three pages in the prescribed order (TL;DR, Why Web4, First Contact) do their job: I came in knowing nothing and left them knowing what problem this solves and what the four moving parts are called.

Then How It Works doubles the reading time and introduces the contradictions. Nearly every friction item above is the same shape: two pages (or two paragraphs on one page) state a number or a rule differently, and each one sounds authoritative. The site has clearly worked hard on hedging amounts ("one model, not the rule") but the hedges do not cover signs, thresholds, or whether something costs anything at all. A naive reader cannot tell a modelling choice from a mistake, so every mismatch reads as a mistake.

The pieces that worked: the Web2/3/4 triplet, the five problems, the "find the row that sounds like you" list, the ordinary Tuesday with Hestia, the plagiarism walkthrough, the credit-card-abroad analogy for coherence. Those are all concrete, all short, and none of them contain a number that another page disputes. More of that; less of the rest.

Would I return? Maybe, if I had a reason to run an AI agent under my own rules, because Hestia's page convinced me that part exists. I would not send a non-technical friend here; they would stop at "three scales of the same posture".
