### /why-web4 (08:00)
- The problem section ("Problems You Already Know") is strong and relatable. The "What it gets right" note on each failed solution felt fair.

A stranger's message reaches you." The example says: "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply at all - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread." The FAQ "Who can see my messages?" then says: "Everyone can see them - nothing is hidden." So which is it? This is the most basic question I had about the mechanism, and the page answers it both ways in the same scroll.

- "CI: not a fifth mechanism". Noted, and then contradicted on the next page (see below).
- The FAQ is huge (72 questions). I skimmed it via the index. It's honest, but sometimes the honesty undoes the headline. "Bad behavior follows you" becomes "only at the hardware tier; software-only records stay sheddable". Recovery becomes "software-only users start over from zero".
- The 5% circular-farming example says "After just 4 **round trips** ... nearly 19%", but it shows 4 single transfers (two round trips). 4 real round trips (8 transfers) would lose about 34%.
- Death/rebirth FAQ: "Crossing below 0.5 ... is recoverable; only staying below it is fatal", then the caveat further down says "trust below 0.5 = permanent death ... But this threshold is high; ordinary teenage mistakes don't approach it." Everyone **starts** at 0.5. How is the line I start on "high"? One bad post would put a newcomer below it.
- Threshold FAQ: 0.5 is "a society-level parameter, not a protocol constant" (a science society might use 0.7). Software-only identities are capped at 0.50. So a software-only person can never meet a 0.7 society. Nobody says that out loud.

  - Step 1: one post moves trust 0.50 → 0.52. /how-it-works and /trust-tensor say a single action moves trust by 0.02 × (quality − 0.5), so at most +0.01 even for perfect work. (The page does say "a single action moves trust far less than the steps here show", but only on /how-it-works, not here.)
  - Step 6: Alice dies of energy exhaustion at trust 0.51 and gets **+12 karma** (reborn at 112 ATP, trust 0.54). On /karma-consequences, an agent that does good work (trust 0.50 → **0.64**) and also dies of ATP exhaustion gets **−15 karma** (reborn at 70 ATP), because "premature death - ATP exhaustion indicates poor resource management" is a *negative* karma source. Same kind of death, better record, opposite sign. I can't build any mental model of karma from these two.
  - Rebirth: trust *rises* through death (0.51 → 0.54). The karma page's formula multiplies trust by 0.95 at rebirth. The page admits "not settled", but then I don't know what to believe.

## Honest Assessment
The first ten minutes are excellent. The landing page and /tldr gave me a crisp idea: AI agents act, and we can't prove what they did. Web4 says trust should come from witnessed behaviour. Four pieces, three scales, and an honest status box. I'd have recommended the site on that alone.

Things go wrong once I start following the numbers. The site keeps telling me "the rules are real" and showing worked arithmetic, so I checked it, and a lot of it doesn't close. The Novice's trust climb is impossible under the page's own update rule and spend. The quality-ramp table fits neither of the two descriptions printed beside it. Karma for the same kind of death has opposite signs on two pages. The coherence page calls 0.5 an alive/dead line and then tells me CI has nothing to do with death. These aren't "not settled" disclaimers; they're the pages disagreeing with themselves. Each new caveat ("one modelling choice, not the rule", "read for the shape") makes me trust the worked examples less, not more. By /karma-consequences I'd stopped believing any specific number.

The qualitative contradictions bother me more than the arithmetic, because they're about whether the idea works at all. Whether strangers can see my message and whether reaching them costs energy are the heart of the spam argument, and three pages give three answers.

On the honesty side: the site is far more candid than any project page I've seen. The software-only equity hole, the bypassable hestia gate (Risk 9) and the "we will not invent a rule the standard does not contain" notes are admirable. But the candour is unevenly placed. The most consequential admission (the one Running piece cannot yet prove what an agent did when the gate is bypassed) is at the bottom of the risk page, while the landing page leads with "proving what an agent did".

If I could ask for one thing: run one consistency pass that only checks numbers and yes/no claims across pages (visibility, out-of-MRH cost, the CI meaning of 0.5, karma sign, mechanism count). Fewer caveats and more agreement would teach me more.
