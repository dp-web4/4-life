## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — trust from witnessed behaviour, not platform declaration or key possession. The /tldr Web2/Web3/Web4 paragraph did it.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — one substrate, three scales. /onramp did it; the landing page alone did not.
- [x] What LCT means — a device-anchored key with a witnessed record, and a ceiling set by the chip. The site taught me; the two formats muddied it.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means — spend to act, receipt on spend, refund on confirmation, payment on commission. "Recharge refunds, payment earns" stuck. The ramp numbers did not.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — three numbers per role, composite 0.4/0.3/0.3, decay per dimension. Taught, not inferred.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — hestia (software-only, gate bypassable), hub (runnable, no live instance... except maybe the lab's), hardbound (unvalidated hardware), federation (spec only). /running-now is excellent.
- [x] Why any of this matters — the agent-accountability framing landed on the landing page and never let go.

Caveat on every tick: I understand the *shapes*. I could not reproduce a single worked example on the site from its own stated rules, and I tried four (Novice, quality ramp, Alice's arc, karma sign).

## Honest Assessment
This is the most honest technical explainer I have read, and that is also its problem. Every page has a paragraph saying "this number is a modelling choice, not the rule" or "the standard does not settle this." I counted at least eight such fences. After the fourth, I stopped reading the numbers as information and started reading them as decoration, which is a shame, because the site clearly wants the numbers to carry the argument (the whole First Contact walkthrough is numbers).

The shape of the idea came through cleanly and early: actions cost energy, quality earns it back, identity lives in a chip, trust is per role, consequences persist. I could explain that to a friend after the /tldr page. What I could not do after twenty pages is run any of the site's own examples forward. The Novice's life does not close under the site's step sizes. The quality ramp has three shapes. Alice's rebirth and the karma widget's rebirth point in opposite directions and both pages say so without fixing it. The neighborhood is a wall on one page and a weight on the next. These are not deep conceptual gaps; they are the kind of thing a single pass with a calculator would catch, and they sit on the pages that most want me to trust them.

The second thing I will remember: the site's candour about Hestia. The only piece that is Running ships a software identity capped at the survival line, with a policy gate two environment variables can skip, and a witness chain that records only what went through the gate. The site says all of that itself, on three pages, in plain words. I believed the rest of the site more because of it. But I also noticed that the Running badge, the "hestia evolves real trust tensors" banner, and the "we run one" line on the hub page are all doing more work than those caveats allow.

Would I come back? Yes, to /running-now and /what-could-go-wrong, which are the two pages that never made a claim they then had to walk back. Would I recommend it? To an engineer who enjoys a site that argues with itself in public, yes. To the "curious non-technical person" the landing page is written for, not until the worked examples agree with their own rules.
