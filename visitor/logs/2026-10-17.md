### /how-it-works (35:00)
- Long, but the table of contents helps.
- Says "five foundational mechanisms" at the top, "Web4 has four core systems" at the diagram, then "The five systems aren't independent" two lines later.
- MRH contradicts itself on cost: the MRH card says spam out of network "arrives with zero weight, and sending it still costs energy"; the aliveness step 2 directly below says "Actions outside your MRH don't cost energy and don't move trust." So does spamming strangers cost me ATP or not? This matters: it's the whole anti-spam story.
- ATP earning table is the first place that explains commission vs self-initiated work. Helpful. But "Work you initiate can hold you steady" isn't true once reading costs 1 ATP each: a non-commissioned good citizen drifts down.
- Life 1 "Novice" arithmetic didn't close for me:
  - Spent 60 ATP across "a dozen+ actions". Posting costs 10-20 ATP each (same page), so 60 ATP buys 3-6 posts, not a dozen+.
  - Trust 0.50 → 0.65. With the page's own rule (+0.007/+0.0056/+0.0042 at 0.85 quality, composite ≈ +0.0057 per action), +0.15 needs ~26 actions at 0.85 quality, or ~18 at perfect quality. "A dozen+" undercounts, and 60 ATP can't pay for 18-26 actions.
  - It also says "earn-back on your own spend is capped at what you spent" and then, a paragraph later, "quality contributions earned more than their cost." Those two sentences disagree unless all the quality work was commissioned.
- Also "talent ↑, training ↑" for the Novice, but every quality action also moves temperament by the rule shown.
- The three-lives example has lives that "ended" without either kind of death, and the page explains at length that it won't name why they ended. Honest, but then why is the main worked example built on lives that end for no defined reason?

## Honest Assessment
The site has a clear, compelling top half. The landing page, the 2-minute version, and Running Now are excellent: I knew what this was, why someone would want it, and exactly how real each piece is. The honesty is not performative. Risk 9 openly says the one running product's gate can be stepped around with two environment variables, and the site keeps that in view instead of burying it. I trust the authors more because of it.

The bottom half is where it frays, and it frays in a specific way: **numbers and worked examples disagree with each other across pages, and sometimes within a page**. I kept doing the arithmetic, because the site invites it ("if you can do the arithmetic, you can reach that question yourself"), and it often didn't close: the quality-ramp table, the Novice's 60-ATP life, the karma signs, Sam's confirmer average. Separately, a few *qualitative* claims flip: whether strangers see your posts, whether out-of-network actions cost energy, whether ATP is capped, whether 0.5 CI is life or death. Each page has a careful "this is not settled" note. But many of the conflicts I hit aren't unsettled questions; they're two pages saying opposite things about something the site presents as settled.

There's also a lot of hedging per paragraph. Many explanations are followed by a paragraph explaining what the explanation doesn't mean, then a link to where that's discussed. Individually each caveat is fair; together they make /how-it-works and /lct-explainer feel like reading a negotiated settlement. I'd trade half of the caveats for making the examples agree with each other.

Net: I believe the problem is real and the people are honest. I'm not yet confident I could explain the ATP/karma mechanics to someone else without contradicting one of the pages.
