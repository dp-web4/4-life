### Why Web4? (07:00)
- The problem section is strong. I nodded along to all five problems.

  - The FAQ "Who can see my messages?": "Everyone can see them - nothing is hidden."

- "fractions of a cent of energy": the ATP section says ATP has no price in money and can't be cashed out. So how is it a fraction of a cent?
- FAQ: I opened a few. In the founding walkthrough, "each quality contribution moves the needle by ~0.02." Further down, the "why these numbers" answer says the step is "0.02 × (quality − 0.5)", which tops out at 0.01 even for perfect work. One is double the other.
- In the pseudonymity answer, "each LCT starts at **zero** and earns trust independently," but everywhere else everyone starts at 0.50. The /hestia and /lct-explainer pages also say "you start over from zero with a fresh identity." I can't tell whether "zero" means zero history or a 0.0 trust score. On a 0-to-1 scale, I read it as the score.
- Sam's CI example says that at low CI Sam gets "higher action costs and **less visibility**." Visibility again, when the cards said everyone stays visible.

### ATP Economics (38:00)
- The 60-second version is good. "Recharge refunds, payment earns" finally explained First Contact's refund puzzle.
- **The quality-ramp table doesn't fit its own text or its own worked example.**
  - Text: below 30% pays zero, linear from 30-70%, "above ~70% earns ~full."
  - Table, for a 50-ATP task: 50% → ~12, 70% → ~30, 85% → ~42.
  - If 70% is "~full", 70% should be about 50, not 30. Linear from 0 at 30% to 50 at 70% gives 25 at 50%, not 12.
  - The Sam example (35% → ~6) *does* fit the linear-to-full-at-70% reading (0.05/0.40 × 50 ≈ 6), so the table and the worked example follow different ramps.

### Trust Tensor (57:00)
- **The update rule can't produce the profiles the page sells.** The rule moves all three dimensions from one quality number by fixed shares (talent ×1.0, training ×0.8, temperament ×0.6). Starting from 0.5/0.5/0.5, Talent therefore always moves furthest, Temperament least, and always in the same direction. Under that rule:
  - "An ethics violation tanks Temperament without touching Talent" can't happen.
  - "High talent + low temperament = measurable pattern" (brilliant but unreliable) can't arise from behavior inside one role. The only way to get that pattern is to stop showing up (decay), which is the opposite of unreliable.
  - The bullets "Talent moves when recipients confirm novel outputs… Training moves when…" describe dimension-specific evidence that the rule doesn't have.
- **Decay vs rebuild**: 6-month break → Temperament "essentially gone" (30-day half-life), Training halved. "A few weeks of consistent activity rebuilds what faded." Rebuilding Temperament from near 0 back to about 0.6 at the max step of 0.006 per perfect action takes about 100 perfect actions, which costs 1000-2000+ ATP at the listed posting prices. "A few weeks" doesn't fit the step size this page teaches.
- Decay also goes toward **zero**, not toward the 0.5 "neutral" baseline. A newcomer who joins and then leaves for a month is below 0.5 (I get about 0.41 composite). That's a newcomer becoming *less trusted than a stranger* by doing nothing. The page raises "can decay push you below the survival line?" and honestly says it's unsettled, but with its own numbers it isn't a "can": it happens within a month to everyone who starts at 0.5.

## Honest Assessment
This is the most self-critical explainer site I've read. It flags its own unsettled points, it publishes a bypassable gate as a risk that "already happens," and the governance and plagiarism walkthrough is genuinely concrete. The 2-minute page alone would make me take the idea seriously.

What eroded my trust was the numbers. The site keeps inviting me to check ("if you can do the arithmetic above, you can reach that question yourself"), so I did, and a lot of it doesn't close: the quality-ramp table vs its own example, per-action trust steps on First Contact vs the stated rule, the Novice's spend vs the action count, and karma signs that flip between pages. Each is small. Together they form a pattern: the prose is careful and hedged, while the worked examples and tables look older and were never re-run against the rules the prose now states. For a project whose whole pitch is "provable, auditable, verifiable," illustrations that don't add up hurt more than they would on an ordinary site.

The second pattern is that the same concept gets different verbs on different pages: visible vs hidden, costs energy vs doesn't, CI 0.5 as life line vs not, four vs five systems. A naive reader can't tell which page is canon, so I end up trusting none of the specific claims and only the general shape.

The biggest thing no page says out loud: the one piece badged "Running" puts you on the software tier, and at that tier, by the site's own statements, you can't climb above where you started and you can shed your record. That's the very problem /why-web4 opens with. Each fact is disclosed somewhere, which I respect, but I had to put four pages together myself to see that today's working system doesn't yet deliver the core promise. I'd rather the site told me that in one sentence.
