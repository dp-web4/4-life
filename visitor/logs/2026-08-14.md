## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: "Trust, built into the internet." Clean, confident, not selling me anything. The subhead did the heavy lifting: "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules." That is a sentence I understood on the first read with zero background, which almost never happens on a site like this.
- The two-sentence problem statement landed: agents write code and move money, and we cannot prove what they did. Fine. I believe that.
- It defined "witness" inline the first time it used it ("observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation on the record"). Good. I did not have to go looking.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes" (/tldr), because the site told me to and because 2 minutes is a cheap bet.
- Confusion points: none serious. The footer disclaimer ("a proposed open standard and an active research project") set my expectations correctly and early, which I appreciated.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (/tldr) (02:00)
- Best page on the site. The "First, what is this site?" paragraph pre-empted the exact question I had, which was whether 4-Life is the product or the explainer. It told me 4-Life is the explainer and is not one of the four pieces. That is a small thing and it saved me real confusion.
- The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 three-way comparison is the single clearest thing here. Platform declares it / key holder is assumed to be it / witnesses observe it. I could repeat that back to someone.
- **First real snag.** The badge key. It says "The tag on each card says how far along that piece is:" and then defines exactly two tags: **Reference** and **Running**. Two paragraphs later it says "The three run in that order" and links to "the full three-tier key (Spec, Reference, Running)." I scrolled back up twice looking for the third definition. It is not there. I was told about three things after being shown two, on the page that is explicitly for people reading cold.
- The honesty tone is unusual and good: "Runnable is not the same as running in production," and "The daily use counted so far is the building lab's own, so this badge does not claim adoption." That last one is a company voluntarily deflating its own best badge. It bought a lot of credibility with me.
- One sentence slid past me and I only caught it later: on software-only devices, the day-zero witness "starts you the same way and carries less weight." I read "carries less weight" as "a bit slower to build up." It does not mean that. See HIGH 1.
- Clicked: "Read the site in order (recommended)" (/learn).

### The Reading Path (/learn) (06:30)
- Five pages, about 30 minutes, first three are about 15 minutes if that is all I have. That is a good offer and I took it.
- Each card has a "why learn this" line, which is more useful than the description. "Problem before solution. Understanding what's broken is what makes the mechanisms make sense." That told me why I should not skip ahead, so I did not.

### Why Web4? (/why-web4) (09:00)
- The five problems (spam is free, reputation does not travel, bans are trivially reset, AI multiplies deception, platforms own you) are all things I have personally experienced. This is the page that made me care.
- "You're a guest in someone else's house, always." Good line.
- Four mechanisms mapped cleanly onto the five problems. Energy budgets make spam expensive. Device-anchored identity makes bans expensive to reset. That is legible.
- The built/not-built split is blunt in a way I trust: "No live network with real users," "ATP pricing calibrated for simulations, not real markets," "No platform integrates Web4 yet," "This is research, not production. We don't attach timelines because honest research doesn't have them."
- **Snag.** Under what IS built: "Hardware integration: the core standard's TPM2 binding validated (Intel TPM 2.0, EK certificate chain through 2049)." I filed that as "the hardware part works." Three pages later /hardbound told me the hardware binding is NOT validated on-device. See MEDIUM 4.
- "424 attack vectors across 84 tracks, with ~85% detection against synthetic adversaries." I have no idea whether 85% is impressive or alarming. Fifteen percent of 424 is 64 attacks that got through. Against attackers the team invented themselves. I could not tell if I should be reassured.
- Clicked: "First Contact."

### First Contact (/first-contact) (15:00)
- The framing is honest up front: "a guided walkthrough of that idea, not a finished product," and "The rules running here are real; Web4 itself is still active research, with no live network to join yet."
- Alice's five acts (Join, Build, Stumble, Recover, Die & Reborn) is a good spine. Watching two numbers move (energy 100, trust 0.50) is much easier than reading definitions.
- **Snag, and I want to flag the ordering specifically.** Act 3 shows Alice's trust dropping to **0.48**. At that point I had been told nothing about what 0.5 means beyond "starts neutral at 0.50." I registered it as "she went a bit below average." One page later, on How It Works, I learned that below 0.5 is the death line. I then had to come back here and re-read Act 3 with my stomach dropping, before finding out on the *other* page that a dip is survivable and only staying under is fatal. The alarming number is on page 3 of the path; the relief is on page 4.
- **Snag.** Act 5. Alice ends around 0.62 trust, dies at ATP 0, and is "reborn at 0.54" with a "+12 bonus." The page calls the +12 "karma, a head start earned by the trust and reputation she built in her first life." But her trust went *down*, 0.62 to 0.54. Nothing tells me why a head start includes a trust haircut, or how 0.54 was arrived at.
- The clarification that death "means Alice's standing is suspended, she can't act until rebirth, not that her account is deleted; her identity and history persist" was necessary and I am glad it is there.
- Clicked: "How It Works."

### How It Works (/how-it-works) (22:00)
- The Birth / Life / Death / Rebirth arc is the right shape for this. Five systems (LCT, ATP, T3, CI, MRH) each got a job in one sentence.
- "Every action costs energy, from about 1 ATP to read something up to 10-20 to post." That is the first time the economy felt like a real thing with prices.
- The two-deaths distinction is well written: "ATP hits zero (energy death) or raw trust falls below 0.5 and stays there," and "crossing below 0.5 restricts your features right away and is recoverable; only staying below it is fatal." That is exactly the sentence I needed one page earlier.
- **Snag, and this is the one that broke my model.** The Life 1 / Life 2 worked example:
  - Life 1: "Born with 100 ATP, neutral T3 (0.5 all dimensions)... spent 60 ATP, earned 105 ATP... T3 to 0.65... **Ended with 145 ATP**."
  - Life 2: "**Reborn with 145 ATP** (karma carried forward)."
  - But this same page just told me a life "Ends when: ATP hits 0, or raw trust stays below 0.5." Life 1 ended with 145 ATP and rising trust. Neither condition was met. **How did Life 1 end?** Nothing on the page says. It just did.
  - And the carry-forward number does not match First Contact, where Alice restarts at 100 + 12 = 112, or Karma & Consequences, where the formula is base 100 with each karma point worth 2 ATP. Here it is simply "you keep your balance." Three pages, three different answers to "how much do I start my next life with."
- "Trust through a chain multiplies, so every hop can only shrink it. A five-step pipeline at 0.9 trust per agent yields approximately 0.59." The math is clear. But the same page says MRH bounds things to about 3 hops. So can a five-step chain exist at all? I could not resolve that.
- Clicked: "What's Running Now."

### Running Now (/running-now) (30:00)
- This is where the badge key finally exists in full: Spec, Reference, Running, all three defined. Retroactively fixes /tldr, but I had to get to page 5 for it.
- Piece-by-piece status is genuinely specific: package names and versions, "171 tests green," a ~6 MB Rust binary, "Claude Code plugin deployed on 4 machines (lab's own)."
- The hestia caveat is startlingly candid: "The witness entry is written by the same policy gate that approves an action, so an action that routes around the gate writes no entry at all." Most sites would not tell me that. Noted, and it does slightly undercut "every action leaves a verifiable record" from /tldr.
- **This page reconciled the TPM thing**: "The primitive has been hardware-tested upstream on real Intel TPM 2.0; on-device Jetson integration remains deferred." That is the sentence that makes /why-web4 and /hardbound agree. It exists on exactly one page.
- **Snag.** Hestia is badged **Running** at version **0.0.3**. The hub is badged **Reference** at version **0.1.0-alpha.0**. As a normal person, 0.1.0 sounds further along than 0.0.3. The badges say the opposite. Nothing addresses this and I spent a minute assuming I had misread one of them.
- "Understanding only: visit 4-lab.io/fleet for a witnessed society example." I clicked it.

- Honestly this was the moment the project felt like actual people doing actual work rather than a whitepaper. ### Side trips: /onramp, /karma-consequences, /lct-explainer, /what-could-go-wrong, /atp-economics, /trust-tensor, /hardbound (38:00)
- **/onramp**: "one substrate, three scales" is the cleanest summary of the four pieces anywhere on the site, better than the /tldr cards. Also honest that the hub-to-hardbound connection has no direct code integration yet.
- **/lct-explainer**: LCT = Linked Context Token, broken into Linked / Context / Token. The example identifier with the hardware half that cannot change and the label half that can was the clearest single illustration I saw. The device-count ceiling table (1 device 0.50, 2 devices 0.75, 3+ up to 0.90; TPM 0.90, Secure Enclave 0.85, FIDO2 0.75, software only 0.50) is where the whole thing clicked, and also where it got uncomfortable. See HIGH 1.
- **/karma-consequences**: "The only clean slate is a new device you had to go out and obtain." and "What is permanent is the identity, not the penalty." Both excellent. Also admits this is Sybil *resistance*, not prevention, and that software-only identity "has no device to walk away from."
- **/what-could-go-wrong**: the most impressive page on the site and I did not expect it to exist. It names the accessibility problem in plain language, including people who cannot use biometrics from age, work, motor impairment or tremors, and says the mitigations "don't fully close the gap," and that this is "an unsolved problem, not a solved one." That is a company telling me its design hurts someone. I trusted the rest of the site more after reading it.
- **/atp-economics**: ATP = Allocation Transfer Packets, ADP = Allocation Discharge Packets. The "you never manage ADP, it is background accounting" line saved me from thinking I had a second currency to learn. The recharge/payment distinction (confirmations refund at most what you spent; net gain needs commissioned work) is a sharp piece of design and clearly explained. "ATP is not a cryptocurrency" with four specific negations was the right way to kill that question.
- **/trust-tensor**: T3 = Talent, Training, Temperament, always in a role context. Weights 0.4/0.3/0.3. The decay half-lives (talent never, training 180 days, temperament 30 days) make intuitive sense: you do not forget how to be clever, but reliability has to be re-earned.
- **/hardbound**: "who did what, when, why were they trusted to do it, and can you prove it" is the best problem statement on the site. Then: "The hardware binding (TPM 2.0) is not yet validated on-device: CI uses trait-based mocks." Which is not what /why-web4 led me to believe.

## Unanswered Questions
1. Which number is the 0.5 survival line measured against: raw trust, a single T3 dimension, or the 0.4/0.3/0.3 weighted composite? Four pages use four different phrasings for it.
2. If I am software-only, capped at exactly 0.50, and 0.50 is the survival line, am I permanently one bad interaction from restriction with no way to ever climb back above it? The site is admirably honest that this is an unsolved equity problem, but it never states this specific consequence in one place.
3. How did Life 1 in the How It Works example end, given it ended with 145 ATP and rising trust and neither stated death condition was met?
4. Do I get reborn with my previous balance (145), or 100 plus karma (112)? The site shows both, confidently.
5. Why does trust go down on rebirth (0.62 to 0.54) if karma is a head start?
6. If the trust neighborhood is about 3 hops, can a five-agent delegation chain exist? Are those the same hops?
7. What kind of attacks are in the undetected 15%?
8. Besides the lab's own 4 machines, does anyone run any of this? The site is clear that the answer is no, and I respect that, but I could not tell what would have to happen for that to change.
9. If a witness entry is only written by the policy gate that approves an action, and an action routing around the gate writes no entry at all, then what does "every action leaves a verifiable record" actually promise?

## Honest Assessment

This is the rare explainer site that made me trust it more the longer I read, and the reason is that it kept telling me things that were not in its interest. "Runnable is not the same as running in production." "The daily use counted so far is the building lab's own, so this badge does not claim adoption." "An action that routes around the gate writes no entry at all." And most of all the entire /what-could-go-wrong page, which names people the design disadvantages, admits the mitigations do not close the gap, and calls it "an unsolved problem, not a solved one." I have never seen a project page do that. It is the best asset here and I would push more readers toward it, earlier.

The teaching structure works. Problem before solution, concrete before abstract, and the /learn path is honest about its own time cost. By page five I could explain Web4 to a friend: instead of a platform declaring your reputation or a key holder being assumed to own it, other participants observe what you do and sign small records of it, and those records follow you because your identity is anchored to a physical chip rather than a database row. That is a real transfer of understanding in half an hour from a cold start.

Where it falls down is arithmetic consistency, and it falls down in exactly the places where the numbers carry the most weight. The lifecycle is the emotional centre of the site: you are born, you spend, you stumble, you can die. So the death rules and the rebirth rules are the parts a reader actually holds onto. Those are the parts that do not line up. A life ends with 145 ATP on a page that says lives end at 0 ATP. Three pages give three different rebirth balances. Four pages name the fatal threshold with four different quantities, and since T3 has published weights, "raw trust below 0.5" and "composite T3 below 0.5" cannot both be the rule. None of these are hard to fix, but each one costs the site a little of the credibility that its honesty pages worked so hard to earn. It is a strange feeling to trust a project's disclosure of its own weaknesses more than I trust its worked examples.

The thing I keep turning over is the software-only 0.50 ceiling. The site states every piece of it plainly: software-only caps at 0.50, 0.50 is the neutral midpoint, 0.50 "doubles as the survival line," crossing below restricts your features. It even says outright, on /lct-explainer, "With software-only hardware, 0.5 is both where you start and the highest you can reach." So the honesty is there. But the /tldr version, on the page most people will read and many will stop at, is "carries less weight." Those are not the same statement. If I do not own a device with a security chip, the actual news is that I live permanently on the line, and the two-minute page does not tell me that. I would rather be told hard news in two minutes than discover it on page nine.

Would I come back? Yes, and specifically to see whether anyone other than the lab is running this a year from now. That is the question the whole site is quietly organised around, and it is the one it cannot answer yet, which it says so itself.
