### How It Works — /how-it-works (28:00 – 40:00)
- Long. The "five systems in plain English" box (LCT = identity, ATP = energy, T3 = trust, CI = consistency, MRH = context boundary) is the best acronym decoder on the site. I wish it were on the landing page.
- Action prices: reading ~1 ATP, posting 10–20, tasks 15–30, broadcasting 20–50.
- The commissioned vs self-chosen distinction is clever: commissioned work pays (net +25, +40), a post you chose yourself can only refund itself (net 0 at best). That's a real insight into how the economy is supposed to reward useful work.
- **Confusion (big): spam is the cheapest action on the price list.** Spam message: cost **5**. A normal post: **10–20**. In the simulator on /atp-economics: spam −5, "low-quality post (your own)" −10. The whole pitch is "make bad behavior expensive," but the price list makes sending spam *half the price* of posting. Even on the same page, the death-spiral example says spam costs "10 ATP per low-quality post," while the earlier list says 5. If spam is supposed to die by exhaustion, why is it the cheapest thing you can do?
- **Confusion:** heading says reading is "Effectively free" and then lists it at 1–2 ATP.
- **Confusion:** the page says "five interlocking systems" at the top and "Web4 has four core systems" in "How the Pieces Fit Together." I counted twice.
- **Confusion:** "Actions outside your MRH cost no energy and move no trust." But the MRH card says spam "has zero weight, and sending it still costs energy." If I spam strangers outside my neighborhood, does it cost me or not? The first sentence sounds like spamming outsiders is *free*.
- The karma lives example: Life 1 ended 145 → Life 2 started 145; Life 2 ended 130 → Life 3 started 130. So the whole balance carries over intact. The page itself flags that this is one of three possible rules. Fine, but see the karma page.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — a proposed standard so actions (by people and AI agents) leave witnessed, verifiable records and trust is earned from those records.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — substrate plus personal / community / enterprise. Clear.
- [~] What LCT means — "identity anchored to your device, witnessed by your other devices." I got the idea; the identifier format confused me.
- [~] What ATP/ADP means — energy budget + spending receipt; commissioned work earns, self-started work only refunds. Understood the *idea*; the *numbers* disagree across pages and spam is cheaper than posting.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — three scores (Talent, Training, Temperament) per role, blended 0.4/0.3/0.3.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — /running-now is excellent.
- [x] Why any of this matters — /why-web4's problem section is convincing.

## Honest Assessment
This is the most *honest* technical project site I've read. It constantly tells me what is not built, what is the lab's own use, what an attacker can route around (two environment variables!), and where its own model has a "genuine hole." /running-now and the "settled vs still moving" box on /atp-economics are model examples. I came away trusting the authors.

The *pitch* lands in the first 15 minutes: the problem framing on /why-web4 is strong and the four-pieces picture is clear.

Where it breaks down is the *rules*. Every time I tried to follow one concrete story through the numbers, two pages disagreed: karma for the same death is +12 on one page and −15 on another; the rebirth formula on the karma page would make First Contact's rebirth impossible; spam costs 5 on one line and 10 on another, and is cheaper than a normal post on every list; strangers "never see" the thread vs "everyone can see." The site often *knows* about the disagreement and apologizes for it in a caveat box, which is honest but leaves me, the beginner, doing the reconciling. I'd rather see one consistent toy model in the reading path, and the "here are three candidate rules" discussion in one clearly-labelled place.

The scariest thing I learned wasn't a contradiction: it's that decay during a long break might push me below the survival line, and whether that's fatal is an open question. That's the kind of thing a normal person would want answered before trusting their identity to it.
