## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: I knew what this was within about eight seconds. "Trust, built into the internet." followed immediately by "AI agents already take real actions: they write code, move money, and make decisions. Today we cannot prove what an agent did, on whose authority, or by what rules." That is a problem statement I recognized from my own life without needing anything explained to me.
- The thing I appreciated most: it told me what to click. "New to Web4? Read in this order: the 2-minute intro, Why Web4, then First Contact." I have never had a site do that and mean it. I did exactly what it said.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes."
- Confusion points: mild. The footer says "4-Life is an educational onramp to Web4" and about two screens above it there is a section heading called "The onramp" that means four pieces of software. Same page, same word, two meanings. I did not notice this until later, but it registered as a small snag.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (03:20)
- Best page on the site. The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 three-way comparison did more work than anything else I read all session:
  - Web2: "A platform *declares* your reputation. Delete the account and it is gone."
  - Web3: "Whoever holds the key *is assumed to be* the rightful owner."
  - Web4: "Trust accrues from *witnessed behavior*."
  That is the whole idea, and I got it in one pass, cold.
- It also caught the onramp word problem before I could complain about it: "when a page here says 'the onramp,' it means the four pieces of Web4 you actually run ... which 4-Life teaches but is not one of." Being told the word is overloaded, up front, defused it. I stopped tripping on it after this.
- The honesty is unusual and it bought a lot of goodwill. "Running: deployed and operational today ... The daily use counted so far is the building lab's own, so this badge does not claim adoption." A site that volunteers "our only user is us" is a site I believe on other things.
- Confusion points: none worth logging. I finished this page thinking I understood Web4.

### Why Web4? (08:45)
- The four-problem framing landed: no cost to act, siloed reputation, disposable identities, AI pretending to be human. All four are things I have personally been annoyed by.
- "Isn't this just a social credit system?" was in the FAQ. Good. That was my next thought and the site got there first. The bottom-up vs top-down answer plus exit rights satisfied me.
- First numbers I could not evaluate: "software-only tops out at 0.50, against 0.75 (FIDO2), 0.85 (Secure Enclave) and 0.90 (TPM)." I did not yet know what a 0.50 was worth. I filed it.
- The line "the people with the least hardware are the people whose reputation cannot grow" is a genuinely uncomfortable admission to publish about your own design and I noted that they published it.
- Confusion: "3-hop trust network" appeared with no explanation of what a hop is. Minor, I guessed.

### First Contact (15:10)
- Alice's five-act arc is a nice device. Join / Build / Stumble / Recover / Die & Reborn.
- I got jolted by Act 5: "Die & Reborn." Dying is a strong word for a website to use about me. The page does clarify: "'death' here means Alice's *standing* is suspended, she can't act until rebirth, not that her account is deleted." That helped, but I had to click a spoiler to get it, after the word "Die" had already done its work on me.
- Act 3 alarmed me more than the page seemed to think it would. Alice starts at 0.50 trust, and one stumble takes her to 0.48. Later I learned 0.5 is the line below which you can eventually die permanently. So the very first mistake in the very first demo puts the protagonist under the fatal line. The page says "a sustained slide, not one dip," which is reassuring, but I had already had the scare.
- "+12 bonus" for karma carried forward. I remembered the 12.

### How It Works (23:30)
- Long, dense, and mostly excellent. The plain-English pass over all five systems before the deep dive was the right call.
- "Two ways to die, and they are very different" answered the question I had been carrying since First Contact: ATP hits zero (energy death, recoverable) or raw trust stays below 0.5 (trust death, permanent). Clean. I wish I had gotten this on First Contact instead of two pages later.
- The arithmetic all checks out where I tested it. 0.9^5 = 0.59. 0.4(0.40)+0.3(0.60)+0.3(0.60) = 0.52. 100 - 60 + 105 = 145. Somebody checks these.
- Then the "Complete Example" section confused me badly, and it is the best-documented confusion of my session, so I want to be precise about it.
  - Life 1 "Ended with 145 ATP." Life 2 "Reborn with 145 ATP."
  - But death is defined on this same page as ATP reaching zero. So why did Life 1 end?
  - The page actually anticipates me: "About these numbers: none of these lives ends at 0 ATP, so none of them is the energy death described above. That is why they read *ended* and not *died*."
  - Which is honest and I respect it, but it does not answer the question. It tells me what did not happen. I still do not know what a "life" is or what ends one. The site is *called* 4-Life. Its central metaphor is Birth, Life, Death, Rebirth. Its showcase example runs three lives that end for reasons never given.
- Also on karma: this page shows the entire 145 carrying forward. First Contact showed "+12." Those are very different stories and I had read them 8 minutes apart. This page does disclose that the rule is unsettled, which is fair, but I met the "+12" first with no such caveat attached.

### Running Now (33:00)

### Karma & Consequences (37:20)
- This is where I hit the thing that bothers me most, and it took me two more pages to confirm it.
- The page argues that Web4 solves the disposable-identity problem, and its whole argument rests on one sentence: "In traditional platforms, this agent would have just created a new account after Life 1. In Web4, **identity is hardware-bound**, so the karma follows." And: "Bad actors can't escape their history."
- I had read on /why-web4, forty minutes earlier, that software-only identities are supported. So my immediate reaction was: what stops a bad actor from being software-only and just making a new one? That is the exact problem this page claims is solved.

### LCT Explainer (41:00)
- Confirmed the premise: software-only identity is allowed and capped at 0.50. The page even calls 0.50 "the neutral midpoint and survival line," so it knows the number is doing double duty.
- Nothing on this page says whether you can spin up unlimited software-only identities. I went looking elsewhere.
- The `#software-only-survival` section, which I only found because /trust-tensor had a parenthetical link to it, is genuinely good: "at every tier you post, earn ATP, build karma, and join societies; what changes is the *terms*," and "Zero margin is the tradeoff you accept by anchoring an identity in software alone." That is the honest framing and it exists. It is just three clicks off the main path and reachable mainly by accident.

### What Could Go Wrong (46:15)
- Found my answer, and it is the opposite of what /karma-consequences told me: "the very gap that makes an honest user's loss unrecoverable also makes a dishonest user's record sheddable."
- So the site knows. It says so plainly, on a page filed under "Going Deeper," while the page that actually makes the no-fresh-start argument asserts the reverse as settled fact.
- Everything else here is strong. Nine named failure modes including "Nobody adopts it" and "The agent routes around its own safety gate." Publishing your own worst case is rare.

### Trust Tensor, ATP Economics, Onramp, Glossary (48:00 to 52:00)

## Unanswered Questions
1. Can a bad actor create unlimited software-only identities and shed their record each time? /what-could-go-wrong says the record is sheddable; /karma-consequences says it is not. Which is it?
2. What ends a life? Not energy death, per the site's own note on its worked example. Then what?
3. How long is "stays below 0.5"? Days, weeks, months? Who sets it?
4. How much karma actually carries forward: a small bonus, the whole balance, or a formula-scaled portion?
5. How does a brand-new member get their first commission? The site says this is open, which I respect, but the worked example depends on it having an answer.
6. What are R6 and R7, and why is a primitive I am told to learn absent from the curriculum?
7. If software-only is capped at exactly the survival line, is a software-only participant permanently one sustained bad stretch from permanent death, with no way to ever build margin? The #software-only-survival section says "zero margin is the tradeoff." Is that considered acceptable, or is it a known problem?

## Honest Assessment

This is the clearest technical explainer I have read in a long time, and the reason is not the writing, it is the posture. The site keeps telling me things that hurt its own case. "The daily use counted so far is the building lab's own, so this badge does not claim adoption." "To be precise, this is not production-ready." "How someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question on this stack rather than a solved one, and you should read it as one." "The people with the least hardware are the people whose reputation cannot grow." A page called "What Could Go Wrong" that opens with "Nobody adopts it."

That posture is the product, as far as I am concerned. I believed the load-bearing claims because I watched the site decline several easy ones.

Which is exactly why the /karma-consequences problem is worth flagging loudly rather than politely. On a site that hedges everything, an unhedged absolute reads as a deliberate signal that this one is settled. "Bad actors can't escape their history" is stated flat, with no caveat, as the premise of the whole no-fresh-start argument. And three clicks away, the site says the opposite about the same mechanism. I did not catch this by being clever. I caught it because /why-web4 had already told me software-only identity exists, and the contradiction was sitting in my head from minute 12 waiting for a page to resolve it. Any reader who reads more than one page will hit this.

The second pattern I noticed: the site is more honest in its prose than in its examples. Every place I found a gap, the prose already conceded it and the illustration did not. /atp-economics says commissioned work is the only channel that grows a balance and the bootstrap is unsolved; the Complete Example shows a novice netting +45 anyway. /how-it-works defines death as ATP reaching zero; the Complete Example runs three lives that end at 145, 130, and 165. The disclosure note attached to that example is careful and correct and still leaves the reader without the thing they came for. The worked examples were probably written before the caveats and never re-run against them.

The one place the site is quieter than it should be is the trust-death duration. Everything else with permanent consequences gets a number. This does not, anywhere I looked, and it is the rule that decides whether living in this system feels safe or feels like standing on ice. "A sustained collapse, not a single stumble" is a reassurance, not a specification. On a site this comfortable saying "we do not know yet," saying nothing at all stands out.

I would come back. I would send this to someone. I would tell them to read /what-could-go-wrong before /karma-consequences, which is a strange thing to have to say about a site whose nav puts them in the opposite order.
