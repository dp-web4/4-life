## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a way to make actions by people and AI agents carry a witnessed, checkable record, with trust earned per role instead of declared by a platform
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one vocabulary, run at personal, community and enterprise scale
- [x] What LCT means: an identity tied to my own device, ideally its security chip, witnessed by my other devices
- [x] What ATP/ADP means: an energy budget per participant; spending leaves a receipt; confirmations refund, commissions pay
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three scores (talent, training, temperament) per role, blended 0.4/0.3/0.3
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: hestia runs (software identity, gate bypassable), hub is runnable but not deployed, hardbound is real code with unvalidated hardware binding, federation is paper
- [x] Why any of this matters: the problems page did that on its own

## Unanswered Questions
1. When I send a message to someone outside my trust neighborhood, does it cost nothing, the normal price, or a lot? Three pages give three answers.
2. If I die of energy exhaustion after doing good work, do I come back stronger (First Contact) or weaker (Karma page)? Both say the number is not settled; neither admits the direction is not settled.
3. Does the lab run a hub? If so why is the hub not Running? If not, what is the "live Web4 society" on the hub page?
4. If I run hestia today, my identity is capped at 0.50, which is the survival line. What does the lab's own trust look like, given they run on the same software?
5. Can a new user with no history be killed by a five-week holiday? The trust page says this is filed with the standard's maintainers. Has anyone answered?
6. How does someone with no track record ever get commissioned, given that only commissioned work nets positive energy? Three pages say this is open. It seems central to whether a newcomer can survive at all.
7. Is 0.5 CI an access threshold or a death line? The coherence page says both.

## Honest Assessment
This is the most honest technical site I have read. Every page says what is real, and the "Running Now" ledger would survive an audit. The problem framing on Why Web4 is genuinely persuasive, and the five-word strip on How It Works (identity, energy budget, trust score, consistency, context boundary) gave me a mental model in ten seconds that the rest of the site then filled in.

The cost of that honesty is a site that keeps interrupting itself. Nearly every number comes with a paragraph explaining that it is one modelling choice, that another page models it differently, and that I should read for the shape. I did that. On three occasions the shapes themselves disagreed: whether strangers see my message, whether out-of-neighborhood actions cost energy, and whether dying with a good record starts me stronger or weaker. Those are not calibration details. They are the mechanism, and I left not knowing which way it points.

The second cost is a gap between the story and what I can run. Alice's climb to 0.62 assumes a security chip. The thing I can install today gives me a software identity pinned at 0.50, with a policy gate the site admits an agent can step over, and a desktop app I cannot download. The site says all of this, clearly, in the right places. It also opens the personal-tier walkthrough by having me open that app.

I would come back to read the FAQ. I would recommend it to a friend who already runs AI agents on their own machine. I would not send it to my parents, and I do not think the site would want me to.
