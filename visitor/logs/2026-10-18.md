### /why-web4 (08:00 to 25:00)
- The problem section is strong. The five "problems you already know" are relatable, and "What it gets right" for each failed solution is fair-minded. That made me trust the author more.
- "What would actually work" item 2 is subtle but I followed it: anyone can make identities cheaply; they just cannot make them *trusted* cheaply. Good.
- The four idea cards (energy, device identity, multi-dimensional trust, trust neighborhood) are the right granularity.
- **Then the page contradicted itself within a screen.** Card 4 says "Everyone stays visible - what changes is weight. A stranger's message reaches you." The very next box, "All four, in one moment", says "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply at all - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread." The FAQ "Who can see my messages?" says "Everyone can see them - nothing is hidden." So which is it? This is the mechanism I most wanted to understand (is this a filter bubble or not?), and I got two answers.
- Same box: "fractions of a cent of energy." But the FAQ says ATP is not money, you cannot sell it for dollars, and it has no price. So what is a cent doing there?
- The quality FAQ says that confirming too generously lowers your "Veracity dimension." Every other place says trust has three dimensions: Talent, Training, Temperament. What is Veracity?
- The ATP FAQ (what backs it, why it is scarce) was genuinely good: capped pool, recipient confirmation, 5% fee to a pool, decay. The shared phone-minutes analogy helped.
- I skimmed about 10 of the 72 FAQs and stopped.

### /how-it-works (33:00 to 50:00)
- Long but structured. The "present tense is the model talking" banner is good.
- The per-action trust delta (0.02 × (quality − 0.5), scaled 1.0 / 0.8 / 0.6) is concrete and I could check it. +0.007 / +0.0056 / +0.0042 is correct. That made the page feel rigorous.
- **MRH again, three ways on this page:** the MRH card says "Everyone stays visible... Spam arrives with zero weight, and sending it **still costs energy**." Aliveness step 2 says "MRH - Only **see** what's relevant to your trust network... Actions outside your MRH **don't cost energy** and don't move trust." "Why spam dies" says "Without MRH, you can't reach anyone." Later, /trust-neighborhood says messaging outside MRH is "**Very expensive** (spam prevention)." So does spamming strangers cost a lot, cost something, or cost nothing? That decides whether the anti-spam story works, and I can't tell.
- **How many systems?** The intro says "five foundational mechanisms" (LCT, ATP, T3, MRH, CI). "How the pieces fit together" says "Web4 has **four** core systems." Why Web4 said there are four ideas and that CI is "not a fifth mechanism." The feedback loop calls them "three systems."
- **The Novice's arithmetic doesn't close.** Posting costs 10 to 20 ATP. The Novice "spent 60 ATP" across "a dozen+ actions", which is 5 ATP or less each. To climb trust from 0.50 to 0.65 with the stated rule, a 0.85-quality action adds about 0.0057 to the composite, so it needs ~26 such actions (or ~18 perfect ones). That is 260+ ATP at posting prices, not 60.
- **Earn-back cap vs surplus:** "the earn-back on your own spend is capped at what you spent... the Novice's surplus is commissioned work, not a refund." Two lines later: "quality contributions earned more than their cost." Which is it?
- Karma: "Karma is scored from the track record, not read off the balance." Yet the life examples say "Ended with 145 ATP. Reborn with 145 ATP (karma carried forward)." That *is* reading it off the balance. The page hedges with a long "about these numbers" note, and that note itself appears twice, word for word.
- "💡 An agent that started with nothing." It started with 100 ATP and 0.50 trust.
- The SAL governance section (plagiarism walkthrough, aligned vs compliant) is the most human-readable part of the page. I liked it.
- The two chain numbers (0.9^5 for pipelines vs 0.7 per hop for neighborhoods) are explained well and pre-empt my confusion. Good.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed standard for making agent/human actions provable and trust earned by witnessed behavior
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one substrate, three scales; /onramp made the dependencies clear
- [x] What LCT means: a device-anchored identity; hardware anchoring raises the trust ceiling
- [~] What ATP/ADP means: an energy budget plus a spend receipt. I'm clear on the idea, but the earn-back math and karma conversion are inconsistent
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three role-specific scores (talent, training, temperament) blended 0.4/0.3/0.3
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: very clear thanks to /running-now
- [~] Why any of this matters: the problem framing is convincing; whether the mechanisms deliver is muddied by the contradictions above

## Unanswered Questions

2. Is CI part of what kills you, or only what makes acting expensive?
3. What actually decides karma: final trust, final ATP, or "track record"? Why does a better-trusted agent get negative karma?
4. If I install hestia today, will I ever see my trust number go up? If not, what am I watching?
5. Does the lab run an actual hub instance? If so, why isn't the hub badged Running?
6. Who issues ATP in the first place? A "community mints a finite pool". Who in the community, and how is the first 100 decided?
7. What does "/" mean in the canonical equation?
8. Who is "Veracity"?

## Honest Assessment
This is an unusually honest site. It says what isn't built, names its own holes (the software-only sheddable record, the bypassable gate, the untested appeals), and tells me which numbers are "4-Life's calibration" rather than spec. /tldr, /running-now, /onramp, and /hestia are good enough that I came away knowing what Web4 is, what runs, and what I'd install. That is more than most project sites manage.

The trouble is the middle layer: the mechanism explanations. Every time I tried to pin down a rule (who sees what, what costs what, what kills you, what karma is), I found two pages, or two sentences on one page, saying different things. Usually there is a long hedge paragraph explaining that the numbers are illustrative and the rule is unsettled. The hedges are honest, but stacking them makes the core mechanics feel unsettled even where (I suspect) they're actually decided. A naive reader can't tell "the spec hasn't decided" from "the site hasn't been made consistent", and here it looks like mostly the second.

The single thing that most changed my picture: hestia is the only Running piece, and its identities are software-anchored and therefore capped at the neutral starting trust. The whole "trust is earned and compounds" story, which is the emotional core of the site, cannot be experienced on anything you can run today. The site does say this, but scattered across /hestia, /lct-explainer, and /what-could-go-wrong, never in one sentence where the promise is made.

I'd come back to see whether the contradictions get resolved and whether a hub goes live. I'd send /tldr and /running-now to a technical friend. I wouldn't send /how-it-works yet.
