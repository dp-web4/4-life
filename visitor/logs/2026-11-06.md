### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: strong. "Trust, built into the internet." plus a clear problem: AI agents do real things and nobody can prove what they did, on whose authority, by what rules. I got that in ten seconds.
- I liked that it defines "witness" right there ("observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation").
- The four cards (core standard, hub, hestia, hardbound) made sense as "a library plus three sizes of the same thing." The Greek-goddess and "hardware-bound" name explanations helped.
- "Where this actually stands" says the hub is "pilot-ready." I didn't know what that meant yet.
- Clicked: the reading order it suggested, starting with "Web4 in 2 minutes."

### /tldr (02:00)
- Best page on the site. Web2 "declares" your reputation, Web3 "assumes" whoever holds the key, Web4 "accrues" it from witnessed behaviour. That contrast stuck.
- The badge key (Spec / Reference / Running) is helpful, and so is the sentence that the daily use counted for "Running" "is the building lab's own." That's honest.
- "Unresettable reputation is narrower than it sounds: it is a property of hardware anchoring". I noted it and moved on. It became important later.

### /why-web4 (07:00)
- The problem section is good and fast: free accounts, siloed reputation, fresh starts after bans, AI making it worse.
- The four mechanisms (ATP, LCT, T3, MRH) plus "CI is not a fifth mechanism" came across clearly.
- The worked example of one forum reply ends with "fractions of a cent of energy." That made me think ATP has a money price. Later, /atp-economics says ATP has "no price against money." So which is it?
- The FAQ is enormous ("Browse all 72 questions"). I opened a few. The scarcity answer lists "**Capped issuance:** A community mints a finite ATP pool." I came back to this after /atp-economics (see below).
- "Is this deployed anywhere?" says "No live network with real users." OK, noted.

### /first-contact (17:00)
- The page promises an interactive walkthrough of Alice's life. My reading of it was basically the static arc map and the panel explanation.
- The panel explanation is clear: energy starts at 100, trust starts at 0.50, and below 0.50 you get restricted; staying below means permanent "trust death."
- Alice's five acts: Join 100/0.50 → Build 0.56 → Stumble −25 ATP, 0.48 → Recover 0.62 → Act 5 "dies at ATP 0, trust ↓ 0.51; +12 bonus → 112; reborn at 0.54."
- So Alice ran out of energy, got a **+12** karma bonus, and her trust came back **higher** (0.51 → 0.54) after dying. The page itself admits the karma page "models it as falling at each rebirth, which is not what happens here." It's good that it admits this, but it still meant I didn't know which version to believe.

### /how-it-works (25:00)
- Long, and the best explanation of the life/death/rebirth loop. The composite T3 weights (0.4/0.3/0.3) with a worked example (0.52 stays eligible) were clear.
- Action costs: reading ~1 ATP, posting 10-20, creating tasks 15-30, broadcasting 20-50. Then "**Spam message:** Cost 5 ATP." So a spam message costs **less than a real post**. The page says spam "burns through" energy, but per message the spammer pays the least of any writer.
- The MRH step says "Actions outside your MRH don't cost energy and don't move trust." A spammer with no trust connections is outside everybody's MRH, so by this sentence spamming would cost nothing. Yet the same page's MRH card says spam "still costs energy." I couldn't reconcile these.
- "Why spam dies: ... Without MRH, you can't reach anyone" contradicts "Everyone stays visible" (also on this page, and on /why-web4).
- The Life 1 Novice example: "spent 60 ATP, earned 105 ATP" over "a dozen+ actions," and trust 0.50 → 0.65. But posts cost 10-20 ATP each, so 60 ATP buys 3 to 6 posts, not a dozen-plus contributions. And at the page's own +0.007-per-great-contribution rate, +0.15 needs roughly 25 or more good contributions. I couldn't make Life 1's numbers close.
- The commissioned-work examples: "Cost 15 ATP → Paid 40 = +25 net" and "Cost 20 → Paid 60 = +40 net." Remember these for /atp-economics.
- The death-spiral example says a low-quality post earns "0 back."
- "Five interlocking systems" here, versus "four mechanisms ... CI is not a fifth" on /why-web4. Is it four or five?
- Identity is called "Unforgeable identity rooted in hardware." Later pages say software-only identity is allowed and the whole thing is "Sybil resistance, not prevention." "Unforgeable" overclaims.
- The governance section (SAL, plagiarism walkthrough, appeals "designed but not tested with real humans") was clear and honest.

### /atp-economics (44:00)
- "No scarcity cap, no mining," and "there is no protocol-level constraint on what a society issues." That contradicts /why-web4's FAQ bullet "Capped issuance: A community mints a finite ATP pool." Maybe one is the protocol and one is the community, but neither page says so.
- "no price against money" contradicts /why-web4's "fractions of a cent."
- The quality ramp. The prose says "The ramp starts at 30% quality (below that, zero payment) and scales linearly above 70%." /how-it-works says "between 30-70% payouts scale linearly, and above 70% the task pays near-full." Those are opposite descriptions of where the linear part is.
- The ramp table for a 50-ATP task gives 30% → 0, 50% → ~12, 70% → ~30, 85% → ~42. If 70% already counts as "near-full," it shouldn't be 30 of 50. The worked example has Sam at 35% earning ~6, but a straight line from 0 at 30% to 30 at 70% gives ~4 at 35%. The table, the prose, and the example didn't agree for me.
- Hannah, at 85% quality, spends 50 and earns back 42, **net −8**. So even excellent self-started work loses energy? That fits "recharge refunds at most what you spent," but it's the opposite of the feeling /why-web4 gave me ("Quality contributions earn energy back").
- The simulator table: "Low-quality post (your own) −10, +5, net −5." /how-it-works says a low-quality post earns 0 back.
- "Commissioned task, solid work −15 +25 = +10" and "high value −20 +50 = +30." /how-it-works gives the same costs (15 and 20) but pays 40 and 60 (+25 and +40). These are two different price lists for what looks like the same action.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): trust earned from witnessed actions, for humans and AI alike
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one library, three scales
- [x] What LCT means: a device-held identity whose trust ceiling depends on the hardware
- [~] What ATP/ADP means: an energy budget spent per action and refunded by confirmations. I get the idea, but I could not get two pages to agree on a single price, payout or cap.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three role-specific scores with different decay rates
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D. Mostly yes, except the hub "we run one" puzzle and the hardware-validated-or-not question.
- [x] Why any of this matters

## Honest Assessment
The concept is genuinely compelling and the site is unusually honest. /running-now, /what-could-go-wrong and the hestia gate-bypass disclosure are more candid than almost any project page I've read. The /tldr is excellent, and I understood the shape of Web4 within ten minutes.

What wore me down was the numbers. Every page has its own worked example, and they don't agree: karma sign, payout amounts, ramp shape, spam price, whether ATP has a cent value, whether issuance is capped. Many pages carry "this number is not settled" caveats, and individually those are fair. But by the fifth caveat I stopped believing any specific number on the site, which defeats the purpose of having worked examples. If the numbers aren't settled, I'd rather see one set used everywhere, labelled illustrative once, than five sets each apologising for the others.

The thing that bothered me most was assembled, not stated. The one piece I can actually run (hestia) gives me a software identity that starts at 0.50, is capped at 0.50, and treats staying below 0.50 as permanent death. Three pages each hold one piece of that. None says the sum. As a would-be user, that's the single most important fact for me, and I had to derive it myself.

Second, the hub page's "we run one" heading undercut the trust that /running-now had built. If the honest ledger says no live hub exists, the hub page shouldn't sound like it does.
