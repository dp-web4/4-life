## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) - "trust is earned through witnessed behavior, not granted from above," and witnessing means signing a record and staking your own reputation on it. Taught, not inferred.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together - "one substrate, three scales" plus the dependency direction. `/onramp` taught this well; the ordering inconsistency cost me a minute of doubt.
- [x] What LCT means - device-anchored identity, ceiling set by chip class and device count. Solid, apart from the 0.85-vs-0.50 clash.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means - energy budget plus receipt; spend to act, recharge when recipients confirm value, and net gain comes from commissioned work rather than refunds. The refund-vs-payment distinction was the piece I would have got wrong on my own.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are - Talent/Training/Temperament, per role, blended 0.4/0.3/0.3. The surgeon-vs-mechanic example did it in one sentence.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D - `/running-now` is unambiguous. Core standard installable, hub runnable-not-deployed, hestia in daily use by its own lab, hardbound hardening, federation spec-only.
- [x] Why any of this matters - spam economics and portable reputation. Though the *personal* why landed on `/day-in-web4`, not on `/why-web4`.

## Unanswered Questions
1. **Does running out of energy kill me permanently or not?** Four places say energy death is recoverable; the eligibility rule and the spam example say it depends entirely on my trust score. I left not knowing, and this is the single most consequential rule on the site for a prospective user.
2. **What is my actual trust ceiling with one phone?** The setup mockup says 0.85, the explainer's own rule computes 0.50. Those imply very different experiences: 0.50 is the survival line.
3. **How do I get a second device onto my identity?** Every recovery, ceiling-raising, and phone-upgrade story depends on having 2-3 devices, and nothing showed me the enrolment step. Do I re-scan my face on the laptop? Does the phone approve it? Is there a window where I am vulnerable?
4. **If I never get commissioned work, can I break even?** The site is clear that self-initiated work only *refunds* up to what you spent, and that net gain requires someone commissioning you. It also admits "how someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question." So does an ordinary participant who just posts helpful things slowly drain to zero?
5. **Who is "the lab"?** `/running-now` says hestia runs on 4 machines, "all the lab's own", and points at 4-lab.io/fleet. I did not follow it, but the whole Running badge rests on a group I met only as "the lab" - which sits oddly next to a site otherwise obsessive about not asking me to take things on faith.
6. **Why is 12 the karma bonus, or 145 the carry-forward, or 3 the hop count?** The site tells me repeatedly that these are "one modelling choice, not the rule." So what decides them in a real deployment - the society's own law? Is that configurable per community?

## Honest Assessment

This site is unusually good at the hardest part of explaining a research project: telling me what is not true yet. `/running-now` is a model of it. I have never seen a project page volunteer "the daily use counted so far is the building lab's own, so this badge does not claim adoption." That sentence bought more credibility than any feature list could have.

And the teaching sequence works. I arrived knowing nothing and left able to explain, unprompted, what an LCT is, why ATP makes spam self-limiting, and why one trust number is worse than three. The vocabulary chips on the landing page, the five-line acronym card on `/how-it-works`, and the "all four, in one moment" forum-reply box on `/why-web4` are the three things that did most of that work. Whoever wrote those understood that acronyms become real when you see them all fire at once on one ordinary action.

Two things worked against it.

The first is **caveat density**. The site's honesty has metastasised into a reflex. On `/how-it-works` I counted four separate boxes disclaiming the numbers immediately above them, sometimes disclaiming the *same* thing twice in one section. On `/first-contact` there are eight hedges before the Start button of a 7-minute story. The effect is not "this project is honest," which is what one or two would achieve. The effect is "nothing here is load-bearing, so relax your attention" - and that is the opposite of what the honesty was for. There is a real tension here worth naming: every one of those blocks was presumably added because someone got confused. But the fix for a confusing claim is usually to *change the claim*, not to append a paragraph explaining that the claim is provisional. `/atp-economics` shows the alternative - it just says "recoverable if you built trust, permanent if not" and moves on. Nine words, no box, correct.

The second is that **the numbers do not agree with each other, and the disagreements cluster on exactly the pages that matter most.** The death rule is contradicted on the page that defines it. The trust ceiling shown in the friendliest onboarding mockup is 0.35 higher than the explainer it links to computes. A newcomer's post costs 3 ATP in one panel and 10 ATP two screens down. The flagship karma illustration reincarnates an agent the rules say is permanently dead. Individually these are small. Together they matter more than usual *here*, because this site's entire pitch is that Web4 replaces "trust the platform's assertion" with "check the record." A site making that argument is held to its own standard, and I checked four numbers against their own stated rules and three of them failed.

What I would fix first, if it were one thing: the death rule. Not because the contradiction is embarrassing, but because it is the question a real prospective user asks - *can this thing kill me, and can I come back?* - and right now the site answers it four different ways depending on which page you land on. The correct answer already exists, in nine words, on `/atp-economics`. It just needs to be the only answer anywhere.

Would I come back? Yes. Would I recommend it? To someone technical and curious, yes, with the caveat that they should read `/running-now` first and treat the specific decimals as illustrative. To a non-technical friend, not yet - `/day-in-web4` is the page that would hook them, and it is the one whose numbers I trust least.
