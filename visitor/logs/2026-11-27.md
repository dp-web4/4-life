## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: good. The tagline "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules" told me in one line what this is, and "Trust, built into the internet" is a headline I could repeat to someone. The definition of "witness" (observe an action and attest to it, with your own reputation at stake) arrived exactly when I needed it.
- The four cards (Core Standard, Hub, Hestia, Hardbound) with "Substrate / Community / Personal / Enterprise" badges made sense as a shape even though I did not know what any of them did yet.
- "Where this actually stands" is disarming. A site that says "this is R&D, not a finished product" on its front page earns some trust.
- Confusion points: the "vocabulary" row (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, Karma) is six acronyms at once. I skipped it. The footer says "4-Life is an educational onramp to Web4" and the section above it is headed "The onramp" and means something else (four pieces of software). I did not notice the clash until /tldr pointed it out.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (/tldr) (03:00)
- Best page on the site. Problem, shift, Web2/Web3/Web4 contrast, four pieces, honest status. I understood all of it.
- The badge key (Spec, Reference, Running) is clear, and the note that "Running" only counts the builders' own daily use is the kind of honesty I rarely see.
- The wording note helped: "the onramp" = the four pieces you run; 4-Life teaches them but is not one of them. But then the footer on this very page still reads "4-Life is an educational onramp to Web4." Two meanings of one word, one of them in the site's permanent footer.
- Small snag: "Your own device is your day-zero witness." A minute earlier, witnessing meant *someone else* observing me and staking *their* reputation. My own phone vouching for me felt like the opposite of that. The page did not flag the shift.
- Clicked: "Why Web4?" (the recommended next page).

### Why Web4? (/why-web4) (08:00)
- The five problems and four failed solutions are well written and I agreed with every one.
- But the pitch changed under me. The landing page and /tldr sold me "prove what an AI agent did, on whose authority." This page sells "fix spam, trolls, trapped reputation, and platform control on social media." Those are two different products in my head. I never found the sentence that joins them.
- The four mechanism cards are the right size: plain name first, acronym second, "just means your energy budget." Good.
- Then the worked example broke something. The Trust Neighborhood card says: "Everyone stays visible - what changes is weight." Six lines later the forum example says: "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply at all - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread." And the FAQ below says: "Everyone can see them - nothing is hidden or censored." So does a far-away stranger see my reply or not? Two answers say yes, the one concrete example says no.
- Same example ends "fractions of a cent of energy." The energy page later insists ATP has no price against money and cannot be bought or cashed out. So what is a cent of it?
- The FAQ. "Browse all 72 questions." I am a first-time visitor on page two of the reading path and I have been handed a 72-question document. I read the 12 "Most Asked" carefully and skimmed about 35 more before giving up. Many answers are excellent (the social-credit table, the "what if I lose my hardware" answer, the "who builds this / why anonymous" answer which openly calls its own anonymity ironic). But this is a reference manual, not page two of an introduction.
- Things from the FAQ that stuck as contradictions with later pages:
  - "Capped issuance: A community mints a finite ATP pool." The energy page says "No scarcity cap and no mining."
  - Trust weights of 0.70 / 0.49 / 0.34 for 1 / 2 / 3 hops, then zero. Fine. But a social-credit table row says only entities in my network *see my score*, while messages are visible to everyone. I could not tell what is hidden and what is merely unweighted.
  - Software-only identity is capped at 0.50, and the FAQ says software-only "is what most early Web4 adoption will actually look like." Yet every illustrative number on the page is far above that ("0.82 trust in Python", "0.87 Talent in Python", "0.85 data analysis trust"). So the examples describe a world most early users cannot be in.
  - The five tiers are listed 1 to 5 and then (unverified wording, from the summarized tail) described as "Tiers 0-3 are reversible... Only Tier 4" is permanent. Two numbering schemes in one answer.
  - Bootstrapping walk-through says (unverified wording) each quality contribution moves trust by about 0.02. Later pages give 0.02 × (quality − 0.5), which is at most 0.01 and typically 0.007.
- Clicked: "First Contact".

### First Contact (/first-contact) (30:00)
- The promise is a 7-minute interactive story of "Alice". I could not drive it (tool limit), so I read the setup and the act map.
- "Only two numbers move" (Energy and Trust) is a great simplification. I felt relieved.
- Then the relief leaked: the Trust Score is "raw" trust, which is not "effective" trust, which is raw × CI², and CI is something I have not met. An "(optional)" box, but it sits right under the two numbers.
- "Energy death" and "trust death" and being "reborn" arrive with no warning that this site is going to talk about my account *dying*. I spent the rest of the session unsure whether a human user actually experiences "death" and "rebirth" or whether this is leftover language from a game or simulation. Nobody told me what a "life" is for a person.
- The act map: Act 5 "dies at ATP 0, trust ↓ 0.51; +12 bonus → 112; reborn at 0.54". So trust was 0.51 at death and 0.54 after rebirth. Dying improved it? The callout says the 12 is "one modelling choice, not the rule" and that the karma page models trust as *falling* at rebirth, "which is not what happens here." I appreciate being told, but being told "the other page says the opposite and neither is settled" on my third page is not teaching, it is a disclaimer.
- Clicked: "How It Works".

### How It Works (/how-it-works) (40:00)
- Long. The callout "No network you can join yet - the present tense below is the model talking" is the right warning and I kept it in mind.
- Counting problem: Key Takeaways says "Five interlocking systems." The section "How The Pieces Fit Together" opens "Web4 has four core systems." /why-web4 said four mechanisms and that CI is "not a fifth mechanism." I do not know if there are four or five.
- Visibility again. The MRH step says: "Defines your reach. Only see what's relevant to your trust network." The MRH card on the same page says everyone stays visible. And: "Actions outside your MRH don't cost energy and don't move trust" versus the card's "Spam arrives with zero weight, and sending it still costs energy." Spam to strangers is by definition outside the neighborhood. Does it cost energy or not?
- The price list finally made the economics concrete, and it surprised me: a spam message costs 5 ATP, a real post costs 10 to 20. Spam is the cheapest thing you can send. And "A post you chose to write... 0 net at best." So an honest person who just posts good things never gains; they need someone to *commission* work, and how a newcomer gets a first commission "is an open question on this stack." That is an important admission, and it means the headline claim ("quality earns energy back") is, for a new honest user, "quality loses energy slower."
- The three-lives example: "Ended with 145 ATP. Reborn with 145 ATP." Then a caveat that none of these lives ended by death, that "what else ends a life is not settled," and that the standard names no lifespan. So the site's central story (birth → life → death → rebirth) is illustrated with lives that end for no stated reason. I could not picture it.
- Life 1 numbers do not close for me. Trust climbs 0.50 → 0.65. The update rule gives about +0.007 talent, +0.0056 training, +0.0042 temperament per excellent (0.85) action, which with the 0.4/0.3/0.3 weights is under +0.006 on the overall score. A +0.15 climb is then roughly 26 excellent actions in a row. The page says the life spent 60 ATP across "a dozen+ actions." 26 actions on 60 ATP is about 2.3 ATP each, which is the price of *viewing* something, not of the 10 to 20 ATP posts that build trust.
- "Remove any one, and the others compensate." Earlier on the same page: without LCT you cannot enter, without ATP you cannot act, and three properties "emerge only in composition." Those cannot both be true.
- The governance section (Society / Authority / Law, the plagiarism case) was readable and the "Honest status: no community has run one yet" lines were welcome. But "no moderators needed" in the takeaways followed by a society with a rotating Moderator role needed the clarification it got, and I had to read it twice.
- Clicked: "Running Now".

Later: `lct:web4:agent:alice.assistant1@Thor#perception`, where `alice.assistant1` is lineage ("created by Alice") and the part after `#` is task scope. I cannot tell which is the real one.

### ATP/ADP (Energy) (/atp-economics) (98:00)
- "The 60-second version" is good. "Recharge refunds, payment earns" is a phrase I will remember. "ATP is cash, ADP is the receipt" finally explained ADP.
- "What's settled vs what's still moving" is a great idea and I wish every concept page had one.
- The quality-ramp table does not match its own description. Description: nothing below 30%, full above 70%, linear in between. Table for a 50-ATP task: 30% → 0, 50% → ~12, 70% → ~30, 85% → ~42. A straight line from 30% to 70% would give 25 at 50% and 50 at 70%. And the sentence under the table says it "scales linearly above 70%," the opposite of "above 70% earns ~full."
- Point 4 of the 60-second version says high-quality work "recharges fully." The worked example's high-quality Hannah (85%) gets back 42 of 50 and ends −8.
- "No scarcity cap" here; "a finite ATP pool" in the FAQ.
- "This is not a currency," but a used camera is listed for 350 ATP and ATP is "that society's unit of account." If I can sell a camera for it, it behaves like money to me, whatever it is called. Also: starting grant 100, self-started work nets zero at best, so who has 350?
- The recharge formula: sum of (confirmer trust × value fraction), capped at the ATP cost. Both factors are between 0 and 1, so twelve very trusted confirmers add up to less than 12. How does that become 20 ATP? There must be a missing multiplier.
- Simulator rows: spam −5, own low-quality post −5 net. Spam and an honest weak post cost the same in the end, and spam costs less up front.
- The section bar lists "At Scale", "Agent's Journey", "Technical" and "Why It Matters", and the text points me to an "ATP vs crypto tokens comparison table." None of those had any content in what I received.

### T3 (Trust Tensor) (/trust-tensor) (112:00)
- The surgeon analogy and "brilliant but unreliable" land immediately. This is the concept I understood best.
- Alice in three roles, with the arithmetic shown (0.4 × 85 + 0.3 × 90 + 0.3 × 95 = 89.5), is exactly the kind of worked example I wanted elsewhere.
- But Alice the mechanic has Talent 20%, Training 15%, composite 28%. Every other page says a newcomer starts at a neutral 0.5 in every dimension. She has never been a mechanic. Why is she not 0.5 / 0.5 / 0.5? And at 28% she is far under the 0.5 "survival line" in that role before she has touched a wrench. Is she dead as a mechanic?
- Decay: half-life example says a 0.80 Training score is 0.40 after six months and 0.20 after a year. So scores decay toward zero, not toward the neutral 0.5. Then "What if you take a 6-month break?": Temperament "essentially gone", Training about half. For star-analyst Alice that is roughly 0.4 × 0.85 + 0.3 × 0.45 + 0.3 × ~0 ≈ 0.48. A six-month break puts the site's own model employee under the survival line. The page says whether that kills you is "not settled" and "filed with the standard's maintainers." Honest, but alarming.
- "A few weeks of consistent activity rebuilds what faded." At +0.0042 Temperament per excellent action, rebuilding ~0.9 is over 200 excellent actions.
- A newcomer who joins and does nothing for a month: Temperament halves from 0.5 to 0.25, composite falls to about 0.42. Restricted for not showing up?

### Karma & Consequences (/karma-consequences) (124:00)
- The framing "What is permanent is the identity, not the penalty" is good and calmed some of my social-credit worry.
- The honesty about software-only identities being able to shed their record ("a genuine hole") is striking. It also means the headline promise of this page does not apply to what "most early adoption will look like."
- Life 1 on this page: the agent did *good work*, trust rose 0.50 → 0.64, it ran out of energy, and got **karma −15**, restarting at 70 ATP. Alice on First Contact: ran out of energy at trust 0.51 and got **+12**, restarting at 112. How It Works: lives end at 145 and restart at 145. Three pages, three different answers, opposite signs for the same event. Each page tells me the others exist and that nothing is settled. I believe them, and I now have no model of karma at all.
- Nothing says how the −15 was computed. The "formula" section only shows how karma converts into starting ATP, not where karma comes from.
- "Building positive karma is harder than destroying it" is stated as a consequence of the formula, but the formula multiplies both by the same 2.
- The "Phone-Verified: Consequences: session" card: I do not know what "session" means as a duration of consequences.
- I stopped here.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:

## Honest Assessment
There are two sites here, and I liked one of them a lot.

The first is the status-and-software site: the landing page, /tldr, /running-now, /hestia, most of /hub. It is concrete, modest, and unusually honest. It tells me what exists, what version it is, who actually uses it (only the builders), and where it is weak (the gate stops accidents, not adversaries; the record is of governed activity only; hardware binding is unproven on device). After those pages I could explain Web4 to a friend in three sentences and tell them truthfully what they could install today. If I ran AI coding agents I would try Hestia this weekend.

The second is the concept site: /why-web4's long tail, /first-contact, /how-it-works, /atp-economics, /karma-consequences, and the back half of /lct-explainer. It describes a full society with energy budgets, death, rebirth, karma, decay, neighborhoods and ceilings, in the present tense, with numbers to three decimal places. Almost every time I tried to follow one of those numbers from one page to the next, or even from a table to the sentence under it, it did not hold: the quality ramp, the karma sign, the Life 1 arithmetic, the visibility rule, the count of systems, capped vs uncapped energy. To the site's credit, many of these come with a caveat saying "this is one modelling choice" or "this is not settled." But the caveats have multiplied to the point where they are the main content. As a newcomer I do not need three incompatible illustrations of karma each footnoted against the other two. I need one, or none.

The deeper problem is that the two sites barely touch. The concept pages never say "and here is the part of this that Hestia or the hub does today." The product pages never mention energy, death or karma. So I finished unsure whether the elaborate economy is a description of the software, a plan for it, or a separate thought experiment that shares its vocabulary. The front page promises proof of what an AI agent did; the longest pages are about spam and reputation on social platforms; the running code is a vault and a permission gate for agents. I believe these connect. The site did not show me how.

What I inferred rather than was taught: that "death/rebirth/lives" is probably inherited from a simulation; that the 0.50 software cap plus the 0.50 survival line means today's real users sit exactly on the line; that the lab's "society" is probably a hub. None of those were said outright.

If I were advising: keep the first site exactly as it is, cut the concept pages to shapes without unsettled numbers, fix the one visibility sentence that contradicts itself across three pages, and add a "what runs this today" line to every concept page. I would come back to see whether Hestia's gate gets an observer the agent cannot reach. That is the thing that would make the front-page tagline true.
