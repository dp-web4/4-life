### How It Works (19:00 - 31:00)
- This is the most substantial page on the site and also where almost all of my friction came from. It is a genuinely good page in structure: five systems in plain English, then a lifecycle, then a worked example, then governance. The plain-English glosses are strong. "If someone usually posts coding tutorials at 9am and suddenly starts posting crypto spam at 3am from a new country, their coherence drops." I understood CI instantly from that.
- The five-filter list is memorable: "Without LCT, you can't enter. Without MRH, you can't reach anyone. Without ATP, you can't act. Without T3, you aren't trusted. Without CI, you're flagged."
- But the page contradicts itself on numbers in four places, and I noticed all four without trying:

  1. **"Every action costs energy"** appears twice near the top (key takeaways and the ATP gloss). Two sections later there is a list headed free actions: reading, browsing, viewing profiles, viewing trust scores, observing activity. So not every action costs energy. Small, but it is in the first three lines I read.

  2. **The earning examples say I can profit on one action.** "High-quality post: Cost 15 ATP → Earn 40 ATP = +25 net." "Helpful contribution: Cost 20 ATP → Earn 60 ATP = +40 net." "Quality compounds. Value creators earn more than they spend." I took this as the core incentive and moved on. On /atp-economics I then read the opposite rule, and it is stated flatly: "The cap means you can't profit on a single action, only recover its cost." Both pages have a reconciliation buried in them (a commissioned task pays what the work is worth), but the examples are labeled with things I would do on my own initiative: a post, a contribution, a creation. Nobody commissioned my post. So the labels put these in the capped channel and the numbers put them in the uncapped one.

  3. **The trust deltas are an order of magnitude apart from the formula on the same page.** The example block says "Delivered high-quality work on time: Talent +0.15, Training +0.20, Temperament +0.10." The formula, also on this page, is "base_delta = 0.02 × (quality − 0.5)", whose largest possible value is 0.01. The cascade example further down says "Talent +0.02, Training +0.02". /trust-tensor works it out as "+0.007" for a 0.85-quality attestation. So one page showed me +0.15, +0.02, and a formula capping at +0.01 for the same kind of event. The page also tells me "trust climbs slowly, one action at a time" while the headline example would take me from 0.50 to 0.90 Training in two actions.

  4. **The worked example dies with no cause.** The death section is clear: "Two ways to die... ATP hits zero (energy death)" and raw trust below 0.5 sustained. Then Life 1: "spent 60 ATP, earned 105 ATP... T3 → 0.65... **Died with 145 ATP**." Neither death condition fired. She has energy and rising trust. I read that line three times. All three lives end this way (145, 130, 165 ATP; T3 0.65, 0.72, 0.85). And since rebirth carries the balance forward unchanged (die with 145, reborn with 145), I could not work out what the life boundary is *for*.

- **The aliveness rule excludes newborns.** "Aliveness (ATP > 0 + Trust > 0.5 + CI coherent)." Everyone is born at exactly 0.5. /first-contact says "Above 0.50: full access." So by the site's own arithmetic a brand new member is not alive and does not have full access on day one. That cannot be intended, but it is what the two pages say together.
- **"Overall T3 score ≥ 0.5" decides whether you get reborn at all**, and nothing tells me how three separate dimensions become one overall number. The site spent real effort teaching me that a single trust score is the thing Web4 rejects, then used a single trust score for the life-or-death rule.
- **The transitivity invariant is named wrong.** "Trust through a chain can never exceed the weakest link. If Alice trusts Bob 0.9 and Bob trusts Carol 0.6, Alice's transitive trust in Carol is at most 0.54." The weakest link is 0.6. 0.54 is the product. The AI orchestration section confirms the real rule: "trust multiplies, it doesn't add." So the label and the math are two different rules and the label is the weaker one.
- Governance (SAL) was better than I expected. The plagiarism walkthrough with an appeal is concrete, and "All four are design, not track record. Each one needs a live community to work" is the kind of line that makes me trust the rest of the page more.
- Clicked: /atp-economics, to check the earning numbers.

## Honest Assessment

This site is much better than most technical explainers and it failed me in a specific, fixable way: the prose is trustworthy and the numbers are not.

The prose is genuinely excellent. "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules" is the clearest positioning sentence I have read for a project this abstract. The witnessing definition arrives inline before I need it. The Web2/Web3/Web4 triptych taught me the pitch in fifteen seconds. "How real is it, honestly?" and the risks page are the reason I would come back: a page that writes "naming it here is not the same as having closed it" has earned the right to be believed elsewhere. The badge key (Spec / Reference / Running, each with a plain definition) is a good idea, well executed, and I only wish /tldr had linked to it since that is where I first met the badges.

What broke my trust was arithmetic. I did not go looking for contradictions. I was trying to answer two ordinary beginner questions, and each one dead-ended in numbers that disagreed across pages.

Question one: can I join without buying hardware? /why-web4 said identity is tied to my security chip. /lct-explainer said software-only is a supported tier. Fine, the second page corrected the first, except the correction came with a number: software-only caps at 0.50. And I had just been taught, twice, that 0.50 is the line for access and the line for aliveness, and that living below it is fatal. So the answer to "can I join" turns out to be "yes, into a tier that by construction can never clear the bar." The risks page knows this tier is a second-class experience and says so bravely, but even it stops at "lower ceiling" and never notices that the ceiling and the threshold are the same number. Then /hestia, the friendliest and only actually-running piece, the one the onramp tells beginners to start with, turns out to be exactly that tier, and says nothing about it. That is the finding I would most want fixed: the recommended front door leads to the constrained tier, silently.

Question two: does contributing pay? /how-it-works said yes, emphatically, with numbers: spend 15, earn 40. /atp-economics said no, in a sentence with no wiggle room: you cannot profit on a single action, only recover its cost. Then its own simulator, the interactive thing on the page, let me profit on a single action. Both pages contain the reconciliation, and it is a good design (recharge refunds, payment earns), but in both cases the examples are labeled with unsolicited self-initiated work while carrying payment-channel numbers. The concept is fine. The illustrations of it are wrong.

Once I had found those two, I started checking things, and the checks kept failing: +0.15 trust from one action against a formula that maxes at +0.01, a worked example whose protagonist dies with 145 ATP under rules that say death means running out of ATP, an aliveness test that excludes everyone on their first day, an invariant called "weakest link" that computes a product, three different definitions of karma. None of these is fatal alone. Together they mean I cannot use the site's numbers to reason, and reasoning with the numbers is exactly what a site teaching a quantitative trust model is for. The narrative pages and the reference pages appear to have been written from different specs, and /how-it-works, which is step 4 of the official path and the page that promises "the whole picture", is where the drift concentrates.

So: yes I would return, because the honesty is real and the ideas are worth the effort. Maybe I would recommend it, and only with a caveat, because I would not want a friend to spend twenty minutes carefully following the arithmetic to arrive where I did, which is at a tier the numbers quietly exclude, doing the one thing the site actually invites them to do.
