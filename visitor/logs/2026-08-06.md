## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: "Trust, built into the internet." Then immediately a concrete reason to care: AI agents already write code and move money, and nobody can prove what one actually did. That is a real problem I have felt without naming it. Best opening line I have read on a site like this.
- The four cards under "The Onramp" told me the shape before I clicked anything: one standard, plus community / personal / enterprise. "A standard alone is inert. These four pieces are how you actually stand it up." That sentence did a lot of work.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 Minutes", because the page told me to read in that order and I appreciated being told.
- Confusion points: none worth reporting. The nav is large but grouped sensibly.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (03:10)
- The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast is the clearest thing on the site. Web2: a platform declares your reputation. Web3: whoever holds the key is assumed to be you. Web4: other participants observe what you do and sign a record. I understood the pitch in about forty seconds.
- "First, what is this site?" pre-empted a question I had already formed on the landing page (is 4-Life one of the four pieces?). Good catch by whoever wrote that.
- Each piece carries a status badge. Hestia is "Running", the other three are "Reference". I noted this and moved on, but it came back later.
- Clicked: "Why Web4?"

### Why Web4? (07:40)
- Five named problems, all recognizable: spam is free, reputation is trapped in silos, banned users get fresh starts, AI makes it worse, platforms have no appeal. I did not need convincing.
- The FAQ is where I started actually testing the site. "Is this deployed or theoretical?" answers "not vaporware, but not a product yet." I trust a site more after it says that.
- One FAQ answer stuck out badly, and I only realized why two pages later. **"What if I lose my hardware?"** answers "No, recovery is built in. LCT supports multiple linked devices." That is stated as a flat, unconditional reassurance. It is not unconditional. See friction.
- Clicked: "Walk through with Alice"

### First Contact (13:20)
- The "reading Alice's panel" preamble is good practice. It defines the two numbers before showing them move.
- Then the sentence that snagged me: "Starts neutral at 0.50. **Above 0.50: full access. Below 0.50:** features get restricted."
- Alice starts at exactly 0.50. Act 1 is literally labeled "Equal start for everyone | 100 ATP, 0.50 trust." So on day one Alice is neither above nor below the line. Does she have full access or not? The page defines both sides of the boundary and not the boundary itself, and then puts every new user precisely on it.
- I assumed "0.50 is fine" and kept reading. Noting honestly: the site did not teach me that, I guessed it.
- Clicked: "How It Works"

### How It Works (20:05)
- Dense but well built. I liked that the numbers are checkable. Rebirth eligibility: 0.4(0.40) + 0.3(0.60) + 0.3(0.60) = 0.52. I did that on paper and it came out right. Transitive trust 0.9 x 0.6 = 0.54, and a 5-hop chain at 0.9 each landing at 0.59. All correct. Checkable arithmetic buys a lot of credibility.
- The coherence example (usually posts coding tutorials at 9am, suddenly posts crypto spam at 3am from a new country) made the Coherence Index click instantly.
- The R&D banner is repeated inline: "No network you can join yet, the present tense below is the model talking." I never lost track of what was real. This is the site's strongest habit.
- **Then the Complete Example broke for me.** The page states death has exactly two causes: ATP hits zero, or raw trust falls below 0.5 and stays there. Trust death is permanent, no rebirth. Then the worked lifecycle says Life 1 "died with **145 ATP**, T3 reached **0.65**", and was reborn.
- I read that four times. 145 ATP is not zero. 0.65 is not below 0.5. Neither of the page's two death conditions applies. The example agent dies for no reason the page allows, on the same page that just told me the rules.
- Clicked: "Running Now"

### Running Now (27:30)
- The badge legend up front (Spec / Reference / Running) is exactly right, and defining "Running" as "live instances actually exist and are in day-to-day use" is a commitment I can check.
- "The honest bottom line" is the single best paragraph on the site. It names what is stable, what is a runnable MVP, what is deployed, what is not production, and what is Spec only, in one breath, and ends with "the value of a witnessed audit trail is that it never lies about which of these is which, and neither will this page."
- "Where you fit today" is excellent. Five rows, I found mine immediately ("here to understand it, not to run it"), and it did not make me feel dismissed for picking it.
- Also caught here: the site pre-empts a contradiction I was about to file. Hardbound's TPM binding is "not yet validated on-device", but the page adds "not to be confused with the core standard's TPM2 work, which *has* been exercised on a real Intel TPM 2.0". I had actually flagged that as an inconsistency from the earlier FAQ. The site got there first. Credit where due.
- The Hestia row is what sent me to the LCT page: it kept mentioning hardware and I do not have a TPM.

### LCT / Verified Presence (33:00)
- "A digital ID card that lives in your device's security chip" is a good analogy, and the Web2/Web3/Web4 identity table reinforces the earlier one.
- The trust ceiling table is where this got personal: TPM 0.90, Secure Enclave 0.85, FIDO2 0.75, **Software Only 0.50**.
- So my tier is capped at exactly 0.50. The same 0.50 that First Contact called the line. The page even says it outright: "What the software-only ceiling really costs you is margin: you start at the line with no buffer above it." That is an honest and well-phrased sentence and I appreciated it.
- But now the ambiguity from First Contact is not academic, it decides whether my tier works at all. If "above 0.50" is the rule, a software-only user is never above it and never has full access, permanently. If "0.50 or higher" is the rule, they are fine but fragile. The site says both things in different places (see friction) and never says which applies to me.
- The LCT page's own answer is "at every tier you can post, earn ATP, build karma, and join societies, what changes is the terms." I could not tell whether "the terms" means "you are restricted right now" or "you are one bad week from being restricted." Those are very different products.
- Clicked: "How the pieces compose"

### The Onramp (38:15)
- "They aren't four separate products that happen to look alike. They are one shape at three scales, all speaking the same vocabulary." That landed.
- Briefly alarmed by hestia described as "an early prototype that a capable agent can route around today" right after Running Now called it deployed and in daily use. Re-read it: the sentence scopes to hestia's **policy gate**, not hestia. Fine, but I had to slow down to see it.
- Clicked: "Hestia", because it is the one that is actually running.

### Hestia (42:00)
- "A day with Hestia" is the best explanatory writing on the site. Six concrete steps with real commands. `hestia delegate grant my-agent --role administrator --expires 24` then `hestia delegate revoke`. I now understand what "scoped revocable delegation" means because I watched it happen, not because it was defined.
- The honesty here is unusual and I want to say so plainly. The page volunteers that the witness chain is tamper-evident but **not complete**: "the same hook that runs the policy gate is the thing that writes the entry, so an action that routes around the gate writes no entry at all. Your witness chain is a record of governed activity, not a record of all activity, and silence in it is not proof that nothing happened." Almost nobody writes that sentence about their own product.
- Same with "the policy gate stops accidents, not adversaries... two environment variables suffice today."
- My complaint is not that this is here. It is that it is only here, and the page that recruited me said the opposite. See friction.

### ATP Economics (47:10)
- The biology origin explains the name well, and "ATP is cash, ADP is the receipt" is a clean handle. "It is not a cryptocurrency, no outside market, no way to cash out" answered my skepticism before I could type it.
- "What's settled vs what's still moving on this page" is a pattern I wish more sites copied.
- Then I did the arithmetic in the worked example and got worried. Hannah does 85% quality work: spends 50, earns back 42, **net −8**. Sam at 35%: net −44. So even excellent self-initiated work loses ATP. The page confirms this is by design: "recharge refunds, payment earns," capped at what you spent.
- So the only way to gain ATP is commissioned work someone else prices. I start with 100 ATP. At −8 per quality post I get about twelve posts before I die. Nothing on the page tells me how a newcomer with zero track record gets commissioned inside twelve actions, or whether there is any floor or refill. I looked for it and did not find it.

### What Could Go Wrong (50:00)
- I clicked this expecting a fig leaf and got the opposite. Nine risks, each with severity, likelihood, mitigation, and an "honest assessment" that frequently concedes the mitigation is partial.
- Risk 8 names my exact situation: software-only users at a 0.50 ceiling are "a real second-class experience", scoped with "roughly 1 in 6 people globally has some form of disability, and ~3 billion people still don't own a smartphone", and closes with "naming it here is not the same as having closed it... this is a policy choice, not a technical inevitability, and the choice is contestable." That is the most credible paragraph on the site.
- Risk 9 concedes the agent gate is bypassable and that the fix is "structural, and unbuilt."
- Risk 8 also contains the sentence that retroactively broke the Why Web4 FAQ for me: "A software-only user has no hardware witness to vouch for them, so there is nothing to recover to: they start over from zero with a fresh identity."
- Also finally read here: "Web4 uses a 0.5 trust threshold as the minimum bar for continued participation." Third phrasing of the same line.

### Karma & Consequences (last stop)
- Came here to resolve the death confusion from How It Works. It got worse.
- This page gives a formula: `next_life_atp = base_atp + (karma * karma_multiplier)`, base 100, multiplier 2, so "+10 karma → 120 ATP".
- How It Works says you are reborn with the balance you died holding ("Died with 145 ATP. Reborn with 145 ATP").
- Those are two different rules. Under the formula, what you died holding does not matter. Under How It Works, karma points do not matter.
- How It Works does admit "how *much* karma carries is also unsettled: whether you keep your whole final balance or a reduced portion of it is not decided." That is honest about the amount, but it does not cover the fact that another page states a specific competing formula as settled fact.

## Honest Assessment

This is the most honest technical site I have read, and I want to lead with that because the friction list below it is long.

The R&D framing is not a disclaimer buried in a footer. It is repeated inline, in the middle of the explanations, in the present tense: "the present tense below is the model talking." "Running Now" defines its own badges and then holds itself to them. "What Could Go Wrong" concedes that its own mitigations are partial, names its worst structural limit as "an accepted limit today, not a scheduled repair," and admits the exclusion of software-only users is "a policy choice, not a technical inevitability, and the choice is contestable." /hestia volunteers that its own audit chain is incomplete by construction. I have never seen a project write that sentence about itself. It made me trust the rest of the site more, not less, which I assume is the point and which I think actually works.

The teaching is also good. The Web2/Web3/Web4 contrast is the clearest framing of the problem I have encountered. The Alice walkthrough gives the numbers something to happen to. "A day with Hestia" taught me what scoped delegation is by showing me the commands rather than defining the term. "Where you fit today" found me a row and did not condescend to it. And the arithmetic is checkable, which matters more than it sounds: I verified four separate calculations by hand and all four were right, and that bought the site credibility it then spent well.

So the problems are worth taking seriously precisely because the rest is careful.

The one that bothered me most is the death and rebirth story, because it fails on the site's own terms. /how-it-works states two death conditions and then, four screens later, walks through an agent dying while meeting neither of them. I re-read it several times assuming I had missed something. Then /karma-consequences gave a rebirth formula that contradicts the one in the example. A site whose entire thesis is "we will not lie about which of these is which" has, in its flagship worked example, a number that cannot happen. It is not a credibility problem yet, because I read it as sloppiness rather than spin. But it is the one place where the arithmetic stopped checking out, and after four successful checks, the fifth failing is loud.

The one that bothered me most *personally* is the 0.50 boundary. I do not have a TPM. That puts me in the software-only tier, capped at 0.50, and the site tells me three different things about what 0.50 means: above it is full access, below it is restriction, and it is the minimum bar. Under one reading I am fine but fragile. Under another I am permanently second-class and can never be otherwise. The site has clearly thought hard about my tier, Risk 8 is genuinely excellent and does not flinch, and /lct-explainer's "what it costs you is margin" is a real answer to a real question. But it is an answer to "what is your risk" and I was asking "what is my status." One extra sentence saying what happens exactly at 0.50 would close it.

The third one is smaller but it is the kind of thing that erodes trust asymmetrically. /running-now told me every consequential act lands in my witness chain. /hestia told me that is not true and explained precisely why not. The retraction is more honest than the promise, but I read the promise first, and if I had stopped at /running-now I would have installed on a false understanding. Given how scrupulous this site is everywhere else, that specific sentence reads like an oversight rather than a pitch, which is why it is worth fixing rather than worth being angry about.

Would I come back? Yes. I want to see whether anyone stands up a hub. Would I recommend it? Yes, with the caveat that the numbers around death and rebirth do not currently add up, and that if you do not own a security chip you should read Risk 8 before you get invested.
