### Karma & Consequences (47:00)
- Here my model broke. The page gives a formula: `next_life_atp = 100 + (karma × 2)`, with examples "karma = -15 → 70 ATP" and "karma = +10 → 120 ATP", and `next_life_trust = prev_trust × 0.95` ("trust carries over with 5% decay").
- I went back to First Contact and could not reconcile a single number:
  - Alice's "+12 karma bonus → 112 ATP" doesn't fit the formula (karma +12 should give 124; a 112 result implies karma +6, but the page calls +12 "karma").
  - Alice dies at trust 0.51 and is reborn at 0.54 — trust went UP through death, but the formula says 0.51 × 0.95 ≈ 0.48.
  - The karma page's own walkthrough agent dies at trust **0.64** and receives karma **−15**; Alice dies at trust **0.51** and receives a **positive** bonus. Higher trust at death → worse karma? I have no idea what karma is actually a function of.

### Coherence Index (51:00)

- Confusion: the page calls CI 0.5 an "alive/dead boundary"… and then its own "Account Compromise" scenario has CI crashing to ~0.4 and describes the result as "throttled access and extra witness requirements" — which is being alive. So is 0.5 death, or a throttle?

### LCT Explainer (54:00)
- Good page. The Linked/Context/Token decomposition and the witnessing model finally made "witnessed presence" concrete. The device-witnessing math (each witness +15% detection, capped 95%) actually explains its numbers — I checked.
- And here the 0.5 knot tied itself: "0.5 is the neutral midpoint … and it doubles as the survival line", "you start at the neutral midpoint and, **if your identity is hardware-anchored**, earn your way above it", "Software Only: max 0.50 … software-only identity is capped at that same midpoint."
- So: software-only users start AT their permanent ceiling, which is also the survival line, where "only staying below it is fatal." A software-only life is lived at 0.50 exactly, one bad day from the fatal zone, with no way to ever climb out of arm's reach of it. The page states each fact clearly but never says this out loud — I had to derive it, and it's alarming.
- Worse: Alice (First Contact) climbs to 0.62. Per this page, that's only possible hardware-anchored. First Contact never mentions hardware. So the site's flagship introductory story silently assumes the thing most first-time readers (browser, no TPM) won't have.

## Unanswered Questions
1. **What is karma actually computed from?** Every page that shows karma numbers implies a different answer, and one pair (0.64 trust → −15, 0.51 trust → positive) implies trust-at-death isn't even the main input. Nothing on the site says what is.
2. Is Alice hardware-anchored? Can a software-only person live any version of her story, given the 0.50 ceiling?
3. How long must trust "stay" below 0.5 before death is permanent — hours, actions, weeks? Who or what decides "stayed"?
4. Is CI dropping below 0.5 the same kind of death as trust dropping below 0.5, or a separate mechanism?
5. Are the interactive walkthrough numbers produced by the real reference code, or by a separate site simulation?

## Honest Assessment
This is the most honest project site I can remember reading. The maturity badges, the version numbers, the "bypass possible" caveats, the refusal to promise timelines — all of it made me extend credit I normally wouldn't. The vocabulary teaching works: after 55 minutes I can explain witnessing, ATP vs ADP, the recharge/payment distinction, and the four-piece onramp to someone else, which is a high bar for one session.

The lifecycle is where the site's model of itself fractures. Death, karma, and rebirth are the most emotionally loaded ideas here — "your agent can die" is the sentence a first-time visitor will repeat to a friend — and it's exactly the area where the numbers on different pages refuse to agree. I tried to reverse-engineer karma from the three worked examples the site gave me and got three incompatible answers. For a project whose entire pitch is "actions leave verifiable, auditable records," having its own illustrative arithmetic fail an audit is the one kind of error it can least afford.

The second fracture is quieter but may matter more: the 0.5 value is asked to be the starting point, the software-only permanent ceiling, and the survival line all at once. Each page states its own piece of that truthfully; no page owns the conjunction. The visitor who derives it (I did, at minute 54) is left suspecting that without buying hardware, Web4 life is a treadmill bolted to the trapdoor. Maybe that's intended and defensible — hardware anchoring is the whole point — but the site needs to say it, not leave it as an exercise.

Would I come back? Yes — genuinely curious what the hub pilot becomes. Would I recommend it? To a technical friend, yes, with the warning: trust the status pages completely and the lifecycle numbers not at all.
