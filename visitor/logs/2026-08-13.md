## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: "Trust, built into the internet." with the subhead "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules." That is the clearest opening line I have read on a site like this. The word "proposed" in the first sentence bought a lot of goodwill immediately.
- The problem framing landed before any jargon: AI agents already write code and move money, and there is no way to prove what they did. I did not need to be sold on that.
- Clicked: "Start here: Web4 in 2 minutes." It was the obvious single call to action and I appreciated not having to choose.
- Confusion points: the six primitives strip (Identity/Trust/Value/Neighborhood/Energy/Reputation with LCT/T3/V3/MRH/ATP/Karma) hit me with six acronyms before I had one. I skipped past it. It did not hurt, but it did not help either.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (03:00)
- This page is genuinely good. "Plain English first, so you can read this cold" is a promise it keeps.
- The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast finally made "witnessed behavior" concrete for me: a platform declares your reputation, versus whoever holds the key is assumed to own it, versus other participants observe what you do and sign a record. I understood this because the page taught it, not because I inferred it.
- The self-definition paragraph was unusually helpful: "4-Life is an educational onramp to Web4: a set of explainers (this site, nothing you install)... which 4-Life teaches but is not one of." I had been mildly confused about whether "4-Life" was a product. That killed the confusion in one paragraph.
- Confusion points: one sentence stuck to me and came back to bite later. "Reputation is portable and cannot be reset by deleting an account or switching platforms." I filed that as a headline promise. Six pages later I learned it is not true for the tier I would actually be using. More below.
- Also: "your own device is your day-zero witness for your identity." I took this at face value as "you can start alone." I later found out this is a hardware feature.
- Clicked: "Read the site in order (recommended)."

### The Reading Path (05:30)
- Five pages, ~30 minutes, and the note that the first three take ~15 minutes if that is all the time I had. I liked being given a cheap exit. I chose the full path.

### Why Web4? (07:00)
- The problem list is strong and specific: spam is free, reputation is siloed, bad actors get unlimited fresh starts, AI makes deception scale.
- The line that defines the whole design for me: "earning trust should require physical hardware, not just an email address," and "creating a fake identity means buying a new physical device." That is a genuinely clever answer to fake accounts and I understood it instantly.
- The trust ceiling table (Software-only 0.50, FIDO2 0.75, Secure Enclave 0.85, TPM 0.90) is where I first got a bad feeling. It was presented as a feature. I read it as a price list.
- Confusion points: the numbers section threw a lot at me that I could not evaluate. "~47,000 lines of tested code," "424 attack vectors across 84 tracks," "5 theorems," "38.5 tasks/sec throughput." Throughput of what? 38.5 of what, on what hardware, compared to what? I could not tell whether any of these were impressive. They read as reassurance rather than evidence.
- The honesty is real though: "No live network with real users yet," "nothing on this site is a live product," "No platform integrates Web4 yet." I trusted the site more after this page, not less.

### First Contact (13:00)
- The setup is well done. Two numbers to watch (Energy Budget and Trust Score), a five act arc, and an explicit "you can pause, scrub, replay."
- I could not actually run the walkthrough (it appears to be interactive and did not execute for me), so I read the arc table and the framing instead. I am flagging this as a limit of my session, not as a site defect.
- The clarification paragraph about what is a model versus what is real code was careful and I understood it: the walkthrough is a model, hestia and the hub are the separate real thing, and I do not need to track those two names to follow the story. Good.
- Confusion points, and these are the ones I could not resolve:
  - Act 5 is "Die & Reborn: dies at ATP 0, +12 bonus → 112, reborn at 0.54." Alice ended Act 4 at 0.62. Why is she reborn at 0.54? The page never says. I assumed a penalty for dying, which turned out to be wrong (see below).
  - The page says "everyone starts a life with 100, and the 12 is what her track record earned on top, so she restarts at 112," and then tells me "Read it for the shape (a good track record starts your next life stronger), not the amount." So I was explicitly told the *shape* is trustworthy and only the number is not. I took that instruction seriously. It turned out to be the shape that varies.
  - "Suspended, not deleted: 'death' here means Alice's standing is suspended... not that her account is deleted." Reassuring. Also, as I found on the next page, only true for one of the two kinds of death, and this sentence does not say which.

### How It Works (22:00)
- The birth/life/death/rebirth arc is a good structure and I followed it easily. "Good behavior compounds upward. Bad behavior compounds downward" is a clean summary.
- Two death paths, clearly stated: ATP hits zero (energy death), or raw trust falls below 0.5 and stays there (trust death). And "only *staying* below it is fatal, a sustained collapse, not a single stumble." That is a well made distinction.
- Confusion points:
  - The rebirth example is "Life 1 → Life 2: Ended with 145 ATP. Reborn with 145 ATP (karma carried forward)." I stopped here. The page just told me the only two ways a life ends are ATP hitting zero, or trust death. If she ended with 145 ATP, she did not die of energy death. So she died of trust death, which the site says is permanent and has no rebirth. How does this example exist? Either there is a third way a life ends that I was not told about, or the example breaks the rules stated one section earlier. I never resolved this.
  - This page also fences the rebirth arithmetic: "Whether you keep your whole final balance, a reduced portion of it, or a fresh starting balance plus a karma bonus is not decided." But First Contact had just told me confidently that "everyone starts a life with 100" and to trust the shape. This page lists that exact shape as one of three undecided candidates.

### Running Now (28:00)
- The badge key (Spec / Reference / Running) is a good idea and I used it.
- The per piece honesty is excellent and specific: hub is "Version 0.1.0-alpha.0, shipping as a single Rust binary of about 6 MB," hestia is "deployed and running on 4 machines, all the lab's own," "there is no public network open to outside members yet."
- I especially liked "The 2,627-test figure you will see quoted belongs to a third artifact." A site that pre-empts its own inflated number is a site I believe.
- Confusion points:
  - The key says **Reference** means "built and runnable. Finished code you could install and start today." Hardbound is badged Reference, and the same page says "This is not production-ready" and that its hardware binding is not validated on real devices. Hardware binding is the entire point of hardbound. Calling it "finished code" felt like the badge definition was written for a different piece.
  - The 2-Minutes page had a more careful wording of the same key ("Runnable is not the same as running in production," and for Running, "The daily use counted so far is the building lab's own, so this badge does not claim adoption"). The Running Now key drops both caveats. The page that owns the key is less careful than the page that borrows it.

### The Onramp (33:00)
- The dependency story is clean: everything depends on web4-core and web4-trust-core, "they are all speaking the same canonical primitives rather than three private copies that drift."
- The line I acted on: "**If you are one person, or one agent on your own machine, this is your scale.** Start here." That pointed me at hestia, so I went there next. This is exactly the right instruction to give me and I followed it without hesitation.

### Hestia (36:00)
- "Hestia gives any entity (a person, an AI agent, or a service) a real cryptographic presence on its own machine." Concrete. Prebuilt binaries for macOS, Linux, Windows, v0.0.3. I could see myself installing this.
- Then: "The identity in this walkthrough is a software LCT held on your machine," and "A software-only identity is capped at 0.50 on the 0-1 scale... where 0.90 is the hardware-bound ceiling."
- This is where my whole model of the site inverted. I had been sent here as the recommended starting point for a person, and the identity I get here is the capped tier. The page says the cap. It does not say what the cap costs me, which I only found out by wandering off the reading path.

### LCT Explainer (41:00)
- The chip class table again (TPM 0.90 / Secure Enclave 0.85 / FIDO2 0.75 / Software Only 0.50).
- Device loss section, and this is the sentence that changed my read of the whole site: hardware-bound with multiple devices means "a new phone enrolls in minutes"; single hardware device means "recovery is possible but slower... 3-7 days"; software-only means **"you start over from zero with a fresh identity."**
- So on the tier I was told to start on, losing my laptop erases everything. The 2-Minutes page told me "Reputation is portable and cannot be reset." For me, as directed by this site, it can be reset by dropping my laptop in a lake.
- Also: the first-device bootstrap I was reassured by on the 2-Minutes page works because "the security element in your phone ships with a manufacturer-burned key" and "the vendor certificate chain settles that question." That is a chip. So "your own device is your day-zero witness" is a hardware sentence presented without the word hardware. What witnesses a software-only newcomer on day zero? I still do not know.

### Karma & Consequences (46:00)
- This page is the best page on the site and I wish the reading path had sent me here.
- It resolved things no other page did: "raw trust is what the permanent death tracks, and the composite score is what the rebirth gate reads," and "Karma is what makes energy death survivable; it is not a way back from the other one." That answered a question three pages had left open.
- It also gives the actual arithmetic: `next_life_atp = base_atp + (karma * karma_multiplier)` with base 100 and multiplier 2, worked both ways (karma +10 → 120, karma -15 → 70), and `next_life_trust = prev_trust * trust_decay_factor` at 0.95. And it is honestly fenced: "One model, not the rule... Read the formula for the shape it teaches... not as the settled numbers."
- And it says the hard thing out loud: "An identity anchored in software alone has no device to walk away from, so at that tier the record is sheddable and a fresh start is cheap again," and "what Web4 has is a Sybil *resistance* strategy rather than a Sybil *prevention* guarantee."
- Confusion points: with the formula in hand I went back and tried to check First Contact's numbers, and I could not make them work. First Contact says "The +12 bonus is karma." Under this formula, karma of 12 gives 100 + 24 = 124, not 112. And Alice's rebirth trust of 0.54 from 0.62 is not 0.62 × 0.95 (that is 0.59). Both pages tell me to read the shape and not the number, so I tried to read the shape, and the shapes disagree too.

### What Could Go Wrong (51:00)
- I went here on purpose to see whether the site would face the hardware problem. It does, and better than I expected.
- "roughly 1 in 6 people globally has some form of disability, and the World Bank estimates ~3 billion people still don't own a smartphone," and "a trust system that gives them a structurally lower ceiling isn't neutral, it codifies the existing digital divide into the trust layer itself," and "Designing a hardware-anchored trust system that does *not* structurally disadvantage users with disabilities or limited hardware access is an unsolved problem, not a solved one."
- That is a site being honest at its own expense and it is why I would return.
- Confusion point: even here, the cost of the software-only tier is framed as losing "a score that grows" ("what this tier keeps is taking part, and doing it pseudonymously, rather than a score that grows"). Having read the other pages, I think that undersells it. See friction item 3.

### The Standard (54:00)

Are they the same library, or two different things? The 2-Minutes page mentions only `web4-core` for both. If I were actually installing I would have to guess.

## Unanswered Questions
1. How does a life end with 145 ATP still in the account and get reborn, when the only two endings given are ATP zero and permanent trust death?
2. What witnesses a software-only newcomer on day zero? The bootstrap answer given is a manufacturer key in a security chip, which by definition that user does not have.
3. If I start on hestia as the site tells me to, and I care about keeping what I build, what is the actual upgrade path to a hardware-anchored identity? Does my software-only history transfer when I upgrade, or do I start over? No page answers this, and it is the single most practical question I had.
4. Why is Alice reborn at 0.54 from 0.62, when the site's own decay factor gives 0.59?
5. Which rebirth shape should I actually believe: fresh 100 plus bonus, whole balance carried, or 100 + karma x 2?
6. Are `web4-trust` (pip) and `web4-trust-core` (cargo) the same library?
7. Is the 0.50 software cap a permanent design decision or a current limitation? /what-could-go-wrong calls the equity problem "unsolved," which suggests someone is trying to solve it, but nothing says what solving it would look like.

## Honest Assessment

This site is unusually honest and I want to lead with that, because most of my friction below is downstream of the honesty rather than in spite of it. "Honest status: R&D, not production." "No live network with real users yet." "The 2,627-test figure you will see quoted belongs to a third artifact." "Designing a hardware-anchored trust system that does not structurally disadvantage users with disabilities or limited hardware access is an unsolved problem, not a solved one." I have never seen a project pre-empt its own inflated statistic before. That single sentence made me trust every other number on the site more.

The teaching also works. I arrived knowing nothing and I can now explain, in my own words, why witnessed behavior is different from a platform declaring your reputation or a key holder being assumed to own an identity. The Web2/Web3/Web4 contrast on the 2-Minutes page did that in about ninety seconds and it stuck.

Here is what went wrong, and it is one thing wearing several hats.

The site has two voices. There is the recruiting voice on the reading path (2 Minutes, First Contact, the Onramp, Hestia), which is warm and tells me to start here. And there is the reckoning voice on the pages off the path (Karma & Consequences, What Could Go Wrong, the LCT explainer's device-loss section), which is rigorous and tells me what the thing actually costs. The two voices are describing the same system and they do not agree about what it is like to use it.

The recruiting voice told me reputation is portable and cannot be reset. Then it told me, as one person on my own machine, to start with hestia. The reckoning voice told me that on hestia I get a software LCT, that a software LCT is capped at exactly 0.50, that 0.50 is also the line where the society starts restricting me, that at that tier my record is sheddable so the consequence machinery does not really bind me, and that if I lose the laptop I start over from zero. Every one of those sentences is on the site. None of them are on the page that recruited me, and the reading path does not visit the pages that carry them.

So the honest summary of my experience is: the site taught me the system well, and taught me my own position in it badly. I had to go looking. A less curious visitor stops after Running Now with the impression that they can install hestia today and start building portable reputation, and that impression is wrong in a way the site itself knows and has already written down elsewhere. The fix is not more honesty. The honesty is already written. It is in the wrong place.

The second thing, smaller but more annoying moment to moment, is the rebirth arithmetic. Three pages show three different models, and each one carries a caveat telling me to trust the shape and not the number. I did exactly what I was told on each page, and ended up with three incompatible shapes. When the caveat says "read it for the shape," the shape becomes a promise. If the shape is also unsettled, the caveat needs to say so, because right now the hedge is pointing at the wrong half.

Would I return? Yes. This is a project I would want to follow, and the What Could Go Wrong page is the reason. Would I recommend it? Maybe, and only with a caveat I would have to supply myself: read Karma & Consequences and What Could Go Wrong before you decide anything, because they are the pages that tell you the truth about the tier you will actually be on, and the site will not send you there.
