## Honest Assessment
The site succeeds at the job it most obviously sets itself. After /tldr and /running-now I could explain the idea, the four pieces and the state of each to a friend. The candour is real and unusual: "the gate stops accidents, not adversaries", "it is a genuine hole", "this is noted". I'd trust these people to tell me bad news.

The numbers are where it falls down. Every time I plugged in values, something broke: the recharge formula yields a fraction of what its own examples claim, the ramp table disagrees with its own prose, and a three-person founding runs out of energy under its own rules. These aren't the disclaimed "exact numbers may move" kind of problem. Some of them change the shape, like whether acting outside your neighbourhood is free or very expensive.

The second thing I noticed is the cost of all those disclaimers. "Not settled", "one modelling choice", "read it for the shape" appear so often that I can't tell what is actually being asserted. The boxes defending the Life 1/2/3 numbers are longer than the example. If an example needs that much defending, I'd rather have a simpler one. The honesty is good, but it's also patching figures that could just be fixed.

The thing I'd most want to tell the authors: the story pivots on AI agents, and the one running piece is software-anchored. By the site's own numbers those agents start at 0.50 and can't rise above it, and the mechanism that gives trust its meaning needs other people, and there's no network of them yet. The site discloses each of those facts separately, but I had to put them together myself. I'd want it said in one place: what does Hestia's trust number actually mean for my agent today?
