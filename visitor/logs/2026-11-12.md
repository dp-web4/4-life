### Web4 in 2 Minutes (/tldr) (02:00)
- Good page. Web2/Web3/Web4 comparison landed: platform declares, key is assumed, behaviour is witnessed.
- The badge key (Spec / Reference / Running) is clear. I appreciated that "Running" is said to mean the lab's own daily use, not adoption.
- Slight unease: Hestia is "Running" here, but on the next page the big FAQ answer says "nothing on this site is a live product" and "no live network with real users yet". I could reconcile those after a minute (the lab uses it, but nobody else does), but I had to stop and think.

### Why Web4? (/why-web4) (07:00)
- The problem section is strong and relatable (spam, reputation silos, fresh starts, AI agents).
- "What it gets right / Failure" for prior solutions is fair-minded. I liked it.
- **Then I hit a contradiction in the four-ideas section.** Card 4 says "Everyone stays visible - what changes is weight. A stranger's message reaches you." Twenty lines later, the "All four, in one moment" example says "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply **at all** - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread." Then the FAQ "Who can see my messages?" says "Everyone can see them - nothing is hidden." So, can strangers see my reply or not? The worked example (the concrete one, the one I'd remember) says no. Everything else says yes.
- The "fractions of a cent of energy" line confused me. Two pages later I learn ATP has no price against money and can't be bought. So what is a "cent" here?
- I opened the FAQ index (72 questions!) and read about 12 answers.
  - "What backs ATP?" says "**Capped issuance**: A community mints a finite ATP pool... ATP matters exactly because it's limited." Later, on /atp-economics, I read "**No scarcity cap**, no mining" and "there is no protocol-level constraint on what a society issues." Those are opposite answers to "is ATP scarce?"
  - "When does an agent die?" says energy death is recoverable, but "ATP itself only comes back through contribution, so recovery means being able to work again, **not waiting for a refill**." But you're suspended and "can't take actions". How do you contribute while suspended? Then on First Contact, Alice is "reborn with 112 ATP", which reads exactly like a refill.
  - "Is 0.5 threshold universal?" says 0.5 is a society-level parameter (a science community might use 0.7). But every other page states "raw trust below 0.5 and stays there = permanent death" as if it were universal. Is death at 0.5 everywhere, or at 0.7 in the science community?
  - The transition FAQ says at the Accountable tier "High-trust users barely notice (their costs are lower). **Spammers notice immediately (their costs are higher).**" Keep that in mind; see /atp-economics below.

- Step 3: "Their confirmations recover 12 of the 13 ATP those two posts cost her. A confirmed contribution recovers its cost rather than beating it, **so quality pays for itself**." But 12 of 13 is not paying for itself. It's a loss of 1. The sentence contradicts its own number.

- "Death" is explained three times on this page alone (Step "What triggers death", the panel guide, the "Suspended, not deleted" box). Each is careful. Together they're heavy.

### How It Works (/how-it-works) (38:00)
- Long. Very long. The "No network you can join yet - the present tense below is the model talking" banner is helpful.
- **Count of mechanisms**: Why-Web4 said "four ideas". This page says "five foundational mechanisms", then the diagram section says "**Web4 has four core systems**", then right under it "The **five** systems aren't independent." Four or five?
- **MRH visibility again**: the "How All Five Systems Create Aliveness" box says MRH means "Only see what's relevant to your trust network" and "Without MRH, you can't reach anyone." The card just above says "Everyone stays visible."
- **Spam pricing**: the action list says "Posting content (10-20 ATP)", then "❌ Spam message: Cost 5 ATP". And "Send 20 spam messages = -100 ATP". So spamming is the **cheapest** action on the price list. A spam message costs a quarter to half of an honest post. Combined with the FAQ claim that "spammers' costs are higher", I'm lost. The death spiral box then prices a "low-quality post" at 10 ATP. So spam is 5, a low-quality post is 10, a good post is 10-20?
- **Self-initiated work**: "A post you chose to write: Cost 15 → recharges up to 15 = 0 net at best." Fine, I understand that (recharge refunds, payment earns). But the same page later says "💎 Quality Compounds: **Value creators earn more than they spend.**" Only if someone commissions them, per the page's own rule. And "How someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question." So a newcomer who writes good stuff on their own breaks even at best, forever, until someone hires them?
- **Life 1 arithmetic**: "spent 60 ATP, earned 105 ATP" across "a dozen+ actions", with trust 0.50 → 0.65. Posting costs 10-20 ATP, so 60 ATP buys 3-6 posts, not a dozen+. Meanwhile, a 0.85-quality action moves the composite by about +0.0057 (0.4×0.007 + 0.3×0.0056 + 0.3×0.0042). Climbing +0.15 takes roughly 26 such actions, which would cost 260-520 ATP at posting prices. The numbers don't fit together.
- **Karma: balance or track record?** The Novice box says "Karma is a separate question - it is scored from the track record, **not read off the balance**." The karma box above says "Ended with 145 ATP. Reborn with 145 ATP (karma carried forward)." That's literally reading karma off the balance. The disclaimer explains that the death rule doesn't fit these lives, but not this.
- **Trust "Conservation" invariant**: "Trust can't be created from nothing. It must be earned..." But every newcomer is handed 0.5 on day one, and Alice is "reborn at 0.54". Isn't the 0.5 created from nothing?
- The plagiarism walkthrough was concrete and good. It's the clearest part of governance.
- Agent chains (0.9^5 ≈ 0.59), and the explicit "two different numbers" note vs the MRH 0.7, were helpful. I would have confused them otherwise.

### ATP Economics (/atp-economics) (60:00)
- "Recharge refunds, payment earns" is a good slogan. Once I had it, a lot made sense.
- **Quality ramp table vs its own rule**: the rule is "below 30% zero, between 30-70% linear, above 70% near-full" (the settled-vs-moving box calls this shape canonical). The table for a 50-ATP task: 30% → 0, **50% → ~12, 70% → ~30**, 85% → ~42. If 70% is "near-full", 70% should be ~50, not 30, and 50% (the midpoint of the linear part) should be ~25, not 12. The Sam example (35% → ~6) fits the linear rule; the table doesn't. The sentence under the table also says it "scales linearly **above** 70%", which contradicts "linear in between".
- **Spam price, again**: the simulator buttons say "Send spam message: Cost -5" vs "Low-quality post (your own): Cost -10". Spam is the cheapest button. The site says "Spam becomes expensive." It's expensive in total because there's no refund, but per message it's the cheapest thing you can do.
- **Universal starter grant**: "Every participant starts at the same baseline - trust ≈ 0.5, 100 ATP grant", vs (same page) "issuance is society law... this page cannot tell you the rate." Is 100 universal or a society choice? The "settled vs moving" box files it under 4-Life sim parameters, but the cold-start answer states it as fact.
- The "What that difference looks like from the outside" Hannah/Sam contrast was very helpful.

### Trust Tensor (/trust-tensor) (70:00)
- Talent/Training/Temperament with the surgeon example: clear. The role widget is fun.
- Decay is well explained (Talent never decays, Training 180d, Temperament 30d). I appreciated the honest "can decay alone push you below the survival line? not settled" box.
- "What if you take a 6-month break? ... **A few weeks of consistent activity rebuilds what faded.**" But a max-quality action moves Temperament by at most +0.006 (0.02 × 0.5 × 0.6). Rebuilding Temperament from ~0 to 0.9 would take about 150 perfect actions. With the "tiny by design" steps, "a few weeks" seems wildly optimistic, unless I'm doing hundreds of things a week.
- **The big one for me: software-only ceiling.** "A software-only identity is capped at that same midpoint" (0.50). The "Running" banner on the same page says "hestia evolves real T3/V3 trust tensors". But /hestia says its identity is a **software** LCT, because hardware binding isn't validated. So every real Hestia user today:
  - starts at 0.50,
  - is capped at 0.50,
  - so their trust can only "evolve" **down**,
  - and below 0.50 is "restricted", and staying there is "fatal".
  
  So the only piece badged Running puts every user at the ceiling and the death line at once, with no room above. Is that right? If yes, the site should say so plainly on /hestia, because "trust that evolves" reads like it can go up.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a standard for witnessed, role-scoped trust for people and AI agents
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one substrate, three scales
- [x] What LCT means: device-anchored, witnessed cryptographic identity
- [~] What ATP/ADP means: energy budget plus receipt, I get it; but whether it's scarce/capped, and how spam is priced, are contradicted
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: Talent/Training/Temperament per role
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D (Running Now is excellent)
- [~] Why any of this matters: the problem framing yes; the "spam dies" promise is undercut by spam being the cheapest action

## Unanswered Questions
1. Can a stranger (5 hops out) see my reply or not? The site says both.
2. Is ATP issuance capped (finite pool) or uncapped (society law, "no scarcity cap")?
3. If Hestia identities are software-only and capped at 0.50, can a Hestia user's trust ever go up? Aren't they permanently sitting on the death line?
4. Why is a spam message cheaper than an honest post if the goal is to make spam expensive?
5. How does an agent in energy death (suspended, can't act) earn ATP back through "contribution"?
6. Does the trust-death line move if my society sets its threshold to 0.7 or 0.3?
7. Is karma derived from my final ATP balance or from my track record?
8. Does the lab's "live society" run on the hub software? If so, why isn't the hub badged Running?
9. How does a brand-new person land their first commission, given self-initiated work can only break even? (The site flags this as open, which I respect, but it feels central.)

## Honest Assessment
The site's voice is honest to a fault. Almost every claim carries a caveat, and the Running Now page is the best "what's real" page I've seen for a research project. The problem framing on Why Web4 and the Alice story on First Contact are genuinely good teaching. I came out knowing what LCT, T3 and the four pieces are.

What wore me down was the numbers. I'm a reader who checks arithmetic, and on almost every page that had a worked example, the example didn't match the rule stated next to it: the quality ramp table, Life 1's budget, "12 of 13 pays for itself", spam being the cheapest action, karma "not read off the balance" sitting beside "reborn with the same balance". Each one individually is small. Together they made me trust the numbers less than the caveats, which is backwards for a site about trust.

The two that actually changed my picture of the system:
- **Visibility.** I still don't know if Web4 hides strangers from me or just down-weights them. The concrete example (the one I'd remember) says hidden; the definitions say visible.
- **The Hestia trap.** If I follow the site's own pointers (Hestia = software LCT, software = capped at 0.50, 0.50 = start, below 0.50 = restricted, staying there = fatal), the one piece I can actually install today looks like it can only ever lower my trust. Maybe that's wrong, but nothing on the site tells me why.

The sheer length is the other problem: /why-web4 is over 100k characters of text, /how-it-works ~40k. The caveats are good individually, but they repeat (death is re-explained on at least five pages, with nearly identical paragraphs), and the repetition made the contradictions easier to spot rather than harder.

Would I come back? Probably, to see the hub or hestia mature. Would I tell a friend? I'd send them /tldr, /first-contact and /running-now, and tell them to skip the rest for now.
