## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: "Trust, built into the internet." is a big claim, but the subheadline rescued it immediately: "proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules." That is a concrete problem I recognize. I have read about agents doing things nobody can audit, so I did not bounce.
- The page told me where to start (three pages, part of a five-page path). I appreciated being given an order instead of a wall of nav. The nav itself is large (about 26 links across four groups) and I would have been lost without the suggested path.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes" (/tldr), because it promised the smallest commitment.
- Confusion points: the four cards use words as proper nouns before defining them ("the hub", "hestia", "hardbound"). "Hestia" in particular reads as a product name with no hint of what it is until I hover or click. Minor.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (02:30)
- This is the best page on the site. "Plain English first" is not a boast here, it delivers. The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 triad landed in about fifteen seconds: a platform declares your reputation, a keyholder is assumed to own it, or it accrues from witnessed behavior. That is the whole pitch and I got it.
- The Spec / Reference / Running badge legend is genuinely useful and I found myself relying on it for the rest of the session. Defining "Runnable is not the same as running in production" up front bought a lot of credibility.
- "No timeline commitments. This is research, and research takes as long as it takes." I believed the site more after reading that line.
- Confusion points: "witnessed behavior" raised the obvious chicken-and-egg question (who witnesses the very first person?). The page gestures at day-zero witnesses but did not answer it here. I carried this question for the rest of the session and never found a direct answer, though /what-could-go-wrong eventually admitted witness bootstrapping is unsolved, which is a fair answer.
- Clicked: "Why Web4?" as instructed.

### Why Web4? (07:00)
- Five problems, all recognizable: actions have no cost, reputation is siloed, identity is cheap, AI outpaces verification, platforms hold all recourse. Nothing here required me to know anything.
- Then I hit the FAQ and stopped. **72 questions.** I do not want to read 72 questions on page two of five. The "Most Asked" group of 12 is a good idea and is what I actually read, but the 72 is stated up front and it made the page feel like a reference manual that I was expected to finish.
- I read three: "Is this deployed or theoretical?", "Isn't this a social credit system?", "If not crypto, what backs ATP?". All three were answered well. That is the frustrating part: the content is good and the volume made me not read it.
- Clicked: "First Contact".

### First Contact (14:00)
- The framing is good: "you'll drive Alice's choices", "the rules running here are real; Web4 itself is still active research". I liked being told what I am and am not looking at.
- **I never got to the walkthrough.** Everything is behind a "Start the 5-act Walkthrough" button, and before that button there is a lot: two metrics explained, an optional "why raw?" note, a collapsed four-ideas reference map, and a full five-act preview with the numbers. I read for several minutes on a page that told me it starts from zero and drives itself. By the time I reached "Start" I had already been told the ending.
- **The preview spoils the story.** Act 5 is printed as "dies at ATP 0, trust 0.51 | +12 bonus → 112 reborn at 0.54" before I have driven anything. If the pitch is "you drive it and see consequences", showing me the consequences first removes the reason to drive it.
- **The two-number tutorial contradicts itself.** The panel says "Only two numbers move while she acts. This is all you need." For energy it says: "If it hits 0, she can't act." For trust it says the 0.50 line "becomes fatal". So I concluded: energy is a pause, trust is the fatal one. Then the act preview immediately says Alice **dies** at ATP 0. Which is it, "can't act" or death? I only learned on the next page that these are two named deaths (energy death, recoverable; trust death, permanent). First Contact is where I first meet both and it is the one place that muddles them.
- Then two paragraphs after "only two numbers move", a third number appears: CI, with the formula raw × CI². A squared term, on the page that promised no memorization, with no explanation at the point of appearance of why it is squared. (It is explained well on /coherence-index. I did not know that page existed yet.)
- Confusion points: also unclear why the walkthrough follows a hardware-anchored identity when the page is my first contact. It flags this honestly and links out, but "Alice's climb is not the shape every tier sees" told me the demo I am about to watch is the best case, which slightly undercut it before I started.
- Clicked: "How It Works".

### How It Works (24:00)
- Dense but the best structural page. Birth / Life / Death / Rebirth is a good spine and the concrete ATP costs (reading 1-2, posting 10-20, broadcasting 20-50) made the economy feel real rather than hand-waved.
- The two death conditions are named clearly here and this is where the First Contact confusion resolved.
- The caveat box under the karma examples is outstanding and I want to say so explicitly, because it is the kind of thing sites usually do not do: it notices that none of its own example lives actually died by either rule it just defined, refuses to invent a third cause, and says so. "So rather than invent a third cause to justify these figures, this page declines to name one." That raised my trust in the whole site more than any claim on it did.
- **But the economics gave me a problem the page never addresses.** Self-initiated work "recharges up to 15 ATP = 0 net at best". Commissioned work is where the surplus is (+25, +40). To be commissioned, someone has to trust me. To be trusted, I have to act. My own actions are net-zero at best, and posting costs 10-20 out of a starting 100. So a newcomer gets roughly five to ten self-directed actions, none of which can grow the budget, before running out. **How does anybody bootstrap?** I could not find this answered anywhere.
- The 5-hop agent chain (0.9^5 ≈ 0.59) was a nice concrete demonstration that trust degrades through delegation.
- Clicked: "Running Now".

### What's Actually Running Now (32:00)
- Version numbers, test counts, install commands. This is what "honest" looks like and it is the reason I would come back.
- The line I did not expect: hestia's own limitation, "The witness entry is written by the same policy gate that approves an action, so an action that routes around the gate writes no entry at all." A project publishing the hole in its only running piece is not normal. Noted, believed.
- Confusion: hestia is badged **Running** but the page says "Running on lab machines only, no public network access yet". The badge legend defines Running as "deployed and operational today, in day-to-day use". Whose day-to-day? Four people in a lab is technically day-to-day use, but Running next to Reference reads to me as "one of these you can join". You cannot join any of them.

### Karma & Consequences (38:00)
- I came here from First Contact's "How living and dying work" link, specifically because First Contact told me its 12 was "one modelling choice, not the rule" and I wanted the rule.
- I got a formula: `next_life_atp = base_atp + (karma * karma_multiplier)`, base 100, multiplier 2, with worked results 70 and 120. And then, to the site's credit, the same hedge again: "One model, not the rule."

### ATP/ADP (42:00)
- "Not printed from thin air", "a starter grant from the society's pool". Good, that answered where the 100 comes from, which I had been quietly wondering.
- "Bad actors who never earned that trust? They die for good." That is the sharpest sentence on the site and it made the model click.
- Friction: this page says trust and identity are what persist, and reads as though ATP does not carry across lives. /how-it-works shows lives ending at 145 and being "reborn with 145 ATP (karma carried forward)". /karma-consequences says next life starts at 100 plus karma. All three are individually hedged as unsettled, but a reader crossing pages in nav order (not in reading-path order) sees three different starting balances before ever meeting the hedge that covers them. The hedges are per-page; the confusion is cross-page.

### Trust Tensor, LCT, Coherence Index (46:00)
- T3 as Talent / Training / Temperament, scored per role, with the analyst-versus-mechanic example: this is clear and it is the idea I would repeat to someone else.
- Canonical weights 0.4 / 0.3 / 0.3 stated as fixed by spec. Good, no ambiguity.
- CI² is explained properly on /coherence-index ("inconsistency breaks trust _and_ creates ambiguity about which version of you is acting"), and the "why doesn't a new account start at 1.0" answer is genuinely satisfying. New accounts pay ~38% more per action. Harsh but coherent.
- **The thing that alarmed me**: software-only identity is capped at **0.50**, and 0.50 is *also* the survival line. So this tier lives permanently at its ceiling with zero margin. /lct-explainer answers "am I dead on arrival?" with "trust death isn't a snapshot, it's falling below 0.5 and staying there". But it never says how this specific tier climbs back. Every recovery route the site names elsewhere (device quorum, community vouching by witnesses) it also tells me this tier does not have: "there is no second witness to vouch for you". So the reassurance is offered to the one tier whose recovery mechanism is never stated.

### What Could Go Wrong (51:00)
- The strongest page on the site, and the one that made "maybe" into "yes" for returning. Nine risks with severity and likelihood, including "Nobody Adopts It / Existential / High" as risk number one. Naming your own existential risk first is a real signal.
- Risk 8 confronts the accessibility problem head-on and does not flinch: "A software-only identity starts at 0.50 and 0.50 is also where it stops." And it admits the gap breaks a project headline: software-only users *can* shed their record by starting fresh, so "no fresh starts" does not hold for them.
- One thing I read as a contradiction inside that same section: mitigation (2) says "Community vouching, trusted contacts witness behavior and raise effective trust progressively", and a few sentences later the section says 0.50 is the terminus for this tier and that these users have no hardware witness. Does vouching raise a software-only ceiling or not? Read plainly, those two sentences point opposite ways.
- Risk 9 (the agent routes around its own safety gate) being marked "the one risk here that is measured rather than predicted", with a public issue number, is the most credible thing on the site.

## Unanswered Questions
1. How does a newcomer bootstrap? Self-initiated work is net-zero at best, commissioned work requires trust I do not have yet, and I start with 100 ATP that only goes down.
2. How does a software-only identity recover if its trust dips below 0.50, when 0.50 is also its ceiling and it has no witnesses to vouch for it?
3. Who witnesses the first participants in a brand-new society? /what-could-go-wrong calls witness bootstrapping unsolved, which is honest, but the earlier pages present witnessing as the foundation without flagging that the foundation's first step is open.
4. What is V3, and why is it in the core formula but not in the reading path?
5. If hestia is "Running" but has no public network, what would I actually do today if I wanted to participate? The site tells me what exists but not what my first action would be.
6. Does trust decay if I simply stop acting for a while, or does it only move when I do something?

## Honest Assessment

I came in cold and I left understanding the thing. That is not a low bar and the site clears it. The 2-minute page is genuinely 2 minutes, the Web2/Web3/Web4 contrast does most of the conceptual work in about fifteen seconds, and the Spec/Reference/Running badges gave me a way to hold "this is a research project" and "here are install commands" in my head at the same time without feeling sold to.

What earned the most trust was the site catching its own inconsistencies before I did. The caveat under the karma examples on /how-it-works notices that the illustrated lives do not die by either rule the page just defined, and then refuses to invent a reason. I have never seen a project do that. Same with risk 9, where the one currently-running piece has a documented, reproducible bypass of its own safety gate, with an issue number. A site about trust that publishes the hole in its own trust mechanism is making its argument by demonstration rather than assertion.

The friction is concentrated in two places. First, First Contact, which is the page doing the most work and carrying the most load. It promises "two numbers, that is all you need", then contradicts one of them (energy at 0 is "can't act" in the tutorial and "dies" in the preview four paragraphs later), then adds a third number with a squared exponent, then spoils its own ending before letting me start. Everything in front of the Start button should be shorter and consistent with itself. The walkthrough itself may well be excellent; I could not judge it, because it is entirely behind the button.

Second, the software-only tier. The site is admirably direct that this tier is capped at 0.50, and /what-could-go-wrong risk 8 does not soften it at all ("0.50 is also where it stops", and it concedes the "no fresh starts" headline does not hold for these users). But 0.50 is also the survival line, so this tier permanently sits at its ceiling with zero margin, and the one thing I most wanted to know, what happens when they dip and how they get back, is the one thing no page states. /lct-explainer offers the general reassurance ("recoverable, not a snapshot") to precisely the tier for which it never names a mechanism, while telling me on the same page that this tier has no witnesses. That reads less like dishonesty and more like a gap nobody has walked yet, but it sits under the site's most vulnerable users.

The bootstrap question bothered me more the longer I sat with it. The economics are laid out concretely enough that I could do the arithmetic, and the arithmetic says a newcomer cannot grow their budget alone. That is either an unaddressed design problem or an unwritten paragraph, and I could not tell which from the site. Being able to derive a problem from a site's own numbers is a compliment to how concretely it explains itself, and also a request for the next paragraph.

Would I recommend it? To someone technical, yes, and specifically I would send them to /what-could-go-wrong first, because it is the page that makes the rest believable. To a general-audience friend, not yet. /why-web4 announcing 72 questions on the second page of a five-page path is the moment a curious non-technical reader closes the tab, and that would be a shame, because the twelve questions above the fold are the good ones.
