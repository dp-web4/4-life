## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: clean, confident pitch. "Trust, built into the internet." The problem statement landed immediately: "AI agents already take real actions... Today we cannot prove what an agent did, on whose authority, or by what rules." That is a real problem I recognize.
- The four-piece framing (Core Standard / Hub / Hestia / Hardbound) was visible but meaningless to me at this point — names without stakes.
- Clicked: "Start here: Web4 in 2 minutes" as the page told me to. I appreciated being given an explicit first step instead of a wall of nav.
- Confusion points: the concept nav (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP/ADP) is alphabet soup on first contact, but the page doesn't force it on you.

### /tldr — Web4 in 2 Minutes (03:00)
- Genuinely a good two minutes. The Web2 → Web3 → Web4 contrast ("trust is earned through witnessed behavior, not granted from above") gave me a frame before any acronyms.
- The honesty section ("R&D, not a finished product") bought a lot of goodwill this early.
- Small wobble: the note that unresettable reputation "is a property of hardware anchoring, not of Web4 as such" planted a question I carried all session — so CAN people reset, or not? (It eventually got answered, but only by me assembling pieces across three pages; see Friction.)
- Followed the recommended path to Why Web4.

### /why-web4 (08:00)
- The problem half is the best writing on the site. "You already know this. You feel it every day." Spam, siloed reputation, free restarts for bad actors — all concrete, all recognizable.
- The solution half compresses hard: four mechanisms arrive with acronyms attached, and the causal chain from "problem" to "this specific mechanism fixes it" is asserted more than shown. I trusted it provisionally and moved on.
- The FAQ is enormous (the index says 72 entries across 7 topics). I did not read it linearly — I treated it as a reference shelf, which I think is the intent. The honest tone in the entries I sampled ("This is a genuine tension...") is a credibility signal.
- Density note: this page is a lot. I was glad I'd read the tldr first; cold, this page would have bounced me.

### /first-contact (20:00)
- The setup is excellent: two numbers (Energy starts at 100, Trust starts at 0.50), five acts, and the promise "Spam burns out. Quality earns energy back." I understood the shape of the whole system from this one framing.
- The spoiler arc map confused me on the numbers, though. The death line reads: starts at 0.50 → "dies at ATP 0, trust ↓ 0.51, +12 bonus → 112, reborn at 0.54". Two problems as a naive reader: (a) a down-arrow pointing at 0.51 when she *started* at 0.50 — down from what? The peak she reached in Acts 2–4 is never shown on the rail, so the ↓ looks like it produced an increase; (b) "+12 bonus → 112" switches units mid-line from trust to energy without labels. I worked it out (112 = 100 + 12 ATP), but I had to stop and decode.
- The karma footnote also stopped me: "the karma page models it as falling at each rebirth, which is not what happens here. Read both lines for the shape... not the amount." So two pages on the same site model karma in opposite directions, and I'm told to trust the shape but not the numbers of either. Honest, but it left me unsure what karma actually does.
- The 5-act walkthrough itself is behind a Start button and step gates; I read the framing and arc map rather than clicking through every act.

### /how-it-works (32:00)
- The mechanical spine of the site, and mostly it delivered. Costs (posting 10–20 ATP), the quality ramp (below ~30% earns ~zero, above ~70% earns ~full, ~7× gap), trust deltas (0.02 × (quality − 0.5)), the two deaths (energy death at ATP 0, recoverable; trust death = "raw trust falls below 0.5 and stays there", permanent). This is where the acronyms finally became machinery.
- The Complete Example (three lives) puzzled me at first — Life 1 "Ended with 145 ATP", healthy trust, so *why did it end?* — but the page catches its own problem: "That is why they read ended and not died: neither death this page defines fits them. What else ends a life is not settled." I respect the honesty; I still left not knowing what ends a normal, well-behaved life.
- One line I flagged: actions outside your MRH don't cost energy. My immediate naive reaction was "so spam outside my neighborhood is free?" I assume it means such actions don't *register* at all, but the sentence as read invited the wrong inference.
- Coherence Index (CI) and "raw vs effective trust" (effective = raw × CI²) appear here for the first time with little warm-up.

### /running-now (45:00)
- The best page on the site for trust in the *authors*. The Spec / Reference / Running badge definitions are crisp, and "The badge says how far a piece has travelled, not how good it is" is exactly the sentence I needed.
- Hestia "Running" = running on the lab's own machines, stated plainly. The disclosed audit gap (an action that routes around the policy gate writes no witness entry at all) is the kind of admission that made me believe the rest of the page.
- After this page I understood the real/aspirational boundary clearly. No complaints.

### /onramp (52:00)
- "One substrate, three scales" finally made the four-piece story click — they're not four products, they're one shape at personal/community/enterprise scale.
- The hub↔hardbound missing seam (communities can admit hardware-bound members but can't currently *verify* the binding) took two reads but rewarded them.
- Small snag: "R6/R7" appears here without introduction or an inline link. Every other acronym had been set up by this point in my path; this one hadn't.

### /atp-economics (58:00)
- The Sam/Hannah worked example (both spend 50; Sam at 35% quality earns ~6 back, Hannah at 85% earns ~42) is the single clearest illustration on the site of why quality pays.
- The refund-vs-payment distinction is load-bearing and I almost missed it: confirmations of your own work refund *at most what you spent* (recharge = min(Σ, ATP_cost)), while commissioned work pays what it's worth to the buyer. That one distinction is the answer to "how does anyone ever net positive?" — it deserves more visual weight than it gets.
- Open thread I couldn't close: unconfirmed receipts (ADP) "age out over a window of weeks" — and then what? Is the ATP I spent just gone? Nothing on the page says.

### /trust-tensor (64:00)
- Talent/Training/Temperament with 0.4/0.3/0.3 weights, per-role scores, different decay half-lives (Temperament fades in ~30 days, Talent never decays from absence) — coherent and surprisingly humane design.
- The sentence that sent me to the LCT page: "A software-only identity is capped at that same midpoint." Capped at 0.5 — the *starting* value?

### /day-in-web4 (68:00)
- The mockups did more for my intuition than any prose page: mail that costs the sender 2 ATP (8 for a stranger), a marketplace listing with seller trust 0.91, distance-adjusted trust 0.64. Recommended.
- Honest framing everywhere: "none of it ships today."

### /lct-explainer + /what-could-go-wrong (71:00)
- LCT page confirmed it: "With software-only hardware, 0.5 is both where you start and the highest you can reach." Hardware tiers (TPM 0.90, Secure Enclave 0.85, FIDO2 0.75, software 0.50), plus device-count caps (a lone TPM laptop caps at 0.75 until two more witnesses unlock 0.90). The page even anticipates my "Am I dead on arrival?" question. But it opened a sharper one it doesn't answer — see Friction #3.
- What Could Go Wrong is the page that made me trust the project most. Nine failure modes, likelihoods attached, and repeated refusal to claim more than "designed, not validated." "Web4 raises the floor, not the ceiling. Casual abuse becomes uneconomical. Sophisticated, well-funded attacks remain possible" is the most honest sentence I've read on a project site.

## Unanswered Questions
1. What ends an ordinary, well-behaved life? The site says ATP-zero stops you and sustained trust collapse is permanent, names no natural end, and then shows three example lives ending anyway. (Disclosed as "not settled" — but it's still the question I left with.)
2. Can a software-only identity that dips below 0.5 actually get back to exactly 0.50 and survive there, or is one stumble effectively permanent at tier 0?
3. When an unconfirmed ADP receipt ages out after weeks, is the spent ATP simply lost?
4. Does karma grow or shrink across successive rebirths? The two pages that model it disagree and say so.
5. Who runs the law oracle in the SAL governance story — humans, code, or both?

## Honest Assessment
This is the most honest project site I can remember reading. The thing that kept me on it for over an hour was not the mechanism design — it was the site's habit of pre-empting my objections and volunteering its own weaknesses ("runnable is not the same as running", "designed but not tested", the disclosed Hestia audit gap, the three-life example that annotates its own inconsistency). That earned enough trust that I read the dense parts charitably.

The recommended 5-page path works. Read in order, each page answered questions the previous one raised, and by /running-now I could hold the whole system in my head: identity anchored in hardware, actions cost energy, quality earns it back, trust is per-role and earned, death is survivable if you built trust.

Where the site still loses me is at the seams between pages. Every individual page is careful, but three of my four medium frictions are cross-page: the karma models that disagree, the fresh-start defense that exists only if you assemble sentences from three different pages, and the software-cap-vs-survival-line arithmetic that no single page confronts. The parts are honest; the joins are where a naive reader falls through.

Would I recommend it? To someone who likes reading specs and appreciates epistemic honesty, enthusiastically. To a general reader, cautiously — the pitch pages are ready, but they should stop at /first-contact unless they want homework.
