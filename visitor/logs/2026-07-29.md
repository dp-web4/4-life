## Honest Assessment

This site is good, and it is good in the specific way that is hardest to fake: it repeatedly told me things that made its own project look less finished. "Not production-ready." "Recovery from total device loss is an unsolved problem." "Appeals mechanism designed but untested with real humans." "This is the #1 existential risk." I have read a lot of project sites and almost none of them do this. It bought a lot of credibility, and it should keep doing it.

The teaching also works. I arrived knowing nothing and I left able to explain, unprompted, what an LCT is, why trust has three dimensions instead of one, why ATP is modeled on cell metabolism, and what the Markov part of MRH means. The pages that explain their own jargon at the moment it appears (tensor, witness, Markov, MCP, RDF) are the pages I got the most out of. The Web2/Web3/Web4 three-panel on /tldr and the surgeon-versus-mechanic example on /trust-tensor did more work than all the diagrams combined.

What I want to flag is a pattern rather than a list of bugs. Most of my friction this session came from **one page making a clean, quotable, absolute claim, and another page quietly not honoring it.** "No market, no price" and then a buy button. "Bad behavior follows you, no fresh starts" and then "you start over from zero with a fresh identity." "Recovery uses your other devices" and "recovery is an unsolved problem" and "recovery takes 3-7 days via community vouching," all true somewhere, none true everywhere. Individually each page is well written. Read in sequence, which is what the reading path asks me to do, they undercut each other.

The absolutes are the risk. This site's honesty muscle is very strong on *maturity* ("not built yet", "not validated", "not tested with humans") and comparatively weak on *mechanism* ("no market", "no fresh starts", "no moderators needed"). The maturity claims are all hedged beautifully. The mechanism claims are stated flat, and the flat ones are the ones that broke. My guess is that the mechanism claims feel safe because they are design intent, and the exceptions live on other pages written at other times. But a reader does not experience them as separate pages. They experience one voice, and when that voice says "no market" and then shows a market, the cost is not confusion about ATP. The cost is that I start reading the other absolutes as marketing.

The other thing I would change: the software-only tier is where three of my findings converge, and it is the tier the site points poor and excluded users toward. Its ceiling equals the survival line, its device loss is unrecoverable, and its unrecoverability is also a hole in the site's central anti-Sybil promise. Each fact is stated somewhere. None of them are stated together, and the equity FAQ on /why-web4, which is where someone worried about affordability will actually read, says "$50 phone" and does not mention any of it. That is the one place I felt the site was not being as straight with me as it was everywhere else, and I do not think it is deliberate. I think it is three pages that were each honest on their own.

Fix the absolutes, and give the software-only tier one honest paragraph in one place, and this is a genuinely excellent explainer for something this abstract. I would come back. I would send it to a friend. I would send it with a note saying "the ATP page and the day-in-the-life page disagree, ask them about it."
