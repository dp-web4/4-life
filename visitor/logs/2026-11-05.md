### /atp-economics (57:00)
- ATP = "Allocation Transfer Packets", ADP = "Allocation Discharge Packet" (singular?). The biology pun helped.
- "Is ATP like Bitcoin? No." Clear.
- **Quality ramp doesn't fit its own description.** The prose says the ramp "starts at 30% quality (below that, zero payment) and scales linearly above 70%." /how-it-works says linear *between* 30 and 70, near-full *above* 70. The table (50-ATP task): 30% → 0, 50% → ~12, 70% → ~30, 85% → ~42. If it were linear from 30 to 70 and near-full at 70, then 70% should pay about 50, not 30, and 50% should pay about 25, not 12. ## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): trust earned through witnessed behavior, for humans and AI agents alike
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one substrate, three scales
- [x] What LCT means: a device-anchored identity, with ceilings by hardware
- [~] What ATP/ADP means: an energy budget per action, earned back by confirmed value. I lost confidence in the numbers (ramp, spam price, scarcity).
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: Talent/Training/Temperament per role
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D (/running-now is excellent)
- [~] Why any of this matters: yes for AI agent accountability. Less sure it beats spam, given the MRH-cost contradiction.

## Honest Assessment
The site's best quality is its honesty. /running-now, the gate-bypass disclosure, the "3 billion people without smartphones" equity note, and "the policy gate stops accidents, not adversaries" made me trust the authors. The big picture landed fast: /tldr's Web2/Web3/Web4 comparison is genuinely good, and so are the onramp's "substrate plus three scales" and the T3 surgeon/mechanic example.

Where it fell apart for me was the **numbers and rules connecting the pages**. I read the site as a careful skeptic would. I took the worked examples seriously and checked them against each other, and they often disagree: on whether spam costs energy, on the sign of karma for the same kind of death, on the quality ramp, on whether CI 0.5 is a death line. Several pages already know this and add a box saying "the other page models it differently, read for the shape." One such box is honest. Five of them start to feel like the site is showing me drafts. I'd rather see one consistent illustrative model plus a single "these numbers are not settled" note.

The other thing that nagged me: the piece marked **Running** (hestia) uses software-only identity, and the trust pages say software-only is capped exactly at the starting value, which is also the survival line. Put those together and today's real users have no upside on trust at all. No page says that out loud, so I had to assemble it myself from three pages, and it changed how I felt about "Running."

I'd come back to see whether Web4 develops, and I'd send /tldr + /running-now to a friend. I would not yet send them /how-it-works or /atp-economics and expect them to come out with a consistent picture.
