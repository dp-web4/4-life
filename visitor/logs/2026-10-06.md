But the canonical rule stated a few paragraphs earlier (and on /how-it-works) moves **all three** dimensions on every action, in fixed proportions, with **Talent moving most (×1.0) and Temperament least (×0.6)**. Under that rule an ethics violation hits Talent *harder* than Temperament. The page sees this coming ("Can decay alone push you below the survival line?... not settled") and I respect that. But "A few weeks of consistent activity rebuilds what faded" doesn't square with Temperament gaining ≤0.006 per action: rebuilding ~0.9 of Temperament takes ~150 perfect actions.
- **"A software-only identity is capped at that same midpoint" (0.50).** Combined with "0.5 = default start" and "0.5 = survival line", a software-only identity starts *on* the death line and can never rise above it.

## Honest Assessment
This is the most honest technical site I've seen in a while. /running-now and /what-could-go-wrong (especially Risk 9, where the team publishes how its own Running product can be bypassed) made me trust the people behind it. The problem framing on the landing page and /tldr is excellent. I knew what this was for within two minutes.

Where it lost me is the numbers. The site invites me to do the arithmetic ("if you can do the arithmetic above, you can reach that question yourself"), so I did, and several worked examples don't satisfy their own stated rules: the quality ramp table vs its "settled" shape, Life 1's action count, First Contact's trust steps, and the karma sign. Many of these are disclaimed as "illustrative" or "not settled", but the disclaimers cover *amounts*, while several of the conflicts are about *direction* or *mechanism*: free vs expensive, visible vs hidden, capped vs uncapped, penalty vs bonus, can-kill vs can't-kill.

The thread that bothered me most as a would-be user: the only thing I can actually run is hestia; hestia is software-only today; software-only trust starts and stops at 0.50; and 0.50 is the death line. The site states every piece of that, on four different pages, but never puts them together, and the hestia page still sells "trust that evolves". If I'm the "run AI agents on my own machine" reader that /running-now points to hestia, that conjunction is the first thing I'd want spelled out.

Biggest strength: candour about maturity. Biggest weakness: worked examples that contradict each other on mechanism, not just on amounts.
