## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed open standard where trust is earned from witnessed behavior instead of declared by a platform or assumed from a key. The site taught this, mostly on /tldr.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one substrate, three scales (person, community, enterprise). Taught. The word "onramp" itself stayed slippery.
- [x] What LCT means: Linked Context Token, an identity anchored to your device (ideally its security chip). Taught. Why it is called "linked context" I inferred nothing; nobody said.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means: an energy budget you spend to act, and the receipt that spending leaves, which converts back when others confirm value. Taught, but only /atp-economics made the "refund vs payment" rule clear.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three scores (Talent, Training, Temperament) per role. Taught well.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: taught very well on /running-now.
- [ ] Why any of this matters: half. I believe the AI-agent accountability problem. I am not convinced the spam / reputation half works, because the numbers that are supposed to show it do not agree with each other.

## Honest Assessment
There are two sites here and they have different levels of credibility.

The first site is /tldr, /running-now, the four product pages and the status boxes. It is careful, specific and almost aggressively honest. It tells me the only deployment is four of the lab's own machines, that the gate can be bypassed with two environment variables, that hardware binding runs against mocks. I came away trusting the people who wrote it. If someone asked me "is this vaporware?", I could answer with facts from that page, and the answer is "no, but it is early, and they say so".

The second site is the society model: lives, deaths, karma, ATP prices, quality ramps. It is vivid and I enjoyed Alice's story, but nearly every number I tried to carry from one page to the next broke. Karma is +12 or -15 for the same death. The ramp table does not follow the ramp. There are five systems, or four, or three. MRH hides things or it does not. Good posts earn more than they cost or they never do. The site frequently notices this itself and adds a paragraph saying "this is one modelling choice, not the rule", and by the fourth such paragraph I stopped treating any figure as information. The caveats are honest, but honesty about a contradiction is not the same as resolving it, and they make already-long pages longer.

The thing that bothered me most is where the two sites meet. The model says trust is earned upward through behavior. The running software is software-only, and software-only is capped at exactly the starting value. So the one place where the model is real is the one place where its central promise (earn trust by behaving well) cannot happen. The pages each say their half accurately. No page says the two halves together.

Would I come back? If I ran AI agents on my own machine, yes: /hestia's "a day with" section made me want to try it, caveats included. As a curious non-builder, I got the idea in the first fifteen minutes and the next hour mostly lowered my confidence rather than raising it. I would tell a friend to read /tldr and /running-now and stop.
