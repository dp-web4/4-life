## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: The headline "Trust, built into the internet." and the subhead "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules" are the clearest thing on the whole site. I understood the problem in ten seconds.
- The one-sentence definition of "witness" ("observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation on the record") is good, but I immediately wondered: *who* are the witnesses? Random people? Companies? The page doesn't say.
- The "Where this actually stands" honesty box is reassuring: R&D, not a product.
- Then a wall of acronyms (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, Karma, Coherence Index) and Greek-goddess product names. The page told me to read in order: 2-minute intro, Why Web4, First Contact. I did that.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".

### /tldr (02:00)
- Good. "Plain English first" is a promise it mostly keeps. "Good behavior compounds. Bad behavior follows you." stuck with me.
- The one-sentence version at the bottom is a nice summary.
- Small snag: "pilot-ready" used without explanation.
- Hestia is called "Running". I noted it and moved on.
- Clicked: "Read the site in order" → Why Web4.

### /why-web4 (06:00)
- The problem framing (spam, siloed reputation, fresh-start trolls, AI agents, platforms with no recourse) is relatable. "Why previous solutions failed" was useful: I could see why CAPTCHAs and "Sign in with Google" don't fix this.
- The "four ideas" cards (Energy Budget, Device-Anchored Identity, Multi-Dimensional Trust, Trust Neighborhood) were the moment it started to click.
- Then the page just keeps going. The FAQ has **72 questions**. I scrolled the topic index and felt exhausted. I read maybe six answers.
- Confusions I wrote down while reading:
  - Item 2 of "What Would Actually Work" says "Earning trust should require physical hardware, not just an email address" and in the same breath "Anyone can take part with a software-only identity." So which is it? Is hardware required or not?
  - The software-only ceiling is 0.50 and the default/starting trust is also 0.50 and 0.50 is "the survival line." So a phone-less, chip-less person starts *at* their ceiling, *on* the death line, with "a reputation that cannot climb" (the page admits this). That's the scariest sentence on the site for me and it's buried in the FAQ.
  - The "four mechanisms in one reply" example says the reply cost "fractions of a cent of energy". A cent? Then a few screens later ATP is "not money, you can't sell it for dollars". Why is it priced in cents?
  - Tier numbering: the adoption ladder is numbered 1 to 5, but an FAQ answer says "Tiers 0-3 are reversible... Only Tier 4 (full native) is permanent." Is there a Tier 0? Is Tier 5 gone?
  - One FAQ says everyone can see messages, "nothing is hidden"; another says "a spammer with no trust connections can't even see your data". Probably messages vs data, but I couldn't tell.
- Clicked: "Walk through with Alice (7 min)" → First Contact.

### /first-contact (20:00)
- The five-act story (Join, Build, Stumble, Recover, Die & Reborn) is the best teaching device on the site. Being told "100 energy, 0.50 trust, below 0.50 is trouble" and then watching Alice is concrete.
- Confusions:
  - "Trust death, permanent" is defined at the top, then Alice's Act 5 death is "Suspended, not deleted" and she's reborn. She died of energy, not trust, I think, but her trust also dropped to 0.51 that act. I wasn't sure which death I was looking at, or whether 0.51 was close enough to be scary.
  - "The rules running here are real" sits next to "How much karma converts is not settled, and 12 is one modelling choice, not the rule." So are the rules real or modelling choices? I'd like a little marker saying which numbers are protocol and which are placeholders.
  - Act 3 says spam cost −25 ATP but Act 2 doesn't say what her balance was, so I couldn't follow the energy line the way I could follow trust.
  - "raw × CI²" appears in an optional box. I skipped it.
- No "next page" at the bottom of the walkthrough. I went back to the sidebar.

### /how-it-works (34:00)
- Long. The Birth → Life → Death → Rebirth structure helps.
- I liked the price list: read ~1 ATP, post 10–20, broadcast 20–50. Finally real numbers.
- Confusions:
  - Key Takeaways says "Five systems" (Identity, ATP, Trust, Context, Coherence). Later, "How the Pieces Fit Together" says "Web4 has four core systems" and the diagram drops MRH/Context. Four or five?
  - The example lives end at 145, 130 and 165 ATP and the "karma" carried forward equals that ending balance every time. But the same page says karma "is scored from the track record, not read off the balance." And First Contact gave Alice a +12 karma bonus on top of 100, which is a completely different shape from "you keep your 145." I came out of two pages with two incompatible pictures of what karma is.
  - Life 1 Novice: trust went 0.50 → 0.65 from "a dozen+ actions". I tried the page's own formula (0.02 × (quality − 0.5), scaled by dimension and weighted 0.4/0.3/0.3). A good action (quality 0.85) moves composite trust by about 0.006. Fifteen hundredths would take ~25 such actions, not a dozen. Even perfect-quality actions max out near 0.008 each. Maybe I did it wrong, but the page gave me the formula and invited me to check.
  - Spam is "cost 5" in one example and the Death Spiral example says "Spend 10 ATP per low-quality post." Fine, spam and low-quality posts might be different things, but see /atp-economics below.
  - LCT card says "Unforgeable identity rooted in hardware" but Birth lists "VM-bound: software identity for AI agents."
  - Top callout says the model was "validated through simulations"; later a section says "not a measured result."

The worked example shows ~12 at 50% and ~30 at 70%, and ~42 at 85%. Only the 35% row (~6) fits the rule. The "~7x" headline (42/6) depends on numbers the rule doesn't produce.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — a proposed standard where trust is earned by witnessed behavior, aimed at AI agents and people
- [~] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — I get "one standard, three scales (personal, community, enterprise)". The seams section lost me.
- [x] What LCT means — an identity key that lives on your device, ceiling depends on the hardware
- [~] What ATP/ADP means — an energy budget for actions; I never really learned what ADP is beyond "spent ATP", and the spam/post prices confused me
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — three role-specific numbers (Talent/Training/Temperament), weighted 0.4/0.3/0.3
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — /running-now did this well; but I could only *run* things as a developer
- [~] Why any of this matters — the AI-agent accountability problem is compelling; less sure Web4 fixes spam given its own price list

## Unanswered Questions

2. Is karma a bonus on top of a fresh start, or your old balance carried over?
3. If I'm software-only and start on the survival line with a ceiling at that same line, is there any way for me to safely make a mistake?
4. Can a long break (Training and Temperament decaying) push me below 0.50 and kill me? The page says "not settled" — is anyone working on it?
5. Why is spam priced cheaper than a post?
6. Can I, a non-coder, do anything today besides read?
7. Is the lab's hub that "we run one" refers to joinable by anyone, ever? Is there a waitlist?
8. What is ADP as its own thing?

## Honest Assessment
The site is unusually honest. Almost every page tells me what's not real yet, and /running-now is a model of how to answer "is this vaporware?" I trust the authors more because of it. The landing headline and the /tldr page are genuinely good; I got the core idea (earned, witnessed trust, especially for AI agents) in under five minutes.

Where it lost me was the numbers. The site invites me to check its arithmetic (formulas, worked examples, simulators) and when I did, several didn't line up: spam is cheaper than posting, the quality ramp's table doesn't follow its rule, a dozen actions can't produce the promised trust gain, and karma means two different things on two pages I read back-to-back. Each one is small, but together they made me feel the numbers were decorative rather than load-bearing, which undercuts a site whose whole pitch is "verifiable." If you show me a formula, I will plug numbers into it.

The second issue is audience. Every "how to touch it" path goes through a terminal. That's fine for developers, but the landing page talks to everyone ("Trust, built into the internet") and then has nothing for a non-developer to do. One honest sentence saying "if you don't code, there's nothing to install yet, here's what to watch for" would make me feel addressed.

Third, length. /why-web4 alone is enormous, with a 72-question FAQ, and some of the most important admissions (software-only users start on the survival line and can't climb; the hub can't verify hardware claims) live deep in FAQs or "seams" sections where most visitors won't reach them.

I'd come back to see whether a public hub opens up. I'd send it to a developer friend building AI agents. I wouldn't send it to my parents yet.
