## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed standard where trust comes from other people signing records of what you did, instead of a platform's say-so or a key.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one library set, three scales (me, my group, my company). The clearest structural idea on the site.
- [ ] What LCT means: I know it is a key on my device that signs my actions, and that a chip makes it count for more. I do not understand the ceilings well enough to say what my own would be.
- [ ] What ATP/ADP means: a per-person energy budget, spent to act, refunded if people confirm my work, paid above cost if someone commissioned it. I got that. The ramp numbers undid some of it.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three scores per role, blended 0.4 / 0.3 / 0.3. The Alice-in-three-roles example did it.
- [ ] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: Running Now is excellent, but the hub page's "we run one" put a crack in it.
- [x] Why any of this matters: the five problems on Why Web4 are the best argument on the site.

## Honest Assessment
This is the most self-critical explainer site I have ever read, and that is both its strength and its problem.

The strength: I never once felt sold to. Every page told me what does not work yet, often before telling me what does. The problem statement on Why Web4 is sharp, the reader-row navigation on Running Now is thoughtful, and "A day with Hestia" made a piece of software I have never seen feel concrete. The trust arithmetic, where I checked it, was right. That is rare.

The problem: the site's honesty is mostly *about* its numbers, and the numbers still disagree with each other. Three pages give three rebirth models and each page points at the other two. A page names a CI threshold and then denies it. The visibility model flips between "everyone sees everything" and "strangers never see it" within one scroll. The lifecycle example uses a kind of death the page says it does not have. Each of these carries a careful caveat saying "read this for the shape, not the amount," but in most cases the disagreement *is* the shape: up versus down, visible versus invisible, penalty versus bonus.

By the end I could explain the pitch to someone else and I could explain the architecture. I could not tell them, with confidence, what a stranger sees, what happens when I run out of energy, or what the software I could actually install today would let my reputation do. The site knows every one of these is open. What it has not done is pick one page to be the page where the open thing is stated once, and make the other pages defer to it instead of to each other.

Would I come back? Maybe, if the four HIGH rows get resolved, because the underlying idea held my attention for nearly two hours, and the honesty made me want to root for it.
