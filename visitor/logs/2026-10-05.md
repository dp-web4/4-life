## Honest Assessment
This is an unusually honest site. Almost every page says what is real and what isn't, and several pages flag their own open questions ("not settled", "one modelling choice, not the rule", "this page will not invent a rule"). The /tldr, /running-now and /onramp pages gave me a solid picture of the project in about 15 minutes, and the problem statement on the landing page is sharp.

The trouble starts when I take the numbers seriously. The site invites that (it shows formulas and worked arithmetic everywhere) and then the numbers don't survive being combined across pages. Each page is internally careful, but the same concept is stated differently on different pages: visibility (everyone sees vs only your neighborhood), out-of-neighborhood cost (free vs still costs), CI 0.5 (alive/dead vs cost only), karma for energy death (+12 vs −15), ATP supply (capped vs uncapped), composite weights (role-weighted vs fixed). Many pages add a paragraph explaining that another page models it differently, and those paragraphs pile up: by the Karma page I was reading more about which example disagrees with which than about karma itself.

The one that bothers me most as someone who might actually try this: the only piece I can run today, hestia, gives me a software identity whose trust ceiling equals its starting point equals the death line. The site is honest about each of those three facts on different pages, but nowhere puts them in one sentence and tells me what that means for me day to day.

I'd come back to see if the contradictions get resolved. I'd send /tldr to a friend. I wouldn't send /how-it-works or /why-web4 to anyone who isn't ready to spend an hour.
