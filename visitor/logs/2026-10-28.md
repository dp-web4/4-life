## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: clean, one sentence up top ("A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules"). I understood the problem statement immediately. "Trust, built into the internet" is the kind of tagline I distrust, but the next paragraph earned it back by being concrete (code, money, decisions).
- Four cards for "the onramp", six vocabulary buttons, a maturity paragraph that says plainly "Hardbound... hardware binding is not yet validated on real devices". That honesty is what kept me reading.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes", as the page told me to.
- Confusion points: "To witness is to observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation on the record." Staking how? What does a witness lose if the record is wrong? That question was never answered anywhere in 22 pages.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (02:30)
- Genuinely good. It says what the site is versus what Web4 is, defines every acronym on first use, and the Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast is the single clearest thing on the site.
- The three badges (Spec / Reference / Running) are explained here, with the honest note that "Running" means the builders' own daily use, not adoption.
- Confusion: "with software alone it is a key on your machine, which starts you the same way and carries less weight." The Web3 row two lines up says "whoever holds the key is assumed to be the rightful owner" is the *problem*. So software-only Web4 is... a key on a machine. The page flags the difference as "carries less weight" and links away. I noted the question and moved on.
- Clicked: "Why Web4?" (step 2 of the path).

### Why Web4? (06:00)
- The problems section is persuasive and I did not need any vocabulary for it. "Spam, bots, silos, fresh starts, platforms" is exactly my experience.
- The four idea cards are good. Each one ends with a "Shorthand:" line that gives the acronym after the plain-English version. That order is right.
- Then the "All four, in one moment" box undid the MRH card for me. The card says "Everyone stays visible - what changes is weight." The box, eleven lines later, says "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply at all - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread." Those are opposite claims about the same mechanism on the same screen. The FAQ further down ("Everyone can see them - nothing is hidden or censored") sides with the card. So the worked example is the odd one out, and it is the one a skimmer reads.
- "Four mechanisms, one reply, fractions of a cent of energy" — fractions of a cent? Twenty minutes later the FAQ told me ATP "can't be sold for dollars" and has "no price against money". So what does "fractions of a cent" mean?
- "Coherence Index (CI): not a fifth mechanism - it's a consistency check on the four above." I filed that sentence away. It mattered later.
- The FAQ is enormous (72 questions). I read the "Most asked" dozen and five more. The deployed-or-theoretical answer is excellent and I believed it. "Isn't this a social credit system?" is the best single answer on the site.
- One FAQ answer stopped me cold: "Right now, Web4 exists as a research prototype - the site you're on." The 2-minute page had gone out of its way to tell me this site is NOT Web4, just the explainer. Here the FAQ says the site is the prototype. Which?
- The 0.5-threshold answer says the T3 blend weights (0.4 / 0.3 / 0.3) are "protocol-level" and "no society can vote itself different weights." Fine, but the same answer says 0.5 itself is "a society-level parameter". So the line is society-set but the number compared against it is protocol-fixed. I think I follow, but it took two reads.

## Unanswered Questions

2. If I install hestia today, my software LCT is capped at 0.50 and starts at 0.50. Can my trust go anywhere but down? What does the Running banner's "evolves" mean for me?
3. Is the lab's "live Web4 society" a running hub? If so, why is the hub Reference and not Running?
4. Is a law oracle software, a person, or a role?
5. Can I see people outside my 3-hop neighborhood or not? (Three pages, both answers.)
6. Is ATP issuance capped or uncapped?
7. Does six months away from a hardware-anchored identity cross the survival line and stay there?
8. What does a witness actually stake, and what happens to a witness whose attestation turns out false?
9. "Fractions of a cent of energy" — if ATP has no price in money, what is the cent?
10. The 1%-adoption FAQ shows "0.82 trust in Python" through a browser extension. Isn't a browser extension software-only, and therefore capped at 0.50?

## Honest Assessment

The honesty is the product. Every page says what is real and what is not, the badges mean what they say, Running Now is the clearest maturity page I have read for any project, and risk 9 on What Could Go Wrong (the one Running piece has a gate you can step around with two environment variables, and they measured it themselves) is the kind of admission that made me believe the rest. I did not once feel sold to.

What wore me down was a different thing: the site has started to use honesty as a substitute for deciding. "How much karma carries is not settled; this page shows one model, First Contact shows another, read both for the shape." "The trust line is open in the same way." "What else ends a life is not settled, so this page declines to name one." "Whether a decayed score is read against this rule is not settled; the question is filed." I counted at least six places where a page tells me that it and another page disagree, and then leaves both standing. One of those is admirable. Six is a pattern, and the pattern reads as: the illustrations were drawn before the rules were, and nobody has gone back to redraw them.

The second thing is numbers. The site is full of them, precise ones (0.007, 0.49, 1.38×, 2,627 tests), and most of the ones I could check closed. But the ones that did not close are not obscure: how many mechanisms there are (three, four, five), how many risks (seven, nine), whether ATP is capped, what a post costs, what the quality ramp pays, and which way the karma sign goes when you run out of energy. Those are the numbers a reader meets first. A site that asks me to trust witnessed records should not let its own worked examples disagree with its own rules.

The third thing is smaller but real: the same disclaimer sentence pasted on five pages, the same "about these numbers" paragraph pasted twice on one page, and the sense that pages are edited one at a time. The pointer on the coherence page to a surcharge that lives on a different page, the glossary calling 0.7 canonical while its own page calls it a teaching number, and VCM expanding two ways are all the same shape of defect.

Would I come back? Maybe. I would come back to Running Now and the 2-minute page without hesitation. I would send a friend the problems half of Why Web4. I would not send anyone the How It Works page until it knows how many systems it is describing, and I would not tell anyone to install hestia until the site can say in one sentence what happens to a trust score that starts at its own ceiling.
