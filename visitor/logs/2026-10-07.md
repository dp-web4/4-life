## Honest Assessment
This is one of the more honest technical sites I've read. Almost every bold claim comes with a caveat about what it covers, a status badge, or a pointer to the risk page, and /running-now's "find the row that sounds like you" is great. I came away with a real model of what Web4 is trying to do.

The friction now is not jargon. It's **arithmetic and consistency**. The site keeps inviting me to check its numbers ("if you can do the arithmetic above..."), and when I do, several of them don't close: the Novice's trust climb, Alice's karma sign, a 6-month break landing below the death line under a reassuring sentence. Two plain-language facts are stated in opposite directions on pages I read back to back: who sees a stranger's message, and whether out-of-neighborhood actions cost energy. Each one alone is small. Together they make me wonder which page to believe, which is a bad feeling on a site whose whole pitch is verifiable trust.

The deepest one is structural. The only piece badged "Running" is software-only, and software-only trust is capped at exactly where it starts. Every number I watched climb (0.55, 0.62, 0.65, 0.85) belongs to a hardware-anchored identity that no shipping piece supports yet. The site does admit this, but I had to put it together across /hestia, /lct-explainer and /running-now. A first-time visitor who only reads Hestia's feature list will believe their trust will grow.

The caveats are also starting to pile up. Several paragraphs (the "About these numbers" box on /how-it-works, the hub/hardbound edge on /onramp, the two-deaths explainer) spend more words on what is not settled than on what is. I respect it, but I skimmed them, so the honesty was there and I still missed it.
