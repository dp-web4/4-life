## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- **First impression**: Strong. "Trust, built into the internet." then immediately a concrete problem: agents write code and move money and we can't prove what they did. I knew what the site was about in about fifteen seconds, which is rare.
- I liked that "witness" was defined inline the first time it appeared ("to observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation on the record"). That one sentence did a lot of work.
- **Clicked**: "Web4 in 2 minutes" — the site explicitly told me to read in that order, so I did.
- **Confusion points**: Minor. The four onramp cards are good but the names are a mixed bag: "The Core Standard" and "The Hub" are descriptive, "Hestia" and "Hardbound" are brand names I have to learn. The cards do gloss both ("Named for the Greek goddess of the hearth", "As in hardware-bound") which helped more than I expected.

### /tldr — "Web4 in 2 Minutes" (02:30)
- This is not two minutes. I counted roughly 1,150 words of body text and it introduced LCT-adjacent ideas, three maturity badges, four products, and a Web2/Web3/Web4 comparison. It took me closer to seven.
- **What worked, and worked well**: the Web2 / Web3 / Web4 triptych. "A platform declares your reputation" / "Whoever holds the key is assumed to be the rightful owner" / "Trust accrues from witnessed behavior." That is the clearest thing on the site. I understood the whole pitch from those three lines.
- **What worked**: "Good behavior compounds. Bad behavior follows you." Six words, whole thesis.
- **Confusion point**: the "Four Pieces" section spends three paragraphs explaining the *badge system* (Spec / Reference / Running, what they mean, that a piece can carry more than one, that the badge measures distance not quality) *before* telling me what the four pieces are. I came here to learn what the pieces are. I got a lesson in the site's own taxonomy first.
- **Small inconsistency I noticed while scanning**: three of the four cards carry a one-line status caveat under the badge ("Stable; the primitives are installable now", "Runnable, but not yet a live network of real users", "Usable and actively hardened; its hardware binding is not yet validated"). The Hestia card has none — it goes straight to the Greek goddess. At the exact moment I was scanning for status, one card silently changed shape.

### /why-web4 (09:00)
- The problem section is the best writing on the site. Five problems, each with a "root cause" line. "Actions have no cost. Bad actors can operate indefinitely at zero expense." That landed.
- "Why Previous Solutions Failed" with a *what it gets right* line for each is a genuinely trust-building move. Conceding that Web3 gets key-ownership right, and that CAPTCHAs do slow mass abuse, made me believe the rest more.
- **Then I hit the Q&A and fell in.** 72 questions. Seventy-two. The page is about 20,000 words. It is labelled "6 min + optional Q&A."
- I don't think the FAQ is bad — a lot of it is excellent, and the "Isn't this just a social credit system?" table is the single best rebuttal on the site. But there is no size signal anywhere. I started reading, kept scrolling, and only understood the scale of what I'd entered several minutes in.
- **This is where I hit my first real contradiction** (see Friction #1).

### /first-contact (28:00)
- Alice's five-act story: Join, Build, Stumble, Recover, Die & Reborn. Concrete, and I liked that she *fails* in act 3. A walkthrough where the protagonist stumbles is much more convincing than one where everything works.
- **The framing genuinely startled me.** "Energy death." "Trust death, permanent." I am reading about an internet protocol and it is telling me I can die. The page does soften it ("Suspended, not deleted: 'death' here means Alice's standing is suspended") but the softening comes *after* the word does its damage.
- **The thing I could not get past**: trust starts at 0.50, and 0.50 is the line where staying below is fatal. I start my life in Web4 standing exactly on the fatal boundary. The site is careful to say "*crossing* below 0.5 restricts your features right away and is recoverable; only *staying* below it is fatal" — fine — but nobody explains why day zero puts me on the line rather than above it. As a new person that reads as: one bad week and I'm in the restricted zone, with no buffer I was ever given.

### /how-it-works (38:00)
- Labelled 25 minutes and that one felt about right. Honest sizing here.
- The LCT / ATP / T3 / CI / MRH assembly is clear, and "Reading and browsing are effectively free, about 1 ATP" was the sentence that made the economics click — I'd been quietly worried I'd be charged to read.
- **New acronym with no warning**: SAL (Society-Authority-Law). It's expanded on the page so I wasn't lost, but it's the sixth acronym in a five-page path and it appears in neither the landing page vocabulary strip nor anywhere earlier in the reading order.

### /running-now (50:00)
- **This is the site's best page and I want to say so plainly.** "Web4 is R&D, not a shipped product. Nothing on this page is a fielded production system, and this page will not pretend otherwise." Version numbers. Test counts. "not yet validated on-device." "about 10 open gaps" in the threat model. I have never seen a project page volunteer its own open gap count.
- **One thing snagged**: Hestia is the only piece badged "Running" and it is version **0.0.3**. The hub is 0.1.0-alpha.0 and badged Reference; web4-core is 0.3.0. So the piece with the highest badge has the lowest version number by an order of magnitude. The badge key does defend this ("The badge says how far a piece has travelled, not how good it is") and the caveat "deployed on lab machines only" is right there. I believed it. But my eye read the badge column first and got the ordering exactly backwards.

### Detour: /trust-tensor, /atp-economics (56:00)
- **T3 is excellent.** Talent / Training / Temperament, role-specific, with the Alice-as-data-analyst-90% vs Alice-as-mechanic-28% example. "Trust is always for a specific role, not a universal score" is the idea I'll remember longest. The decay rates (Talent none, Training 180-day half-life, Temperament 30 days) are the kind of specificity that makes a thing feel designed rather than asserted.
- **ATP's recharge cap is the cleverest thing on the site.** "recharge = min(sum of slices, ATP_cost)" — you can never profit from confirmations of your own work, only recover its cost. And then the distinction that saves it: "recharge refunds, payment earns." That closed the loophole I was already forming in my head, before I finished forming it.

### Detour: /lct-explainer, /what-could-go-wrong (63:00)
- I went looking for a worry and the site had already gotten there first. The FAQ is literally titled "Software-only caps at 0.50 - and 0.5 is the survival line. Am I dead on arrival?" and answers "What the software-only ceiling really costs you is *margin*."
- /what-could-go-wrong lists nine risks with likelihoods, including "Nobody adopts it (Existential · High likelihood)" as risk number one. Naming your own most likely fatal outcome first is a real choice.
- The accessibility risk section says of its own architecture: "That's a real second-class experience baked into the architecture" and "it codifies the existing digital divide into the trust layer itself." I did not expect to read that on a project's own website.

### /learn, /glossary, /onramp (70:00)
- /learn gave me the five-step path with time estimates, and that's where I could finally check the estimates against what I'd just lived (see Friction #2).
- **The glossary was a shock.** The landing page told me "A handful of primitives everything else is built from... Start anywhere, or read them in order in the glossary." I expected the six terms in the vocabulary strip, maybe a dozen. There are **55 entries**, and they include SAGE, "D-Numbers (D1-D9)" from something called the Synchronism framework, "Modal Awareness", "Coherence Levels (Reactive through Conscious)", and "Cumulative Identity Context." None of these appear anywhere in the five-page reading path. I went from "handful of primitives" to AI consciousness research in one click.

### /day-in-web4, /karma-consequences (76:00)
- /day-in-web4 opens by saying "none of it ships today" and calls itself a thought experiment. Good.
- The karma formula is clean: `next_life_atp = base_atp + (karma × karma_multiplier)`, 100 + (10 × 2) = 120. I could check Alice's rebirth at 112 against it and it works out.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — trust earned through witnessed behavior instead of declared by a platform or assumed from a key. The Web2/Web3/Web4 triptych taught me this cleanly.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — one substrate, three scales; personal / community / enterprise. Solid.
- [~] What LCT means — I know what it does and what it's anchored to. I don't know what "Linked Context Token" means as words, or what's actually inside one.
- [~] What ATP/ADP means — ATP yes, thoroughly, including the recharge cap and why it closes the farming loophole. ADP no; it's in the name everywhere and explained only in the glossary.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — Talent/Training/Temperament, role-specific, 0.4/0.3/0.3, with per-dimension decay. The clearest concept page on the site.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — comprehensively. This is the site's strongest suit by a wide margin.
- [x] Why any of this matters — yes, and the problem-before-solution ordering is why.

## Unanswered Questions
1. **If I start at 0.50 and staying below 0.50 is fatal, what protects me in week one?** I begin standing on the line with no margin I ever earned. Is there a grace period, a probationary state, a floor? The site is precise about the crossing/staying distinction and silent on the day-zero position.
2. **What is ADP?** It's in the nav item, the landing page vocabulary strip, and the section title on every page that touches energy. The page named for it teaches only ATP.
3. **Can people in a new community actually see my track record or not?** `/why-web4` tells me both — "Beyond 3 hops: Invisible" and "The new community can see your track record." I could not determine which is true, and it's the difference between portable reputation working and not working.
4. **Why "Linked Context Token"?**
5. **What are the 24 "concepts" on `/learn`, and how would I complete one?**
6. **Who witnesses the witnesses, early on?** The bootstrap answer ("3 founding members create the society", "Founders are ahead on record length, not on rate") tells me founders don't get a rate advantage. It doesn't tell me what stops the first handful of witness nodes from being the de facto authority for a long time simply by being everyone's shortest path.

## Honest Assessment

This site is unusually good at the hard part and oddly careless about the easy part.

The hard part is being truthful about an unfinished research project without either overselling it or making it sound worthless, and 4-Life does that better than anything I've read. `/running-now` volunteers version numbers, test counts, "not yet validated on-device", and "about 10 open gaps." `/what-could-go-wrong` lists "Nobody adopts it" as risk number one at high likelihood, and the accessibility section describes the project's own architecture as "a second-class experience baked into the architecture" that "codifies the existing digital divide into the trust layer itself." I went in prepared to catch this site overselling and I could not catch it once. Every time I formed a skeptical question, I found the site had already asked it, in blunter words than I would have used. That earned an enormous amount of credibility, and it's why my understanding ended up "good" rather than "partial."

The easy part is arithmetic and consistency, and that's where it slips. The first number the site gave me — "2 min" — was wrong by about three times, and I could tell within ten minutes. The second — "6 min + optional Q&A" for a twenty-thousand-word page — was wrong by an order of magnitude. For most sites those are cosmetic. For this one they're not, because calibrated honesty is the entire pitch. If I can't trust the reading-path estimate, the instinct to check the trust-ceiling numbers follows immediately, and that's a bad instinct to install in a reader on page one.

The MRH problem is the one I'd fix first. Reading in the order I was told to, I came away believing Web4 walls you off so you can only see people your network vouches for — which is the filter-bubble outcome the project is clearly trying to avoid. `/trust-neighborhood` exists specifically to correct that, says exactly the right thing ("Strangers aren't invisible; they're unverified"), and sits outside the reading path where a first-time visitor won't reach it. The correct model is already written on this site. It just isn't on the pages where the belief gets formed. And because "Beyond 3 hops: Invisible" also contradicts the same page's own answer about carrying reputation into a new community, this isn't only a clarity issue — it makes the site's headline promise look broken by the site's own mechanism.

The things I'll actually remember, a week from now, are three sentences: "Good behavior compounds. Bad behavior follows you." "Trust is always for a specific role, not a universal score." And "recharge refunds, payment earns." All three are doing real conceptual work in very few words. The site is at its best when it writes like that and at its weakest when it explains its own taxonomy to me — I sat through three paragraphs about what the badges mean before being told what the four pieces are, and a glossary billed as "a handful of primitives" turned out to contain fifty-five entries ending in AI consciousness research.

The death-and-rebirth vocabulary is the one aesthetic choice I'd push back on. "Trust death, permanent" is a startling phrase to meet on page three of an explainer, and the reassurance that nothing is actually deleted always arrives after the word has already landed. It made the system feel punitive at exactly the moment the site was trying to make it feel fair.

Would I come back? Yes — but for `/running-now` and `/what-could-go-wrong`, not for the reading path. Those two pages are the reason I believe this project is real. Would I recommend it? To someone technical and patient, yes, with the warning that "45 minutes" means closer to ninety.
