## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: The hero line is clear and strong: "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules." I got the problem right away.
- Four pieces (core standard, hub, hestia, hardbound) at "substrate + three scales" made sense as a map, though I didn't know yet why I'd care about any of them.

- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".

### /tldr (02:00)
- The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 comparison is the best thing on the site. "Platform declares it / key holder assumed / others witness and sign" is a mental model I could repeat to a friend.
- Status table with badges (Spec / Reference / Running) is helpful. I noticed only **hestia** is "Running", and that "daily use" means the lab's own.
- Clicked: Why Web4.

### /why-web4 (06:00)
- Problem section is good: spam, silos, fresh starts, AI agents, platform control, each with a "root cause". This is the page that made me care.
- The FAQ is huge (I gave up partway). Some answers are great (the social-credit one is honest).
- Things I noticed:
  - "What backs ATP?" says scarcity comes partly from: **each community mints a limited supply**. Hold that thought (see /atp-economics below).
  - Newcomers are fragile for the "first ~30 actions". Later /atp-economics says ~100 quality actions are needed "before trust starts sorting". Are these the same window or different ones? I couldn't tell.
  - "Make bad behavior expensive" is the core promise. Hold that thought too (spam prices below).

### /first-contact (14:00)
- Alice's five acts with an ATP number and a trust number is a nice idea. Two numbers to watch is the right amount for a beginner.
- Act 5 "Die & Reborn": Alice runs out of energy, trust is 0.51 at death, she gets a **+12 karma bonus** and restarts at **112 ATP** and trust **0.54**.
- The page itself warns that the trust reset doesn't match the karma page's formula. I appreciated the warning, but it means the first concrete story I was given is already flagged as "not how it works". I didn't know what to believe.

### /onramp (20:00)

- Build-order list (web4-core → web4-trust-core → web4-policy) was too inside-baseball for me but harmless.

### /atp-economics (24:00)
- "Budget, not wealth" lands. ADP as an automatic receipt that turns back into ATP when confirmed: got it.
- "Recharge refunds, payment earns" is a great slogan. That finally explained how anyone gets *more* than 100.
- Confusions:
  - **"No scarcity cap, no mining"** and "the core spec places no protocol-level constraint on issuance... cannot tell you the rate." But /why-web4 told me "each community mints a limited supply". Is supply limited or not? These read as opposite answers to the same question.
  - The price list: **spam message costs 5 ATP net −5**, and a **low-quality post is also net −5**. So spamming costs exactly the same as posting something mediocre? And spam is the cheapest thing on the list. That undercuts "make bad behavior expensive" from /why-web4.
  - The quality ramp table: page itself says the numbers don't fit one line. Sam at 35% "earns back ~6", but by the 30%→0, 50%→12 line he'd get ~3. More "illustrative" numbers I'm told not to trust.

### /lct-explainer (31:00)
- Clear expansion of the acronym; the "Linked / Context / Token" breakdown helped.
- The trust-ceiling table was the most important thing I read all session, though I only realised it later:
  - 0.50 = "neutral midpoint and survival line. Falling below restricts features; staying below is fatal."
  - **Software only: capped at 0.50.**
- So a software-only identity's *ceiling* is the death line. It can never be above the line, only on it or under it.

### /karma-consequences (39:00)
- Two kinds of death explained well.
- **Formula**: next_life_atp = 100 + karma × 2; next_life_trust = prev_trust × 0.95.
- Confusions:
  - "Premature death from ATP exhaustion" is listed as **negative** karma, and the worked "Life 1" ends in ATP exhaustion with **karma −15 → 70 ATP**. But First Contact's Alice dies from ATP exhaustion and gets **+12 karma → 112 ATP**. Same kind of death, opposite sign.
  - Even if +12 were right, the formula gives 100 + 12×2 = **124**, not 112.
  - Trust decay: Alice died at 0.51. 0.51 × 0.95 = **0.4845**, which is *below* the 0.5 survival line. So by this page's formula, a hardware user who dies near 0.5 is reborn already inside the trust-death zone. Nobody on either page mentions this; First Contact just says the formulas don't match.

## Honest Assessment
The *why* is the best I've seen for a project like this. The landing, /tldr, and /why-web4 problem section got me from zero to "I get it and I care" in about ten minutes, and the honesty (badges, "stops accidents, not adversaries", the risk page) made me trust the authors.

The *how* is where it fell apart for me, and specifically wherever there are numbers. Three times I was shown a worked example and then told on the same or the next page that it doesn't match the formula. The site is honest about each mismatch individually, but the effect of many "illustrative, not canonical" caveats is that I stopped believing any number. The karma/rebirth story in particular contradicts itself on sign, magnitude, and whether death is even required.

The single most consequential thing I pieced together was never stated where I'd need it: the only piece I can actually run (hestia) seems to give me a software-only identity whose trust ceiling is exactly the death line. I only assembled that from four separate pages. If that's true it should be on /hestia in one sentence; if it's false, the pages that led me there need fixing.
