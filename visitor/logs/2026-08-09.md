## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: "Trust, built into the internet." is a strong hook, and the subheadline actually told me what the thing is: "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules." That is the clearest one-liner I have read on a site like this. It names a problem I already believe in.
- The nav is enormous. Four sections, roughly 27 links, all visible at once. My eye glazed. What saved me was the small orientation paragraph: "New to Web4? Read in this order: the 2-minute intro, Why Web4, then First Contact." That single sentence is doing a lot of work. Without it I would have clicked something random and bounced.
- The four onramp cards read well. "Named for the Greek goddess of the hearth: your machine is the home fire, your agents are guests under your rules" made Hestia stick immediately. "As in hardware-bound" for Hardbound did the same. Naming things after what they mean is underrated.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes" (/tldr), following the site's own instruction.
- Confusion points: none blocking. Mild: "the core standard is the substrate you build on" uses "substrate" before defining it.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (03:30)
- This delivered. The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast is the moment it clicked for me:
  - Web2: "A platform declares your reputation. Delete the account and it is gone."
  - Web3: "Whoever holds the key is assumed to be the rightful owner."
  - Web4: "Trust accrues from witnessed behavior."
  That three-way comparison taught me more in ten seconds than the rest of the page.
- The four pieces carry status tags. I braced for undefined jargon, but the page defines them right there: "Referencebuilt and runnable. Runnable is not the same as running in production. Runningdeployed and operational today, in day-to-day use." Good. I had been ready to complain that "Reference" is a weird word for "finished code" and it still is, but the page does not leave me guessing.
- Small snag: the tag key on this page covers two tiers (Reference, Running), and the core standard is tagged "Reference" here. When I later reached /running-now, the same core standard is tagged "Spec + Reference" and there is a third tier, "Spec", that never appears on this page. Not a contradiction, but the tag I was shown was not the tag the detail page uses.
- Clicked: Why Web4?

### Why Web4? (07:00)
- The four root causes (actions have no cost, reputation is siloed, identity is disposable, AI complicates verification) are all things I have personally experienced. Problem-before-solution works.
- The honesty here is genuinely disarming: "The protocol spec and ~47,000 lines of reference code are real, tested, and public" but "there is no live network with real users yet." I trust a site more after reading that, not less.
- The FAQ volunteering the access gap ("creates an access gap for people who don't own any personal device") is the kind of thing most projects bury.
- Clicked: First Contact.

### First Contact (13:00)
- The Alice walkthrough is the best thing on the site. Five acts, real numbers moving, and I could see the mechanic instead of being told it.
- Alice's arc: Act 1 join at 100 ATP / 0.50 trust. Act 2 build, trust 0.56. Act 3 stumble, −25 ATP, trust 0.48. Act 4 recover, trust 0.62. Act 5 "Die & Reborn", ATP=0, "+12 bonus".
- When Alice hit 0.48 I thought "wait, is she dead?" and the page answered before I could stew: "Below 0.50: features get restricted until she rebuilds it, and if she stays below, that same line becomes fatal." That is a good catch by whoever wrote it. The distinction between crossing and staying is exactly what I needed.
- Where I actually got stuck: Act 5. Alice dies with **ATP = 0** and is reborn with **+12**. Then the caveat says: "Whether you keep your whole final balance or a reduced portion of it is undecided, and 12 is one modelling choice, not the rule."
  I read that three times. Alice's whole final balance is **zero**. A reduced portion of zero is also **zero**. Neither of the two options the caveat offers can produce 12. So where does 12 come from? The caveat is explaining a number using a dichotomy that cannot generate that number.
  And I could not tell whether 12 is her *total* starting energy or a bonus *on top of* something. The "+" says bonus-on-top. The caveat's "whole final balance" framing says total. These point opposite ways.
- Jargon slip near the top: "The number compared against 0.50 is raw trust, not effective trust (raw × CI²)." On page three of a beginner path, "CI²" arrives with no explanation of CI, no explanation of why it is squared. CI is defined lower down in a collapsed section. I skipped the sentence.
- Clicked: How It Works.

### How It Works (22:00)
- Solid integration page. Five systems, then the Birth → Life → Death → Rebirth lifecycle. The coherence example is vivid: "If someone usually posts coding tutorials at 9am and suddenly starts posting crypto spam at 3am from a new country, their coherence drops."
- Then I hit a line that undid Act 3 for me. The page has an **Aliveness** summary: "You're 'alive' when: ATP > 0 + Trust > 0.5 + CI coherent."
  Two problems, from a reader who had just come from First Contact:
  1. It says **Trust > 0.5**, strictly. But I later learned on /trust-tensor that "0.5 = newcomer baseline" and "you start at the neutral midpoint." So on the strict reading, **every newcomer is born not-alive**. Everyone starts at exactly 0.50, which is not greater than 0.5.
  2. It drops the durability qualifier. The same page's own death section says trust death is "raw trust falls below 0.5 **and stays there**." First Contact said crossing is recoverable. But the Aliveness checklist says a momentary dip means you are not alive, which is precisely what Alice's 0.48 was.
  One page, two rules, and the summary version is the wrong one.
- The karma examples confused me in a different way: "Life 1 → Life 2. Ended with 145 ATP. Reborn with 145 ATP (karma carried forward)." My first thought was "how do you die holding 145 ATP?" The page anticipates it: "none of these lives ends at 0 ATP, so none of them is the energy death described above. That is why they read ended and not died."
  But that answer only closes half the door. The page told me there are exactly two ways to die (ATP zero, or trust death), and rebirth requires T3 ≥ 0.5 which rules out trust death. So these lives ended by neither documented route. The note tells me what did **not** end the life without ever saying what **did**. If a life can just "end" without dying, that is a third thing the lifecycle never named.
- Clicked: the reading path, to see what I had left.

Those are compatible once you read the "the lab's own" line here, but that reconciling line lives only on this page, and it is the fifth page of five.

### Karma & Consequences (36:00)
- Here the rebirth math finally appeared:
  ```
  next_life_atp = base_atp + (karma * karma_multiplier)
  base_atp = 100, karma_multiplier = 2, trust_decay_factor = 0.95
  ```
  with worked examples "karma = -15 → 70 ATP" and "karma = +10 → 120 ATP".
- So now I have counted **three different rebirth models** across the site:
  - here: start = 100 + (karma × 2), so Alice's +12 karma would give **124**
  - /how-it-works: ended 145 → reborn **145**, the balance intact
  - /first-contact: ATP=0 → **12**
- To its credit, this page knows about one of the others: "Whether you keep your whole final balance or a reduced portion of it is not decided, and the lifecycle walkthrough models it the other way, carrying the balance forward intact." And it caveats itself: "One model, not the rule."
  But the cross-reference only reaches /how-it-works. First Contact's 12 is neither the formula (124) nor the intact balance (0). It is a third number that no caveat on the site accounts for, and First Contact is the page most visitors will see first.
- "permanent is the identity, not the penalty" is a nice line.

## Unanswered Questions
1. **What does Alice actually restart with?** 12 ATP total, or 100 + 12? The page shows "+12" and then explains it with a "whole final balance vs reduced portion" framing that yields 0 either way.
2. **Is rebirth better or worse than being new?** Newcomers get 100. If a trusted agent is reborn with 12, dying is worse than never having existed, which inverts the "head start" story.
3. **Am I alive at exactly 0.50?** I start there as a newcomer, /how-it-works says alive requires > 0.5, and software-only identities are capped at exactly 0.50 forever. Does that mean software-only users are permanently at the aliveness boundary?
4. **What ended the lives in the 145 → 145 example?** Not energy death (the page says so), and not trust death (they were eligible for rebirth). So what?
5. **Why is coherence squared** in "raw × CI²"? Squaring is a strong choice and it is never motivated anywhere I looked.
6. Does the 5% transfer fee apply to the karma carried across a rebirth, or only to peer transfers?

## Honest Assessment

I came in cold and I left able to explain Web4 to a friend. That is not a low bar and this site clears it. The Web2/Web3/Web4 triptych on /tldr, the Alice walkthrough, and the driver's-license analogy for LCT are three genuinely good pieces of teaching, and the reading path means I never had to guess where to go next.

What distinguishes this site is that it argues against itself in public. /what-could-go-wrong lists nine risks with honest severity ratings and admits several are unsolved. /running-now refuses to let its own "Running" badge mean more than it does, volunteering that the daily use is the lab's own. /onramp tells me two of the four pieces have no integration at all. I finished the session trusting the project more than when I started, which for a site about trust is the right outcome.

Which makes the rebirth problem stand out, because it is the same failure the site avoids everywhere else. The site is careful, sometimes to a fault, about labelling unsettled things as unsettled. Every rebirth number I found carried a caveat saying "one modelling choice, not the rule." But three caveats do not add up to one explanation. I was shown 12, 124, and 145 as answers to the same question, and the caveat on the page I read first offers me two possibilities that both compute to zero for the example it is captioning. Being told a number is provisional does not help when the provisional number cannot be reached by either route I was offered.

The version of this that actually bothered me is not the inconsistency, it is the implication. I was told karma means a good track record "starts your next life stronger." Then I learned strangers start at 100 and Alice restarts at 12. If I take the page literally, the lesson of Alice's story is that building trust and then running out of energy leaves you eight times worse off than if you had never joined. I do not think that is what anyone meant. But it is what the two pages say when you read them in the order the site recommends, which is what I did.

Smaller version of the same thing: /how-it-works has an "Aliveness" checklist that says trust must be **greater than** 0.5, while /trust-tensor says 0.5 is exactly where everyone begins. The site is clearly aware this number is delicate, since /what-could-go-wrong flags the 0.50 threshold as a named risk. A summary line that makes every newcomer technically dead on arrival is a small thing, but it is small in the way a typo in a price is small.

One structural note. Both of my real confusions came from the same place: a *summary* surface stating a rule more crudely than the *detailed* surface elsewhere on the same site. The Aliveness bullet drops "and stays there". The "+12" caption drops the base grant. The detailed versions are correct, and someone clearly thought hard about them. The summaries are where the precision leaked out, and summaries are what a first-time visitor reads.

I would come back, and I would send this to a skeptical friend, with one warning: read /karma-consequences before you read Act 5 of First Contact, or the numbers will not add up.
