## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest, and that honesty is what kept me reading for an hour and a half. Every page says what runs and what does not, the badge key is explained on the first click, and the "Where you fit today" section on /running-now is the kindest thing I have seen a technical project say to a non-technical reader. The problem framing on /why-web4 is persuasive without hype. I believe these people are trying to tell the truth.

The site's weakness is that it tells the truth in too many places at once, and the places disagree. I counted five pairs of statements that flatly contradict each other on the things I most wanted to nail down: whether strangers can see a message, whether sending outside your neighbourhood costs energy, whether the energy pool is capped, whether dying with good trust earns or costs karma, and whether a software identity can build any trust at all. None of these are subtle; they are the concrete examples, the ones written to make the abstractions land, and in three of the five cases the contradiction is on a single page.

The site also knows its numbers are unsettled and says so, again and again, with the same disclaimer paragraph pasted across three pages. By the fourth "one modelling choice, not the rule" I stopped trusting any number, which I suspect is the opposite of what the disclaimers are for. If the karma amount is open, show one example and say it is illustrative, rather than three examples that disagree and a paragraph explaining why disagreement is fine.

What I would tell a friend: read /tldr, /why-web4's top half, and /running-now. That is twenty minutes and it is coherent. Everything past that is where the site starts contradicting itself, and where a careful reader begins to wonder whether the mechanism is as settled as the vocabulary suggests.
