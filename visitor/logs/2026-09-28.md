## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): trust earned from witnessed behavior, not granted by a platform or assumed from a key.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one substrate, three scales. I got this from /tldr and /onramp.
- [x] What LCT means: device-anchored identity, with a ceiling set by what kind of chip holds it.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means: energy budget and its receipt; recharged when recipients confirm. I do not understand why one page prices it in cents.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three scores per role, blended 0.4/0.3/0.3. The /trust-tensor page taught me this; /first-contact nearly untaught it.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: yes, and this is the site's strength. Every page says.
- [ ] Why any of this matters: half. The problem framing convinced me. The mechanism did not, because the numbers that are supposed to show it working (karma, rebirth, MRH cost, CI death) disagree with each other and the site says so itself.

## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest and unusually inconsistent, and those two facts are related: it keeps telling me where its own pages disagree. On rebirth trust, on karma amounts, on what ends a life, on whether decay can kill you, the text says "this is one model, not the rule" or "not settled" and points at the other page. I respect that more than a slick site would earn. But the cumulative effect after 80 minutes is that I trust the problem statement and the status reporting, and I do not trust any specific number. When a site tells me its two worked examples give opposite karma for the same death, the walkthrough stops being evidence that the mechanism works and becomes evidence that the authors have not decided how it works.

The high points are real. The 2-minute page, the problems half of Why Web4, the trust tensor page, and the "day with Hestia" walkthrough each taught me something I could repeat to a friend. The "what could go wrong" page, and especially risk 9 saying "this one already does," is why I would consider coming back.

The low points cluster around one thing: the survival number 0.50 is asked to be the neutral start, the software ceiling, and the death line at once, and the one piece of software that actually runs lives exactly on it. Nobody on the site says what daily life at that point looks like. Until a page owns that conjunction, the honest badge "Running" on hestia describes something whose trust story the site cannot tell.

Three dead in-page anchors (reading cost, at-scale, recovery) and one stale count (7 risks vs 9) are small, but they are the kind of thing that makes a careful reader wonder what else was not re-checked.
