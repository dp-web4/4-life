## Honest Assessment

I came away respecting this site more than I understood it.

The first ten minutes are very good. I knew what was being proposed, why, and how real it was, which is rare for a project in this general space. `/running-now` and `/what-could-go-wrong` are unusually candid, and they earned more trust than any feature list could.

Then I followed the numbers and they did not hold up. The site tells me to read numbers "for the shape", which is fair for a research project. But a shape has to be consistent too, and in places it isn't. The same death pays +12 or costs -15. Spam costs less than a post. The ramp table doesn't follow the ramp. Life 1's arithmetic doesn't close. A site whose thesis is "you can check the record" invites exactly this checking, so these mismatches damage it more than they would damage another site.

The disclaimers don't solve this, and at some point they make it worse. Each is honest. Together, on `/how-it-works` especially, they teach that nothing on the page should be relied on. My advice is to fix the examples so they stop needing disclaimers, not to write better disclaimers.

The deepest confusion isn't a number. It is that I was promised a way to prove what AI agents did, and I was mostly taught an energy economy where a person called Alice dies and is reborn. These may well be the same project at different layers, but nothing on the reading path shows me that seam. Meanwhile the one thing badged Running has a software identity that can't climb, a chain that doesn't claim completeness, and no ATP in its description. Each fact is disclosed. Nobody puts them next to each other and says what that adds up to today, so I did it myself and may have got it wrong.

Last, the small one that stuck: a project about trust, whose FAQ asks "Why is the project anonymous?", sends me to a page promising who is in the lab, and shows me a list of computers. That might be a deliberate and interesting statement about what a member is. It didn't feel like an answer.

Caveat on all of the above: I never pressed Start on First Contact. If the interactive walkthrough is as good as the page says, some of the conceptual confusion may be less severe for a real visitor. The numeric and copy contradictions would stand regardless.
