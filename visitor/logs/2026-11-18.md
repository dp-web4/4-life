## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: The tagline is good. "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules" told me in one line what this is about. "Trust, built into the internet" is vaguer but fine as a headline.
- The first three paragraphs worked: agents act, nobody can prove what they did, trust should be earned by witnessed behavior. The definition of "to witness" (observe and attest, staking your own reputation) is the single most useful sentence on the page.
- Then "The onramp" section lost me. Four cards in a row with: "open ontology", "witnessed presence", "role-contextual trust", "sovereign Web4 society", "seven roles", "signed founding law", "append-only witnessed ledger", "sealed member channels", "cryptographic LCT", "scoped revocable delegation", "pre-action policy enforcement". None explained. I understood the *scales* (community / personal / enterprise) but not what any of the things *do*.
- "The vocabulary" lists Identity (LCT), Trust (T3), Value (V3), Neighborhood (MRH), Energy (ATP/ADP), Reputation (Karma). The plain word in front of each acronym helps a lot. But six acronyms on a landing page is a lot.
- "Where this actually stands" has a link "what does that tag mean?" but I saw no tags/badges on this page for it to refer to. (Possibly a badge renders visually and did not come through as text.)
- I appreciated the honesty paragraph: "research and development, not a finished product."
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".

### /tldr — Web4 in 2 Minutes (03:00)
- This is the best page on the site. Problem, shift (Web2 / Web3 / Web4 cards), four pieces, result, "how real is it, honestly?".
- The Web2/Web3/Web4 comparison finally made the idea click: platform declares your reputation / key-holder is assumed to be owner / trust accrues from what others saw you do.
- It promises "every term is spelled out the first time it appears". Mostly true for this page. R&D, Web2, Web3 are assumed.
- The badge key (Spec / Reference / Running) is clear, and I liked the admission that "Running" only counts the lab's own daily use.
- Confusion: "The three tiers run in that order" directly above cards ordered hub (Reference), Hestia (Running), Hardbound (Reference). I had to reread to work out that "tiers" meant the badges, not the cards.
- Confusion: the Result says hardware-anchored reputation "cannot be reset", and the same page says hardware binding is "not yet validated on real devices". So the headline benefit rests on the part that is least proven. The page does say this, but in two places far apart.
- It is not 2 minutes. More like 5 to 6.
- **Important for later**: this page never mentions LCT, T3, trust tensors, ATP, MRH or "witness chain" in its body. Remember that.
- Clicked: "Read the site in order" → /learn.

### /learn — The Reading Path (09:00)
- Clear and reassuring: five pages, 45 minutes, stop after three if short on time. The times add up (2+6+7+25+5 = 45).
- Small snags: the Onramp path card says "4 pages" but the sidebar's Onramp group has five entries. The five Start Here cards sit under a sub-label "Core Concepts", which is also the name of a different 9-page path. The closing note says "the code at the bottom of this page is what you can actually run", but the bottom of the page has links, no code.
- "0 / 24 concepts completed" made the site feel like a course. 24 is daunting right after being told five pages are enough.
- Clicked: Why Web4?

### /why-web4 (12:00)
- The five "problems you already know" are genuinely relatable (spam is free, reputation is trapped, bans don't stick, AI makes it worse, platforms have all the power). Each with a one-line root cause. Good.
- "Why previous solutions failed" was fair to the alternatives, which made me trust the page more.
- "This is what Web4 proposes": four mechanisms with plain names first and the acronym second. This is where I finally learned ATP = Allocation Transfer Packets, LCT = Linked Context Token, T3 = Talent/Training/Temperament. The MRH line telling me the name is "math-flavored jargon you can ignore" made me laugh and relax.
- The "all four, in one moment" forum-reply example is excellent. But it ends "fractions of a cent of energy" — and every other page insists ATP is not money and cannot be cashed out. So what is a cent of it?
- Then the page says "Coherence Index (CI): not a fifth mechanism". Later pages call it one of "five interlocking systems". I started keeping a tally of how many things there are (see friction log).
- The FAQ: "72 questions". I skimmed the "Most asked" 12. Good answers, honest caveats ("email in the 1980s", "no timeline"). I gave up after that. Nobody reads 72.
- Confusion: the lost-hardware answer says recovery is "built in" (your laptop attests if you lose your phone). Other pages say multi-device witnessing has never been run on a real second device. So "built in" means "designed in".
- Confusion: "What backs ATP?" says scarcity comes from "capped issuance". The ATP page says "No scarcity cap" and that the protocol puts no constraint on issuance.
- The hop weights (0.70, 0.49, 0.34, then 0) are given with no rule. I guessed "multiply by 0.7 each hop". The site did not tell me.
- Clicked: Walk through with Alice.

### /first-contact (22:00)
- Promised an interactive 7-minute walkthrough. As fetched text I only got the preamble and the five-act map, so I cannot judge the walkthrough itself.
- The two-number panel (Energy starts 100, Trust starts 0.50) is a great simplification.
- The act map: Join (100, 0.50) → Build (0.56) → Stumble (−25 ATP, 0.48) → Recover (0.62) → Die & Reborn (dies at ATP 0, trust 0.51; +12 bonus → 112; reborn at 0.54).
- Questions I had immediately: in Act 3 trust is 0.48, below the 0.50 line that the panel just told me restricts features. Does Alice get restricted? The map does not say. In Act 5 trust drops from 0.62 to 0.51 and then she is "reborn at 0.54" — why does dying raise it?
- The page spends a full paragraph telling me the +12 "is one modelling choice, not the rule" and that the karma page "models it as falling at each rebirth, which is not what happens here". I respect the candor, but as a first-timer it reads as: the walkthrough's ending is disputed by the site itself.
- "The rules running here are real" vs "a model with nothing deployed". Which?
- "Death", "rebirth", "karma", "lives". This vocabulary is strange for software about proving what an AI agent did. Nobody has told me why an account "dies" rather than "runs out of budget".

### /how-it-works (30:00)
- Listed as 25 minutes. It is the longest thing I read and it felt like 40.
- Good: the "No network you can join yet... the present tense is the model talking" callout up top. That set expectations correctly.
- Good: the price list (read ~1 ATP, post 10–20, task 15–30, broadcast 20–50) and the four earn examples with nets. Concrete.
- Good: the trust update rule with a worked number (quality 0.85 → +0.007). I could follow the arithmetic and it checked out everywhere I tried.
- Confusing: **spam costs 5 ATP; an honest post costs 10–20.** On a page whose thesis is "spam is expensive", spam is the cheapest thing you can send. I understand the spam earns nothing back, but an honest self-started post also nets "0 at best". So the difference between an honest poster and a spammer is −5 vs 0, and the spammer pays less up front.
- Confusing: the three-lives example. Life 2: "ATP fluctuated 80–180" and also "one crisis dropped it to 15". Life 1: trust rises 0.50 → 0.65 over "a dozen+" actions, but each action moves trust by a few thousandths, so a dozen cannot get you +0.15 (I make it roughly 25 or more excellent actions). The page says the climb is "the net of many such moves", which is not a dozen.
- Confusing: "Web4 has four core systems" in the "How the pieces fit" section, on a page that opens with "Five interlocking systems". 
- Confusing: rebirth. Here a life "ends" with 145 ATP and you are reborn with 145 (the whole balance). First Contact gave 100 + 12. The karma page gives 100 + karma × 2. Three pages, three rules. Each one admits it is unsettled, which is honest, but I walked away not knowing what karma is.
- The governance section (Society–Authority–Law, the plagiarism appeal walkthrough) was readable and concrete. "Hasn't been tested with real humans yet" is the right caveat.
- "No moderators needed to police spam" followed by a society with a rotating Moderator role. The page does explain (spam vs disputes) but the slogan lands first.
- Two different trust-fade numbers (0.9 per step in agent chains, 0.7 per hop in neighborhoods). The page flags they differ. I still do not know when I would use which.

### /running-now (48:00)
- Opens with a "New to Web4?" box: this page "assumes the vocabulary (LCT, trust tensors, witness chain)... start with the 2-minute TL;DR... every term is spelled out in the TL;DR". **I had just read the TL;DR. None of those three terms is in it.** I would have been sent in a circle if I had not also read /why-web4.
- Otherwise the clearest status page I have seen on a research project. Each piece, its badge, what is tested, what is not. "this is not production-ready", "about 10 open gaps", "CI runs against mocks".
- The Hestia caveat stopped me: the record of actions is written by the same gate that approves them, and "two environment variables suffice" to route around it. So the witness chain is "a record of governed activity, not a record of all activity". That is a large hole in "proving what an AI agent did", stated calmly and called "an accepted limit". I am glad it is stated. I am not sure the landing page's promise survives it.
- Hardware binding is described three ways: tested on a real Intel TPM in the core standard; "trait contracts only" in Hestia; real code but not validated on-device in Hardbound. I could not form one sentence that says whether hardware-anchored identity works today.
- "Where you fit today" is a great section. I am the first bullet (here to understand, nothing to install).

### /hub (61:00)

But the hub's badge is Reference, the TL;DR card says "not yet a live network of real users", and /running-now says the hub "would earn Running once live instances operate day-to-day" and that an adopter would be "standing up one of the first live instances". If the lab runs one daily, why is it not Running, by the same rule that gave Hestia its Running badge for the lab's own use? Or is the lab's society not a hub? I could not tell.

### /hestia (64:00)
- "A day with Hestia" is the most concrete thing on the site: real commands, a 24-hour delegation, revoke. I finally saw what a person *does* with this.
- "You can't quietly rewrite your own history" (features list) vs "the chain does not promise completeness... silence in the chain is not proof that nothing happened" (further down). Both true, I think, but the first one sounds like a bigger promise than it is.
- The policy gate "stops accidents, not adversaries". Clear and memorable.
- Big question: Hestia today uses a software identity, and software identities are "capped at 0.50". 0.50 is where everyone starts and also the line below which you get restricted. So for everybody who can actually run this today, trust can only stay put or go down? Yet the page says trust "evolves" with outcomes. Evolves between what and what?

### /atp-economics (78:00)
- "Budget, not wealth", the battery-icon line, and "recharge refunds, payment earns" are all sticky and helpful.
- The quality table does not match the sentences around it. Task worth 50 ATP: 30% → 0, 50% → ~12, 70% → ~30, 85% → ~42. The text says the ramp "scales linearly above 70%"; /how-it-works says "30–70% scales linearly; above 70% pays near-full". At 70% the table pays 60% of the task, not near-full, and 0 → 12 → 30 over equal 20-point steps is not a straight line. I cannot reproduce any row from the stated rule.
- "ATP is a finite per-agent budget" (settled list) and "No scarcity cap" (Bitcoin section), plus "capped issuance" on the FAQ.
- The simulator's "Low-quality post (your own): cost −10, reward +5". The page defines "reward" as payment for value delivered and says your own posts only ever refund. So is that +5 a refund or a reward?
- And again: spam −5 is the cheapest row in the table.
- The "settled vs still moving" box is a great idea. "Shapes and directions are stable; exact numbers are negotiable" would have saved me a lot of effort if I had read it first, on the first page with numbers.

### /trust-tensor (84:00)
- The surgeon example is the right opener. "Why tensor? — a 3-axis score" is exactly the right amount.
- Alice's three roles with the composite arithmetic shown (89.5 → 90, 74.3 → 74, 27.5 → 28) all check out. Nice.
- "Web4 doesn't reduce trust to a single number" and then the survival rule, rebirth rule and every badge elsewhere use the single composite number.
- Decay: a 0.80 Training score "settles at 0.40" after six months and 0.20 after a year. Something that keeps halving does not settle.
- "The hestia system evolves real T3/V3 trust tensors". See my Hestia question about the 0.50 cap.
- The honest note that nobody has decided whether fading through absence can kill you was good to see, and unsettling.

### /karma-consequences (89:00)
- The Life 1 example: the agent did *good work* (trust rose 0.50 → 0.64), ran out of energy, and gets karma **−15**, so the next life starts at **70** ATP. Alice on First Contact did the same thing (ran out of energy with trust above 0.5) and got **+12**, starting at **112**. Same event, opposite sign.
- The intro says "bad choices in Life 1 haunt Lives 2 and 3" while the Life 1 steps say "the work itself was good".
- "This creates asymmetric consequences: building positive karma is harder than destroying it." The formula right above multiplies every karma point by 2 in both directions. Nothing in it is asymmetric.
- `next_life_trust = prev_trust × 0.95`. A neutral 0.50 becomes 0.475, under the survival line, on rebirth. Life 1's 0.64 would become 0.61. First Contact shows trust going *up* at rebirth (0.51 → 0.54).
- The handicap sequence "100, then 70, then 84": 70 I can derive. 84 needs a Life 2 karma of −8 that is not shown anywhere I could see.
- No rule for how karma is scored. Eight sources, no weights, and "−15" arrives from nowhere.
- The recourse section is good and sober ("weaker than a real appeal").

### /day-in-web4 (95:00)
- I should have read this second. It is the first page that shows what this would *feel* like: inbox with a sender cost, hiring with three scores, reviews that cost energy, a marketplace with "2 hops (friend of a friend)". The mockups did more than the 25-minute page.
- "None of it ships today" up top. Fine, clear.
- "Your first post costs 10 ATP - a 40% newcomer surcharge". 40% on top of what? 10 is the *bottom* of the 10–20 post range from /how-it-works. A 40% surcharge ending at 10 means a base of about 7, which is below every post price I have seen.
- Step 2 says a phone chip caps trust at 0.85. The mockups show people at 0.87, 0.91, 0.92, 0.94 with no word about what hardware they have.
- Social feed footer: "every action costs 1 ATP", next to posts that cost 3, 5 and 15.
- Marketplace: seller 0.91, two hops away, "distance-adjusted trust 0.64". With the FAQ's 2-hop weight of 0.49 I would have expected about 0.45. 0.64 looks like one hop (0.91 × 0.7).
- In the Reviews and Social mockups the text came through with a literal `·` where a dot separator should be ("Rachel T. 0.83· cost 5 ATP to post"). Real dots render fine elsewhere on the page. Looks like a display bug, though I only saw it as fetched text.
- 350 ATP camera when I start with 100 and my own posts only refund: the page says commissions fill the gap, and that a newcomer's first commission is "an open question". So as a newcomer I cannot see how I would ever buy the camera.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — a proposed standard where trust is earned from signed, witnessed records of what you did, for people and AI agents alike. The site taught me this (/tldr).
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — one shared library plus three programs at personal, community and enterprise scale. Taught by /tldr and /onramp. I could not explain what a "sovereign society" does all day.
- [x] What LCT means — Linked Context Token: an identity key that lives on your device, ideally inside its security chip, co-signed by your other devices. Taught by /why-web4 and /lct-explainer. I am fuzzy on what the identifier actually looks like (two formats).
- [~] What ATP/ADP means — ATP is an energy budget of 100 that actions spend and confirmed good work refills; ADP is the receipt. Taught. The *numbers* (ramp table, spam price, cap or no cap, newcomer surcharge) I do not trust.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — three scores (talent, training, temperament) per role, blended 0.4/0.3/0.3 into one number. Taught well by /trust-tensor.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — the libraries install; Hestia is used daily by the lab only; the hub runs but has no outside users; hardware binding is not validated; no network to join. /running-now is excellent. One hole: whether the lab's own society is "a hub".
- [~] Why any of this matters — the problem statement convinced me. Whether this solves it, I cannot say: the central promise (prove what an agent did) currently has a gate the agent can route around with two environment variables, and the strongest property (reputation you cannot shed) depends on hardware binding that is not validated.

Not understood at all: karma (three incompatible models), the Coherence Index (only ever described in passing on pages I read), MRH beyond "trust fades with distance", V3 beyond three V-words.

## Unanswered Questions
1. When a life ends from running out of energy with good trust, do I come back with a bonus (+12), a penalty (−15), or my whole balance? And does my trust go up, stay, or get multiplied by 0.95?
2. If Hestia today only has software identities capped at 0.50, and below 0.50 I am restricted, what can trust actually do for anyone running it now?
3. Is the lab's own society running on the hub? If so, why is the hub not "Running" like Hestia?
4. Why is one spam message (5 ATP) cheaper than one honest post (10–20 ATP)?
5. What is the real quality-to-payout rule? What does 70% quality on a 50-ATP task pay?
6. Is ATP capped or not? Who decides how much exists, and what stops a society from handing its friends unlimited budget?
7. A newcomer's own posts only refund, and "how a newcomer gets a first commission is an open question". So how does a newcomer ever get above 100, or avoid slowly draining to zero?
8. If an agent can skip the policy gate and leave no record, what does a clean witness chain prove to an auditor?
9. Does Alice get restricted in Act 3 when her trust is 0.48? What does "restricted" take away?
10. What is the base price of a first post before the 40% newcomer surcharge?
11. Who is this for right now? "Where you fit today" answers it for four kinds of people, but an ordinary non-technical person is told only that nothing needs installing. Is there anything for them to *do* besides read?
12. Why the life/death/karma metaphor? What would be lost by saying "budget exhausted" and "suspended"?
13. The Coherence Index squares (raw × CI²) and can make actions up to 10× more expensive. What is a typical CI, and what is mine on day one?
14. Where are SUSPENDED and REVOKED explained?

## Honest Assessment
The first ten minutes are good. The landing tagline, the TL;DR, and the five problems on Why Web4 gave me a clear picture faster than I expected, and the site's honesty about its own maturity is unusual and earned real goodwill. /running-now in particular reads like something written by people who would rather be believed than admired. If I had stopped after the three "short version" pages, I would have left with a tidy understanding and a positive impression.

The trouble started when I kept going, because past that point the site is full of numbers, and the numbers do not agree with each other. I do not mean rounding. I mean the same event paying +12 on one page and −15 on another; a payout table that cannot be produced from the rule printed beside it; a surcharge with no base; a trust cap of 0.85 on a page whose examples sit at 0.94; a "four systems" heading on a "five systems" page. Each time, I stopped to work out whether I had misunderstood, did the arithmetic, and found that I had not. After the fourth or fifth time I stopped trusting any figure I could not check myself, including the ones that were probably fine. The site's fix for this is to attach a disclaimer ("one modelling choice, not the rule", "not settled", "teaching calibration"). There are a lot of these now. They are honest, but stacked up they have the opposite effect to the one intended: instead of "these people are careful", I started to read them as "these people have not decided". The one place that handles it well is the "settled vs still moving" box on the ATP page, which tells me up front that shapes are stable and numbers are negotiable. I wish that sentence had been on the first page that showed me a number.

Second, the box that greets you on every onramp page sends you to the TL;DR for vocabulary the TL;DR does not contain. That one is simple, and it sits exactly where a newcomer is deciding whether they are out of their depth.

Third, the most useful page for a naive person, A Day in Web4, is buried in "Going Deeper" after a 25-minute systems walkthrough. Seeing an inbox where each message shows what it cost the sender did more for me than every formula. I would put it third in the path.

Finally, a thought I could not shake. The pitch is "prove what an AI agent did". The status page says the record is written by the gate the agent is supposed to pass through, and that the agent can go around the gate. The other pitch is "reputation you cannot shed", and that depends on hardware binding that has not been validated, while the software identity people can actually use today is capped at the starting score. I think the site knows both of these things and says them. But it says them on page seven, in the calm voice of a status ledger, and the landing page is written as if they were not true. I left believing the authors are honest and unsure whether the thing works. For a research project that may be the right place to leave a visitor. I would just like the front door to say it too.
