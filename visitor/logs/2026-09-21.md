## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00 - 03:00)
- First impression: clear, calm headline. "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules." That sentence stuck, and it's the best thing on the site.
- "To witness is to observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation on the record." Good, a real definition right away.
- Four cards (core standard, hub, hestia, hardbound). The one-line glosses under Hestia ("goddess of the hearth") and Hardbound ("as in hardware-bound") helped.
- It told me to read in order: 2-minute intro, Why Web4, First Contact. I did.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (/tldr) (03:00 - 09:00)
- Clearly more than 2 minutes, but it's readable. The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 comparison is the clearest part of the whole site.
- The badge key (Spec / Reference / Running) is useful. Hestia is the only "Running" one, and the page says right away that the "daily use" is the lab's own. I appreciated that.
- "your own device is your day-zero witness ... with software alone it is a key on your machine, which starts you the same way and carries less weight." Remember this sentence. Later pages turn "carries less weight" into something much harsher (see /onramp and /lct-explainer).
- "Unresettable reputation is narrower than it sounds" is honest.

### Why Web4? (09:00 - 25:00)
- The problem section is excellent: spam, reputation stuck on one platform, unlimited fresh starts, AI agents. Each gets a "Root cause" line. This is the part that sold me on the problem.
- "Why previous solutions failed" with "What it gets right" for each: fair and convincing.
- The four idea cards were good until I hit a contradiction inside the same section:
  - Card 4 (MRH): "Everyone stays visible - what changes is weight. A stranger's message reaches you, but it counts for little."
  - Six lines later, "All four, in one moment": "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply at all - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread."
  - The filter-bubble FAQ further down agrees with card 4 ("MRH limits influence, not visibility"). The privacy FAQ sides with the moment ("A spammer with no trust connections can't even see your data"). So: can strangers see me or not? I really don't know.
- "If not crypto, what backs ATP?" FAQ lists **"Capped issuance: A community mints a finite ATP pool ... ATP matters exactly because it's limited."** Keep that in mind for /atp-economics.
- The "72 questions" index is intimidating, but at least it's labeled "reference depth, not a read-through".

### First Contact (25:00 - 35:00)
- The static story of Alice is easy to follow. Why start at 0.50 ("prove yourself") is nicely explained.

### How It Works (35:00 - 50:00)
- Long, but the "Key Takeaways" box up top is good.
- **Contradiction on out-of-neighborhood actions:** The MRH card says "Spam arrives with zero weight, and sending it still costs energy." Five lines later, in the aliveness diagram, step 2 says "Actions outside your MRH don't cost energy and don't move trust." A spammer messaging strangers is by definition acting outside their neighborhood. So does it cost energy or not? The whole anti-spam argument depends on the answer.
- Step 2 of the same diagram: "MRH - ... Only see what's relevant to your trust network". That's the visibility question from /why-web4 again.
- **Reading costs ATP.** "Effectively free (1-2 ATP)... a read costs about 1 ATP and viewing content about 2." I start with 100. Reading 50 posts at 2 ATP each uses it all up. Reading earns nothing back, and self-initiated posts refund "0 net at best". So a newcomer who lurks to learn the community before posting can starve from reading alone? The heading says "effectively free", but the arithmetic says no.
- Karma lives: "Life 2 ... Ended with 130 ATP. Life 3: Reborn with 130 ATP." Then under "Why This Design Works": "Quality Compounds ... Each life starts stronger than the last." Life 3 started *weaker* (130) than Life 2 (145). The page's own example contradicts its headline.
- Quality ramp: "below 30% quality the task pays zero ... A high-quality contribution can earn back up to ~7× what an equally-priced low-effort one earns." If low-effort earns zero, the ratio is infinite, not 7×. (/atp-economics explains that 7× is 42/6 at 35% quality, so it depends on picking 35% as "low-effort".)
- Trust deltas (+0.007 per action) vs Alice's +0.02 to +0.03 per step. The page flags the demo as "illustrative", which is fair.

### ATP Economics (55:00 - 63:00)
- The 60-second version is good. The ATP/ADP biology nod is cute and explained.
- **Direct contradiction with /why-web4:** "No scarcity cap, no mining." Later: "there is no protocol-level constraint on what a society issues ... A recurring allowance is a choice a society is free to write into its law." But /why-web4 says ATP is scarce *because of* "Capped issuance: A community mints a finite ATP pool ... ATP matters exactly because it's limited." One page says it's capped and that's why it matters. The other says there's no cap and that's by design.
- **Quality ramp described two ways on the same page:**
  - "Settled" box: below ~30% earns ~zero, above ~70% earns ~full, linear in between.
  - Two screens later: "The ramp starts at 30% quality (below that, zero payment) and scales linearly above 70%."
  - The table (task worth 50): 70% → ~30, 85% → ~42. If "above 70% earns ~full", 70% should already be near 50. The numbers follow neither description.
  - Sam at 35% "earns back ~6". A straight line from 0 at 30% to 30 at 70% gives ~4 at 35%.
- Point 4 of the 60-second version: "high-quality work recharges fully, up to what you spent." But Hannah at 85% quality spends 50 and gets back 42, which is not full.
- The page splits "recharge refunds" from "payment earns", then runs the same quality ramp over both the payment table ("a task worth 50") and the refund example (Sam and Hannah "earns back"). Which channel does the ramp belong to? I finished unsure.
- It openly says "How someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question." OK, but put that next to "self-initiated work only refunds" and "reading costs 1 ATP", and it means a brand-new member has no known way to avoid slowly running out. It's the biggest hole I found, and the site admits it. Good honesty, but it's scattered across three pages and I had to put it together myself.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed standard for provable, witnessed actions by humans and AI agents, with trust earned from behavior.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one set of libraries, three scales (personal, community, enterprise).
- [x] What LCT means: a device-held cryptographic identity with a stable fingerprint and a changeable label.
- [~] What ATP/ADP means: I get "energy budget + receipt". I do NOT understand whether ATP is capped/scarce, the exact quality ramp, or how a newcomer stays alive.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: Talent/Training/Temperament per role, blended 0.4/0.3/0.3.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: Running Now is very clear.
- [~] Why any of this matters: the problem framing convinced me. Whether the mechanism works, I'm less sure after the arithmetic.

## Unanswered Questions
1. Is a society's ATP pool capped or not? (/why-web4 and /atp-economics disagree.)
2. Does sending a message to someone outside my trust neighborhood cost energy?
3. Can strangers see my posts, or only people in my neighborhood?
4. If hestia identities are software-only and capped at 0.50 (= the survival line), what can a hestia user actually do? Are they permanently "restricted" after one bad step?
5. How does a brand-new member, who only does self-initiated work and reads to learn, avoid running out of ATP?
6. What is karma actually computed from? Why does a 0.64-trust death get -15 and a 0.51-trust death get +12?
7. Does trust go up or down across a rebirth?
8. What exactly does the quality ramp pay at 70%?
9. Who receives my GPS and heartbeat data when my devices "witness" me?
10. If the witness chain can be bypassed with two environment variables, what does "proving what an AI agent did" mean today?

## Honest Assessment
This is a very honest site. More than once a page told me the exact weakness I was about to ask about: no live network, the chain only records governed activity, software identities are capped, the first commission is an open problem, hardware binding is unvalidated. That honesty earned my trust, and the problem framing on /why-web4 is excellent.

The trouble is the numbers. Every page has a worked example, and when I did the arithmetic across pages, the examples disagreed: capped vs uncapped ATP, energy vs no energy outside your neighborhood, visible vs invisible to strangers, karma bonus vs penalty, trust up vs down after rebirth, a quality ramp with two shapes and a table that fits neither, and "each life starts stronger" next to a life that starts weaker. Many are flagged with "this is one modelling choice, not the rule". After the fifth such disclaimer I stopped knowing which numbers to believe, and I started reading the disclaimers as the real content.

The deeper thing I pieced together myself, and wish one page had said outright: today the only running piece gives you a software identity that is capped at the same number that is also the survival line. Self-started work can at best break even, reading costs energy, and nobody knows how a newcomer gets paid work. Each fact is disclosed somewhere. Put together, it reads as "a newcomer today can't get ahead", and I'd rather be told that directly than assemble it.

I'd come back to see whether the contradictions get fixed. I'd send it to a technical friend with "read Why Web4 and Running Now, skip the numbers".
