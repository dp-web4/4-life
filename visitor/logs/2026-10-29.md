## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: clean, confident. "A proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules." I understood that sentence on the first read, which is rare for a project page.
- The "New to Web4? Read in this order" box told me exactly what to click. I liked that.
- Four cards (core standard, hub, hestia, hardbound). I didn't know what "substrate" or "sovereign Web4 society" meant yet, but the cards said "Read more", so I assumed I'd learn.
- The "Where this actually stands" block was the thing that made me keep reading: "research and development, not a finished product". Nobody says that on their own homepage.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".
- Confusion points: "witnessed behavior" — witnessed by whom? "To witness is to observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation on the record." So what happens to the witness if the record turns out to be wrong? Not answered here.

### /tldr — Web4 in 2 Minutes (00:03)
- Took me about 5 minutes, not 2. Still, it earned the time.
- The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast was the clearest thing on the site. "Delete the account and it is gone" vs "whoever holds the key is assumed to be the owner" vs "other participants observe what you do and sign a small record of it." Good.
- The three badges (Spec, Reference, Running) are introduced here. Then the sentence "A piece can carry more than one... the core standard below is both Spec and Reference." But Spec was defined two lines earlier as "Written down and stable, **not yet built here**" and Reference as "**built** and runnable". So the core standard is both not-yet-built and built? I re-read it three times. /running-now later explained the intended meaning (spec written AND primitives built), but /tldr's own definition of Spec forbids the combination it then asserts.
- "Unresettable reputation is narrower than it sounds: it is a property of hardware anchoring, not of Web4 as such." This is the first appearance of what became the recurring theme of my whole visit: every strong claim has a software-only asterisk. Good that it's here; it's going to come up a lot.
- Clicked: "Why Web4?"

### /why-web4 (00:09)

- "Why Previous Solutions Failed" with "what it gets right" for each — fair, not strawmanned.
- The four mechanism cards (Energy Budget, Device-Anchored Identity, Multi-Dimensional Trust, Trust Neighborhood) each gave me a plain-English name before the acronym. This worked.

Six lines later, "All four, in one moment" says "only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply at all - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread." ## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — a proposed standard where trust comes from signed records of witnessed actions, not from a platform or a key
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — one vocabulary, three sizes (me / my group / my company)
- [x] What LCT means — an identity key on my own device, stronger if the device has a security chip, witnessed by my other devices
- [~] What ATP/ADP means — energy budget spent to act, receipt when spent, recharged by confirmations. I understood the shape. I could not tell you whether the supply is capped, or what the quality ramp pays at 70%
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — three numbers per role (talent, training, temperament), blended 0.4/0.3/0.3
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — /running-now did this well; hestia runs, hub is runnable, hardbound is real but unvalidated on hardware, federation is paper
- [~] Why any of this matters — the problems page convinced me. The "proving what an agent did" promise then got qualified by Risk 9 to "proving what an agent did through the gate", which is a different promise

## Honest Assessment

This site is more honest than any project site I can remember. It says "R&D, not a product" on the homepage, it tells me its own safety gate can be bypassed with two environment variables, it tells me which numbers are made up for teaching and which are from the standard, and it flags when its own pages disagree. I want to be clear that this is rare and good.

It is also the reason the site is exhausting. Honesty on this site has become a way of not deciding. Four separate places tell me "how much karma carries is not settled, read for the shape, not the amount". But the pages are built out of amounts. First Contact is a number-driven demo. The karma page has a formula with named constants. How It Works has three worked lives. When all of those produce different numbers for the same event and every one of them carries a note saying "don't read the number", the notes don't cancel the numbers. They just teach me that the numbers on this site are decorative. After that I stopped checking arithmetic, which is probably the opposite of what the site wants from a reader.

The three things that actually broke my understanding, as opposed to tiring it, were all cases where a page contradicts itself or its neighbour on a mechanism, not a number: whether strangers beyond the horizon can see me (visibility vs weight), whether acting outside the horizon costs energy, and whether CI has a death line.
