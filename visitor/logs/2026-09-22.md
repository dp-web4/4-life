## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest, and that honesty is what kept me reading for an hour and a half. "Nobody adopts it: Existential, High likelihood" as the first listed risk, "the deployment counted here is the lab's own," and "research takes as long as it takes" are sentences I do not see on project sites. The reading path works: by page five I could explain Web4 to someone else in a paragraph. The two-minute page, the problems half of Why Web4, Hestia's "ordinary Tuesday," and Running Now are the strongest pages.

Where it lost me was numbers. Not the existence of numbers, which help, but the site's habit of stating a specific figure and then, a paragraph later or a page later, stating a different one and adding a note that neither is the rule. Visibility outside the neighborhood, whether out-of-neighborhood actions cost energy, whether ATP is finite, what karma does at rebirth, whether the lab runs a hub: on each of these I found two site passages that disagree, usually with both written carefully. A careful reader notices careful contradictions more, not less.

If I were told to fix one thing, it would be the MRH visibility question, because it is the mechanism the site uses to explain why spam dies, and I left not knowing whether a spammer's message arrives with zero weight or never arrives at all. The second would be the hub: "we run one" and "not yet deployed" cannot both stand on a site whose whole pitch is not overstating what is real.

I would come back, mostly to see whether the contradictions get resolved, which is itself a compliment to the site's stated method.
