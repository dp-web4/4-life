### Web4 in 2 Minutes — /tldr (01:00)
- Best page on the site. Problem (agents act, nobody can prove what they did), shift (trust is earned through witnessed behaviour), four pieces, honest status. "Good behavior compounds. Bad behavior follows you." stuck with me.

### Why Web4? — /why-web4 (05:00)
- The five problems (spam, siloed reputation, unlimited fresh starts, AI making it worse, platforms control everything) are ones I already feel. Each has a "root cause" line, which is good writing.
- The four ideas are introduced with plain names first (energy budget, device-anchored identity, multi-dimensional trust, trust neighborhood) and the acronym second. That works.
- Then the page starts contradicting itself:
  - The Trust Neighborhood idea says "Everyone stays visible - what changes is weight." A few lines later the worked forum example says only people inside the asker's neighborhood see the reply and "a stranger five hops away never sees the thread." The FAQ then says everyone can see your messages. So do strangers see my reply or not?
  - The worked example ends by pricing the reply at "fractions of a cent of energy". Later pages insist ATP has no price against money and cannot be bought or sold. So where does "a cent" come from?
- The FAQ says there are 72 questions. I read the 12 "most asked" and they are good and candid ("research prototype with substantial working code, not production", "email in the 1980s").
- More things that did not line up inside the FAQ:
  - "What backs ATP?" lists "capped issuance" as a source of scarcity. The ATP page later says "No scarcity cap" and that the protocol does not constrain issuance.
  - Adoption tiers: one answer says "Tiers 0-3 are reversible, only Tier 4 is permanent"; the list next to it numbers the tiers 1 to 5.
  - How many people does a community need? "As few as 5-10" in one answer, "3-5 people is the floor" in another, and the risk page later has a third set of bands (5-10, 10-30, 50+ versus 5-10, 20-50, 100+ here).
  - The 0.5 threshold "draws from phase transition mathematics" and below it behaviour is "statistically indistinguishable from random". The very next answer says almost every number is "calibrated, not derived" and 0.5 is a design choice. One of those is overselling.
- Jargon left unexplained in answers I read: EK certificate chain, TPM, AGPL, WASM, Sybil is explained but "gas fees" is not.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level) — a proposed open standard for making actions by people and AI agents provable and for earning trust through witnessed behaviour. Taught by /tldr.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together — one shared vocabulary plus three sizes to run it at. Taught by /tldr and /onramp. Still unsure whether a hub is actually running anywhere.
- [x] What LCT means — Linked Context Token, an identity that lives on my device, ideally in its security chip, vouched for by my other devices. Taught.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means — an energy budget I spend to act (ATP) and the receipt left behind (ADP) that confirmations turn back into energy. Taught as a concept; I could not reproduce any of the numbers.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are — three scores (Talent, Training, Temperament) per role instead of one global score. Taught well.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D — the site's strongest point. Taught by /running-now.
- [x] Why any of this matters — yes for AI-agent accountability (the /tldr framing). Less convinced by the "fix social media spam" framing on /why-web4, since nothing consumer-facing exists.

Partial / inferred rather than taught: V3 (I know its three words, not how it works), Coherence Index (a consistency score that multiplies trust; never read its page), MRH visibility rule (pages disagree), karma (three versions).

3. If I run out of energy after doing good work, am I rewarded (+12) or penalised (-15) next time?
4. What ends a "life" if I never hit zero energy and my trust is fine? Why is there a life cycle at all?

7. How does a brand-new person get their first paid commission, given that is the only way to end up ahead?
8. Why is spam the cheapest thing on every price list?

## Honest Assessment
The site is unusually honest and I ended up trusting the people behind it more than I trust most project sites. Nearly every page says what is not built, not tested, or not settled, and the "What Could Go Wrong" and "Running Now" pages are the best things here. After the two-minute page I could explain Web4 to a friend: AI agents and people act online, nobody can prove what they did or whether they should be trusted, and this is a proposed standard where identity sits on your device, actions cost a small energy budget, and trust is earned per role from what others witness you doing.

Where it falls down is the numbers. The ideas are clear in words and then almost every page attaches specific figures that do not agree with each other or with the page's own formulas: the karma sign, the Novice's trust climb, the quality ramp, the spam price, the reading cost, the rebirth rules. Individually each has a disclaimer ("one modelling choice", "teaching calibration", "not settled"). Collectively the disclaimers add up to: none of the worked examples can be checked. I would rather have one small example that closes than ten that each need a footnote. A reader who checks arithmetic will stop trusting the mechanics around the middle of How It Works, and that is a shame because the honesty elsewhere earns a lot of goodwill.

The second problem is that the site answers the same question differently on different pages, and visibility in the trust neighborhood is the worst case because it changes what the system *is*: a place where strangers cannot see my posts, or a place where they can but discount them.

The third is audience drift. The first three pages are written for me. From Running Now onward the pages are increasingly written for someone who will install a Rust binary, and the game-like vocabulary (lives, death, karma, rebirth, synthons) sits oddly next to deployment notes about loopback planes and TPM attestation. I was never sure whether I was reading about a product for my organisation's AI agents or a simulation of a society.

Would I come back? If I ran AI agents, yes, for hestia; that page showed me something concrete. As a general reader I got the idea in ten minutes and the next hour mostly taught me how much is still open, which the site would probably say is the correct thing to have learned.
