## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: I knew what this was within about eight seconds, which is rare. "Trust, built into the internet" plus "AI agents already take real actions: they write code, move money, and make decisions. Today we cannot prove what an agent did, on whose authority, or by what rules. Web4 closes that gap." That is a real problem stated in words I already own. The definition of "witness" is given inline in the same paragraph, before I could wonder about it. Good.
- The line I trusted most on the whole page: "Web4 is research and development, not a finished product." A site that leads with its own limits earns a lot of patience from me.
- Clicked: the explicit instruction "New to Web4? Read in this order: the 2-minute intro, Why Web4, then First Contact." I did exactly that. Being told the order removed all the anxiety about where to start.
- Confusion points: the nav menu has 28 links in four groups. I ignored it entirely because the reading path told me to. Had that sentence not been there I would have bounced.

### /tldr — Web4 in 2 Minutes (02:10)
- The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast is the single best thing on this site. "Web2: a platform declares your reputation. Delete the account and it is gone." "Web3: whoever holds the key is assumed to be the rightful owner." "Web4: trust accrues from witnessed behavior." Three sentences and I understood the entire pitch. Nothing else I read all session taught me as much per word.

### /why-web4 (05:40)
- The four problems (spam, siloed reputation, unlimited fresh starts, AI making it worse) landed. Problem before solution, exactly as promised.
- Then I hit the FAQ, and I hit a wall. I counted roughly seventy questions. The reading path budgets this page at "6 min + optional Q&A." That is not a Q&A, that is a second website.
- Worse, it repeats itself. "What about people who can't afford devices with security chips?" appears, and later "What about device affordability?" appears again, and later "Can't afford TPM chips?" a third time. "Can't someone with lots of hardware create many identities?" and "Multiple hardware = multiple IDs?" are the same question. "What if my device is stolen?" appears twice. "Who runs the infrastructure?" appears twice. After the third repeat I stopped trusting that scrolling further would show me anything new, and I started skimming, which means I probably missed things that mattered.
- The answers themselves are good. "No timeline, and that's intentional. Honest research doesn't ship deadlines" is a great answer. So is the social-credit one.
- **The thing that actually confused me**: this page told me twice, in plain words, "Creating one LCT requires a physical device with a security chip (TPM or Secure Enclave)." I read "requires." I concluded: no chip, no Web4. I felt a bit shut out, since I do not know whether my machine has one. Then later on the same page a stray line said "A software-only LCT has no hardware-fingerprint binding... the cost is a lower trust ceiling." So it is not required after all? I could not tell which sentence to believe, and this is the most basic question a newcomer has: can I do this or not?

### /lct-explainer (37:00)
- "Imagine your phone becoming your driver's license, but one that can't be photocopied, faked from a stolen photo, or impersonated from a remote computer." Best analogy on the site. I understood LCT from this one sentence.
- The ceiling table is clear: TPM 0.90, Secure Enclave 0.85, FIDO2 key 0.75, software only 0.50.
- Confusion point: this page settles the question /why-web4 confused me about (software-only is possible), but it does not tell me what a 0.50 cap actually costs me in practice. ### /trust-tensor (40:20)
- T3 as Talent, Training, Temperament, scored per role, is a genuinely nice idea and the decay rules sold it: "Talent doesn't decay at all, demonstrated aptitude is durable," Training 180-day half-life, Temperament 30-day half-life. That asymmetry feels right and made the model believable.
- Then I hit the thing that stopped me. This page says "**0.5** = newcomer baseline." It also says "Crossing below 0.5 restricts your features right away and is recoverable; only staying below it is fatal." And it says "a software-only identity tops out at 0.50 however good the work."
- So: 0.50 is where everyone starts. 0.50 is the line where features get restricted. And 0.50 is the permanent ceiling for anyone without a security chip. Three different jobs, one number, and the page never puts them in the same paragraph.
- What that means, if I've done the arithmetic right, is that a software-only person can never rise even one point above the number they were handed on day one. Not "a lower ceiling." No reputation at all, ever, no matter how good the work. And they sit permanently on the exact line where a bad day restricts their features.
- I want to be clear that the site never told me this. I inferred it by holding three sentences from two pages next to each other. If I am right, it is the single most important fact on the site for anyone deciding whether to bother. If I am wrong, I have no idea how I was supposed to find that out.

### /value-tensor (44:00)
- V3 scoring the work rather than the worker is a clean distinction and "Reputation tells you whether to trust a person. It doesn't tell you whether a particular thing they made is any good" earns it in one line.
- Valuation 30 / Veracity 35 / Validity 35, with the note that "Veracity + Validity (0.70 combined) outweigh Valuation (0.30). Web4 rewards truth and rigor over popularity by design." That is a real design position stated plainly, and I liked it.
- The three-different-scorers answer to "who stops manipulation" was satisfying: recipient scores usefulness, witnesses score truth, the system verifies delivery, so "no single party controls all three dimensions."

### /atp-economics (46:30)
- ATP as Allocation Transfer Packets, ADP as Allocation Discharge Packets, with the biology borrowed openly. The receipt framing for ADP is good: "You never manually manage ADP; it's background accounting." That removed a whole category of worry.
- The quality ramp is clear: under 30 percent earns roughly nothing, 30 to 70 scales linearly, above 70 is roughly full return.
- **And then the worked example broke my model of the whole system.** Sam does 35 percent quality work, spends 50 ATP, earns back about 6, net minus 44. Hannah does 85 percent quality work, spends 50 ATP, earns back about 42, **net minus 8**. Hannah is the hero of the example. Hannah did excellent work. **Hannah still lost energy.**
- The page is explicit that this is by design: recharge "refund at most what you spent." So no amount of quality on my own initiated work ever nets me anything. The only net-positive channel is "payment" for tasks other people commission. But their ATP came from the same place mine did.
- I went looking for where new energy enters. The page says "It's a starter grant from the society's pool when you join, not printed from thin air" and "Each society mints a fixed pool when it forms." A fixed pool. Plus five percent skimmed off every transfer. So as far as I can tell from this page: energy only ever moves sideways or drains, everyone is net negative even doing great work, and eventually everybody hits zero and dies.
- That cannot be the intent, but the page never says what I'm getting wrong. This is the largest unanswered question I left with.

### /trust-neighborhood (49:00)
- MRH as "Markov Relevancy Horizon," everyday name "Trust Neighborhood," and "you see your connections, their connections, and their connections, nothing more." Understood instantly. Degrees of separation is a concept I already had.
- The numbers are clean: 0.70 at one hop, 0.49 at two, 0.34 at three, zero beyond.
- Small wobble: the page also says reference models use "roughly 0.85 after one hop." It does explain that both converge by depth 3-4, which I appreciated, but I still could not tell which of the two is the answer if someone asked me.

### /karma-consequences (50:30)
- This retroactively fixed my alarm from First Contact. "What is permanent is the identity, not the penalty" is the reassurance I needed four pages earlier. ATP exhaustion means unable to act, but trust "keeps recovering and decaying over time (on the order of weeks to months)."
- The karma numbers are concrete: karma multiplier of 2, trust carries over at 0.95, minus 15 karma means starting the next life at 70 ATP instead of 100.

"Nobody adopts it" listed first, severity existential, likelihood high. "Witness governance is one of the hardest unsolved problems." The residual device-loss case "is genuinely unsolved and an active area of research." Nobody writes this unless they mean it.
- Risk 8 is where I finally found, in plain words, the thing I had spent twenty minutes inferring: "**Software-only users hit a permanent 0.50 trust ceiling.**" And it goes further than I had: "Device loss is unrecoverable for this tier." And it refuses to claim the mitigations work: "Designing a hardware-anchored trust system that does not structurally disadvantage users... is an unsolved problem, not a solved one."
- I am genuinely glad this sentence exists. But it is on the page I read thirteenth, under a heading about disability, and it contradicts the softer framing on the two pages I read first. The honest version of this fact is buried under the reassuring version.
- Risk 9 stopped me cold. On Hestia, the one piece carrying the **Running** badge, "two environment variables are enough to get past" the policy gate, and this is "an accepted limit today, not a scheduled repair." I went back to /hestia and confirmed the page says it there too, along with something even sharper: "an action that routes around the gate writes no entry at all. Your witness chain is a record of governed activity, not a record of all activity."
- I want to give real credit here: saying that out loud, on the product page and not just the risk page, is more honesty than I have ever seen a project volunteer. But it sits at right angles to the sentence that got me in the door. The landing page told me "Today we cannot prove what an agent did, on whose authority, or by what rules. Web4 closes that gap." On the only piece that is actually running, an agent can decline to be recorded. I do not think the landing page is lying. I think nothing on the path from that promise to the one running implementation warned me the promise does not hold there yet, and I had to reach page thirteen to find out.

## Unanswered Questions
1. If even 85 percent quality work is net negative on ATP, and the society pool is fixed, where does new energy come from? Does the pool get topped up, and if so by what?
2. Can a software-only participant ever rise above 0.50, by any route other than buying hardware? If not, what is the actual point of participating at that tier?
3. Does a brand-new member, sitting at exactly the 0.50 baseline, have restricted features? Is exactly 0.50 above the line or below it?
4. If Hestia's witness chain is "a record of governed activity, not a record of all activity," what stops an agent from simply not being governed? What would make the chain complete rather than partial?
5. Which decay rate is the real one, 0.7 per hop or 0.85?
6. Is "pilot-ready" a stronger or weaker claim than the Reference badge?
7. What are the literal commands for the 30-second identity mint?

## Honest Assessment

This is the most intellectually honest site I have ever been sent to. That is not a compliment I hand out. A whole page enumerating nine ways the project could fail, several graded flatly unsolved, with adoption listed first at "existential / high," and a product page that volunteers that its own safety gate can be defeated by two environment variables. Most projects would bury that in an issue tracker. Putting it under a heading I was invited to click is the reason I would recommend this site even though almost nothing here is deployed.

The teaching is good too. The reading path removed the anxiety of a 28-link nav. /tldr taught me more per word than anything else. The driver's-license line for LCT and the degrees-of-separation line for MRH both worked on the first read. "Concrete before abstract, problem before solution" is not just stated on /learn, it is actually done.

So my criticism is narrow, and it is one shape repeated three times: **the honest version of a fact keeps living further down the path than the reassuring version.**

Software-only identity gets called "a lower trust ceiling" on page nine and "a permanent 0.50 trust ceiling" on page thirteen. Hestia's gate gets a Running badge on page six and a two-environment-variable bypass on page twelve. The landing page says Web4 closes the agent-accountability gap, and page thirteen says that on the one running piece, an agent that routes around the gate leaves no record at all. In every case the truthful sentence exists, and in every case I reached it after I had already formed the wrong impression from an earlier page. A reader who stops at page nine, which is most readers, leaves with a picture the site itself disagrees with.

That is a much better problem to have than dishonesty. It is a sequencing problem, and the fix is small: move the sharp clause up to where the soft claim is made. The site has already done the hard part, which is being willing to say the sharp thing at all.

The one place I think something is actually missing rather than misplaced is the ATP economics. The worked example is the most concrete, most memorable thing on that page, and what it demonstrates is that the site's best-case worker loses energy. Every other page tells me quality earns energy back. I do not think both can be true, and I could not find the sentence that resolves it. I left the site able to explain what ATP is and unable to explain why it does not run out.

I would come back. I want to know how the ATP question resolves, and I want to see whether Hestia's gate gets moved out of the agent's process. Those are the two things I would check on a return visit, which is probably the best signal I can give: I left with specific curiosity rather than vague good feeling.
