## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: clear and calm. "Trust, built into the internet." The subtitle says it is a proposed open standard for proving what an AI agent did, on whose authority, and by what rules. I understood that sentence on first read.
- "Witness" is defined right away (observe an action and attest to it, staking your own reputation). Good.
- The four cards (Core Standard, Hub, Hestia, Hardbound) are tidy, but each one is dense: "sovereign Web4 society", "signed founding law", "append-only witnessed ledger", "cryptographic LCT", "scoped revocable delegation". I could not have told you what any card does for me.
- "Where this actually stands" is the best paragraph on the page. It tells me this is R&D, not a product.
- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes".
- Confusion points: the page is about AI agents. Hold that thought, because the next pages are about something else.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes /tldr (03:00)
- This worked. Problem, shift (Web2 platform declares, Web3 key assumed, Web4 witnessed), four pieces, honest status. The badge key (Spec / Reference / Running) is explained in plain words, including that "Running" only means the lab's own daily use.
- I liked the caveat that unresettable reputation is "narrower than it sounds".
- Confusion: "day-zero witness" being my own device. If my own device vouches for me, why would anybody else believe it? The page says software-only "carries less weight" but I did not yet know less than what.
- It took me more like 4 minutes than 2.

### Why Web4? /why-web4 (08:00)
- The topic changed under me. The landing page and /tldr were about proving what AI agents did. This page is about spam, bots, trolls, reputation trapped on platforms, shadowbans. That is a social media pitch. Both are interesting, but I now do not know which product I am reading about: an audit trail for AI agents, or a replacement for how forums and social networks handle reputation.
- The five problems are well written and I recognised all of them.
- "This Is What Web4 Proposes" introduces ATP, LCT, T3, V3, MRH and CI inside about a screen. Six acronyms. MRH is expanded as "Markov Relevancy Horizon" and then I am told it is "math-flavored jargon you can ignore". If I can ignore it, why show it to me?
- The forum-reply example ends by saying it costs "fractions of a cent of energy". Two pages later I am told ATP has no price against money at all. So what is a cent of energy?
- The FAQ: "Browse all 72 questions". Every answer is collapsed. The reading path calls this page 6 minutes. The 12 "Most asked" answers alone are longer than that.
- Good answers I read: "Is this real, or just an idea?" (Both), the social credit comparison table, "What if I lose my hardware?".
- Bad moment: the lost-hardware answer says recovery is "built in" via my other devices, and the deployed-anywhere answer says multi-device enrollment has "not yet been driven on a real second device". So the recovery I was just reassured with has never been tried on two real devices.
- "How does the system decide who can see my messages?" answers "Everyone can see them." Later the LCT page's privacy table says "only recipients see content". I could not square those.
- The ATP answer lists "capped issuance" as a reason ATP is scarce. Remember that for the ATP page.

### First Contact /first-contact (22:00)
- The setup text is careful: two numbers move (energy, trust), energy starts at 100, trust at 0.50, below 0.50 is restricted, staying below is fatal.
- I could not run the walkthrough, so all I had was the act map: 0.50 → 0.56 → 0.48 → 0.62 → dies at ATP 0 with trust 0.51 → "+12 bonus → 112" → reborn at 0.54.

I appreciate the honesty, but a first-time reader is being told, on the very first story, that the ending is one of several versions. I did not know what to take away.
- "Death" and "rebirth" for a forum account is a strange metaphor. The page says "Suspended, not deleted", which helped. But if it is a suspension, why call it death? And who or what is "reborn"? Same person, same device, same record. It reads like a game mechanic, not an internet proposal.

### How It Works /how-it-works (34:00)
- 25 minutes is accurate. It is long but it is the page where the model finally hangs together: identity, then energy, trust and consistency in a loop, bounded by your neighbourhood.
- The banner "No network you can join yet - the present tense below is the model talking" is excellent. I wish it were on every concept page.
- Then the numbers started fighting each other:
  - Key takeaways say "Five interlocking systems". The section "How The Pieces Fit Together" opens "Web4 has four core systems" and its diagram leaves one out.
  - Spam costs 5 ATP in the price list. A post costs 10 to 20. So spamming is the cheapest thing you can do besides reading. The page's whole argument is that spam is expensive.
  - In the "Death Spiral" example spam is "10 ATP per low-quality post", not 5.
  - "Actions outside your MRH don't cost energy and don't move trust." Two cards earlier: spam arrives with zero weight "but sending it still costs energy". Which?
  - Life 2 "ATP fluctuated 80-180" and in the same list "dropped to 15".
  - Lives 1, 2 and 3 end with 145, 130 and 165 ATP. The page itself admits none of these is a death by either of the two ways to die it just taught me, and "declines to name a third cause". So why did these lives end? I spent a while on this and the page's answer is "not settled".
  - Trust death is described as "recoverable if you built trust, permanent if not". But trust death is the state of not having trust. I think that phrase belongs to energy death and got attached to both.
- The plagiarism walkthrough (Dr. Chen, 40% overlap, 30-day suspension, Training −0.15, appeal) was the most concrete thing on the site and I followed all of it. Then: the appeal process "hasn't been tested with real humans yet".
- "What Prevents Unfair Rules?" lists federation and leaving with your trust as protections. Bottom of the page: federation is "Spec only, specified but not yet built". So one of the four protections does not exist in code.
- The Dive Deeper card for risks says "7 risks, 4 tensions". The risks page has 9 and 9.

- Two example LCTs in two formats: `lct:web4:0x4c8f…a3f2#alice.assistant1` ("names just two things") and `lct:web4:agent:alice.assistant1@Thor#perception` (six segments, "any missing segment means rejected"). By the second rule the first example would be rejected.

### ATP /atp-economics (70:00)
- "The 60-second version" is good and "recharge refunds, payment earns" is the single most useful sentence about ATP anywhere on the site. It corrected the impression every earlier page gave me, that good posts earn energy. They do not; they get your own spend back at best. You only come out ahead when someone commissions you.
- That changes the story a lot, and it leaves a hole the page admits: how does a newcomer get a first commission? "An open question."
- The quality table does not match its own rule. Rule: below 30% earns zero, above 70% earns "~full", linear in between. Table for a 50 ATP task: 30% → 0, 50% → ~12, 70% → ~30, 85% → ~42. Full would be 50. Halfway up a linear ramp would be 25, not 12. And then "scales linearly above 70%", which is a different rule again. The headline "7×" comes from 42 ÷ 6, where the 6 is Sam at 35%, a row that is not in the table.
- "No scarcity cap, no mining" on this page. "Capped issuance" in the /why-web4 FAQ. "ATP is a finite per-agent budget" in the settled list. I do not know if ATP is limited or not.
- The simulator's price list again has "Send spam message −5" as the cheapest action, cheaper than a low-quality post (−10 with +5 back, so the same net −5). Spam and a weak honest post cost the same.
- ADP is explained well: a receipt, you never touch it.
- Six sections named in the page's own jump list (At Scale, Agent's Journey, Earn ATP Back, First 100 ATP, Technical, Why It Matters) were not in what I could read.

- This is where the three stories collide.
  - Here, Life 1: starts 100 ATP and 0.50 trust, ends at ATP 0 with trust 0.64, karma **−15**, reborn with **70** ATP.

  - How It Works: lives end with 145 and are reborn with 145.

## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed standard where trust comes from witnessed, signed records of behaviour rather than from a platform or from holding a key.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one rulebook plus three programs at personal, community and enterprise scale. Taught by the site (/tldr and /onramp).
- [x] What LCT means: Linked Context Token, an identity anchored to a device's security chip and vouched for by your other devices. Taught.
- [x] What ATP/ADP means: Allocation Transfer / Discharge Packets; an energy budget you spend to act, and the receipt that lets it recharge when others confirm value. Taught, but the numbers around it do not agree.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three scores (Talent, Training, Temperament) per role. Taught clearly.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: yes, this is the thing the site does best.
- [ ] Why any of this matters: partly. I got two different answers (AI agent accountability; fixing spam and platform lock-in) and could not tell which is the point.

## Honest Assessment
The site is unusually honest and that is its best quality. Nearly every page tells me what is built, what is only written down, and what nobody has tried. /running-now, /tldr and /what-could-go-wrong are the pages I would show someone. I came away believing the authors are not selling me anything.

The ideas, at the level of words, came through: identity tied to a device, energy you spend to act, trust in three parts per role, a record others sign. I could explain that to a friend.

Where it fell apart was the numbers. Whenever a page gave me a worked example, I could usually find another page, or another paragraph on the same page, that gave a different figure for the same thing: what spam costs, what quality pays, how many systems there are, what happens when you die, whether ATP is capped. Many of these are flagged as "not settled" or "one modelling choice". After the fifth such flag I stopped trusting any number on the site, including the ones that are probably fine. Honesty about uncertainty is good; three different endings to the same story is something else.

The second problem is audience. The landing page speaks to people worried about AI agents. The walkthrough is about Alice posting on a forum and "dying". The software pages are for people who use a terminal. I am none of those people cleanly, and the site does not tell me until /running-now ("you are here to understand it, not to run it") that my job is just to read.

Third, the life/death/karma language. I understand it is a metaphor for a budget running out and a suspension. It still made a serious proposal about accountability read like the rules of a game, and the pages with the most contradictions are exactly the ones that lean on it hardest.

Would I come back? Maybe, to see if a real group ever runs a hub. Would I recommend it? To a developer running AI agents on their own machine, yes: Hestia sounds like a real thing they could try this afternoon. To a general reader, I would send only /tldr and /running-now.
