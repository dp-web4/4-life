## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)
- First impression: "Trust, built into the internet" plus the AI-agents framing landed immediately. The problem statement (we can't prove what an agent did, on whose authority, by what rules) is concrete and current. Good hook.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes — /tldr (03:00)

- The distinction "4-Life is the educational onramp; the onramp itself is four operational pieces" is a bit of a tongue-twister but I got it.
- Lingering question I carried forward: *who* does the witnessing? (Answered later by the LCT page — devices and other participants.)

### Why Web4? — /why-web4 (08:00)
- The problem framing (spam free, reputation siloed, bad actors get infinite fresh starts) is persuasive and plain.
- Dense. Four mechanisms with four acronyms (ATP, LCT, T3/V3, MRH) arrive close together; I coped by holding onto the plain-English glosses (energy budget, device ID, trust score, neighborhood).
- The FAQ is enormous (the index says 72 entries) but honestly organized; the questions are the ones I actually had ("Isn't this a social credit system?", "What if I lose my hardware?").
- The Alice & Bob circular-transfer example taught me a specific convention: send 100, receiver gets 95, send the 95 back, receiver gets 90.25 — the *transferred amount shrinks* each hop. Remember this; it matters later.

### First Contact — /first-contact (18:00)
- The five-act Alice arc is a good teaching device. Two dials (ATP, trust) is the right amount to track.
- **The finale confused me in a way the page never resolves.** Alice overextends and "dies at ATP 0, trust 0.51" — then is "reborn at 0.54" with "+12 bonus → 112" ATP. So the death→rebirth step *improved both her numbers*: 0 → 112 ATP (more than the 100 a newcomer gets) and 0.51 → 0.54 trust. The page fences the magnitude ("How much karma converts is not settled, and 12 is one modelling choice, not the rule") but not the direction. I finished the walkthrough thinking: if hitting zero triggers a grant bigger than the newcomer grant plus a trust bump, why would anyone near zero *not* just spend themselves to death? I went looking for the answer on three more pages (below) and never found it.
- Smaller version of the same confusion: why does trust *rise* through death (0.51 → 0.54)? Karma explains the ATP head start; nothing explains a trust score going up by dying.

### How It Works — /how-it-works (28:00)
- Long but well-ordered. The three-life example (100 ATP → 145 → 130 → 165, trust 0.5 → 0.65 → 0.72 → 0.85) made the lifecycle concrete, and the arithmetic checks out (100 − 60 spent + 105 earned = 145).
- "Recharge refunds, payment earns" — the site's most load-bearing sentence, and it's stated clearly: confirmations on your own work refund at most your cost; only commissioned work nets positive.
- The trust-decay chain (0.9⁵ ≈ 0.59 across five agents) was the first time multi-agent delegation risk clicked for me.
- Mild gripes: "raw trust" vs "effective trust (raw × CI²)" appears before CI is really explained; ADP is listed alongside ATP but only explained later on /atp-economics; "synthons" got one sentence and I still can't tell you what one is.

### ATP Economics — /atp-economics (42:00)

The page says the good-actor rebirth exists and bad actors "die for good," but never prices rebirth or explains why 112 > 100.

### What Could Go Wrong — /what-could-go-wrong (50:00)
- I came here specifically hunting for the death-farming answer via the risk-gaming link. Nine risk categories, an honest "genuinely unsolved" section — but nothing about deliberate death/rebirth economics.
- **Arithmetic that doesn't reproduce**: "Circular transfers, the simplest farming tactic, lose money: 30 circular transfers of 100 ATP shed ~150 ATP in fees." I tried to verify this with the convention /why-web4 taught me (the transferred amount shrinks: 100 → 95 → 90.25 → …). Under that convention, a 100 ATP ball bounced 30 times sheds 100 × (1 − 0.95³⁰) ≈ 78.5 ATP — and can *never* shed 150, since total fees asymptote at 100. The ~150 figure only works if every one of the 30 transfers is a fresh flat 100 ATP (30 × 5 = 150), i.e. the farmer tops the amount back up each time from some other pool. The page doesn't say that. Same site, two circular-transfer illustrations, two unstated accounting conventions.

### A Day in Web4 — /day-in-web4 (55:00)
- The five UI mockups are the most "I can picture using this" moment on the site. Costs varying by sender trust (expert 3 ATP, low-trust 15 ATP) is intuitive immediately.
- **The camera scene is silent about the fee I was just taught.** The market mockup sells a camera for 350 ATP with "Escrow cost 2 ATP (reduced - high seller trust)." /atp-economics says "5% is skimmed on every transfer" — on 350 ATP that's 17.5 ATP, which is nearly 9× the escrow cost the scene *does* itemize. Does the buyer pay 367.5? Does Nina receive 332.5? The page mentions no fee at all. Itemizing the 2 ATP escrow while omitting a 17.5 ATP fee reads like an ad showing the price without tax.
- To its credit, when I wondered how anyone affords 350 ATP starting from 100, the page had already fenced it: "How someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question on this stack rather than a solved one, and you should read it as one." Honest. I'll take it.

## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest, and that honesty is its best feature. Three separate times I formed a skeptical question and found the site had pre-fenced it with an explicit "this is unsettled" sentence (karma magnitude, first-commission bootstrapping, issuance rates). The Running Now page's badge discipline — including admitting "Running" means four lab machines — bought a lot of my goodwill for the more speculative pages.

The writing quality is high and the recommended path works: 2 Minutes → Why → First Contact → How It Works is a genuine ramp, each page assuming roughly what the previous one taught. The plain-English glosses on acronyms mostly rescue the acronym density, though /why-web4 still fires four of them in close succession.

Where the site let me down was *numbers at the seams*. Within any single page the arithmetic checks out (I verified several by hand: the three-life ATP ledger, the 7× quality gap, 0.9⁵ ≈ 0.59). But across pages, numbers stop talking to each other: the walkthrough's rebirth outruns the newcomer grant with no bridge; the market mockup forgets the fee the economics page calls universal; the risk page's fee arithmetic silently switches conventions from the FAQ's worked example. A visitor who does the math — and this site's audience will — hits these seams exactly where the site is trying to prove the system is exploit-resistant. The death-farming question is the worst of these, because the site *teaches* me to think in incentives for four straight pages and then ends its flagship walkthrough with an incentive it never closes.

I'd come back. I want to read /trust-tensor and /karma-consequences, and I want to know if the rebirth question has an answer.
