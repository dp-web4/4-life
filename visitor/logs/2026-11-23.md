## Understanding Gained
By the end of this session, I understood:
- [x] What Web4 is (at a high level): a proposed standard where trust comes from witnessed, signed records of behaviour instead of a platform's say-so or possession of a key. The site taught me this.
- [x] What the onramp is (the core standard, the hub, hestia, hardbound) and how they fit together: one vocabulary, three sizes to run it at (personal, community, enterprise). Taught by /tldr and /onramp.
- [x] What LCT means: an identity that lives on your device, ideally in its security chip, vouched for by your other devices. Taught. (The identifier format I did not get.)
- [~] What ATP/ADP means: an energy budget you spend to act and get back when others confirm value; ADP is the receipt. The idea, yes. How much comes back, by which formula, and whether supply is capped: no.
- [x] What Trust Tensors are: three scores (talent, training, temperament) per role. /trust-tensor taught this well. V3 (the work's score) I half got; who assigns it I did not.
- [x] What is real and runnable today vs still R&D: yes, clearly. This is the site's strongest suit.
- [~] Why any of this matters: the problem statement convinced me. Whether this design solves it I cannot judge, and the site mostly says nobody can yet.

## Honest Assessment
The site is unusually honest and I believe it. Every page tells me what is not done. Running Now, What Could Go Wrong, and the bottom of each onramp page are better at this than most real products. If the goal is "do not oversell", it is met.

The story also works. After the TL;DR and Why Web4 I could explain to a friend what the problem is and what the idea is: trust from witnessed behaviour, an identity anchored in your device, an energy cost on every action, trust scored per role. I got that from the site, not from guessing.

Where it falls apart is the numbers. Almost every time a page gives me a worked example, I can find another page (sometimes the same page) whose example gives a different answer, and often a small grey note acknowledging that. The quality ramp table does not follow its own rule. The Novice cannot climb that fast. Karma is +12 here and −15 there. Earnings have two formulas. The 0.7 is canonical in the glossary and a teaching number on its own page. Visibility is described six ways. After the fourth or fifth of these I stopped trying to do the arithmetic and started treating every number as decoration. That is a shame, because the site clearly wants me to be able to check its sums.

A related feeling: the disclaimers have started to outnumber the claims. "One modelling choice", "not settled", "filed with the maintainers", "this site's calibration, not the standard's", "read the shape, not the amount". Each one is honest. Together they make me wonder what, concretely, is being proposed. A page that says "here is the rule, and here is the one thing about it that is undecided" would teach me more than a page with three illustrations and three caveats.

The life / death / rebirth / karma language is vivid and I remember it, but I never settled whether it is a metaphor for "balance hit zero, account suspended" or something more. For a person, "you die" is a strange thing for a reputation system to say, and the glossary quietly says humans cannot be reborn anyway.

Would I come back? If I were someone who runs AI agents on my own machine, the Hestia page would bring me back; it is the one place I could picture myself doing something. As a curious reader, I got the idea in the first twenty minutes and the following eighty mostly subtracted confidence.
