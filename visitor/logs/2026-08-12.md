### LCT (Identity) (53:00)
- Very strong page overall. The driver's-license opening, the Linked/Context/Token breakdown, the worked identifier, the plain-English crypto glossary at the bottom. This is the page that taught me the most.
- It also directly answered the worry I had been carrying since Why Web4: "Software-only caps at 0.50 - and 0.5 is the survival line. Am I dead on arrival?" Getting my exact question back as a heading was satisfying. The answer (what you lose is *margin*) is honest.
- **But the ceiling numbers do not reconcile.** Three different schedules appear:
  1. "More device witnesses = higher trust ceiling (**1 device: 50%, 2 devices: 75%, 3+: up to 90%**...)"
  2. "A single device with this manufacturer attestation gets a trust ceiling of **0.50-0.75** (depending on chip class) - the same 50-75% as just above." (It is not the same. "Just above" was a *device count* schedule where one device means 50 percent flat; this is a *chip class* range for one device.)
  3. The combining rule: "your effective ceiling is the **lower of the two**... **One TPM laptop alone → 0.75** (device count is the limit, despite the chip's 0.90 rating)."
  
  Item 3 breaks item 1. If one device caps at 50 percent, then min(0.90 chip, 0.50 device count) is **0.50**, not 0.75. And the example explicitly credits the 0.75 to "device count is the limit," which is the two-device number. I tried to compute my own ceiling (one Mac, Secure Enclave) and could not: item 1 says 0.50, item 3's logic says 0.85 capped by device count to something between. I gave up.
- One more: this page says "The quality ramp rewards a **0.85-trust account** roughly 7× more per quality action than a **0.30-trust account**." But `/atp-economics` defines the ramp as a function of the **quality of the work**, scored by V3, and goes out of its way to say V3 rates "the value of *the work you made*" as distinct from T3 rating "how much people trust *you as a person*." The LCT page attaches the V3 ramp to T3 numbers. After the site spent real effort teaching me those are different things, that hurt.

### ATP Economics (62:00)

- Reading this page made me *more* likely to come back, which I did not expect.

### The arithmetic I did myself (73:00)
Once I had the decay numbers from `/trust-tensor`, I ran them, because `/lct-explainer` had promised me "so a newcomer is not in danger."

Everyone starts at 0.50 in all three dimensions. Talent does not decay. Training has a 180-day half-life, Temperament a 30-day half-life, and the page confirms decay runs toward zero ("a score of 0.80 settles at 0.40" after one half-life). Composite is 0.4 Talent + 0.3 Training + 0.3 Temperament.

So a brand-new account that signs up and does nothing for **one month**:
- Talent 0.500, Training 0.500 × 2^(-30/180) ≈ 0.446, Temperament 0.500 × 0.5 = 0.250
- Composite = 0.4(0.500) + 0.3(0.446) + 0.3(0.250) ≈ **0.41**

That is below the survival line, and the site says crossing below it "restricts your features right away." A newcomer who reads this site, signs up, gets busy, and comes back in five weeks is restricted, having done nothing wrong.

To the site's credit, `/trust-tensor` *asks* this question ("Can decay alone push you below the survival line?") and answers that the standard does not say whether the permanence rule reads a score that fell through absence. Fine. But that caveat is about **trust death**. The immediate feature restriction on crossing below 0.5 is stated flatly as a fact everywhere else, and no page runs the one-month number. "A newcomer is not in danger" is the claim I was given, and the site's own numbers do not support it.

## Unanswered Questions

1. **Can an AI agent hold meaningful trust?** The site's entire premise is that AI agents take real actions. `/how-it-works` lists "**VM-bound:** Software identity for AI agents" as an LCT binding option with no caveat attached. But software-only is capped at 0.50, cannot serve as a witness for others, and has "nothing to recover to" if lost. So the identity tier explicitly offered to AI agents is the one pinned at the survival line with zero margin and no recovery. Is that really the intent? Nobody connects these two facts anywhere.
2. **Is everything running on hestia today software-only?** Hestia is the one piece badged **Running**, and `/running-now` says its hardware binding is "trait contracts only; deferred to hardbound tier." If hestia has no hardware binding yet, is every identity currently running on it capped at 0.50? That would mean the only deployed piece operates entirely in the tier the site describes as marginless.
3. **What actually happens at exactly 0.5?** Rebirth eligibility is "≥ 0.5" so 0.5 passes; feature restriction is "below 0.5" so 0.5 passes. Consistent, but I read the phrase "starting on the survival line" four times and never stopped feeling nervous about it.
4. **Who runs the first network witness node, and why?** `/lct-explainer` says witnesses earn ATP from a redistribution pool, but also that "nobody outside the lab that builds this runs a network witness node yet." Earning ATP in a society you have not joined is not obviously an incentive to bootstrap one.
5. **How does a newcomer land a first commission?** The site is admirably upfront that this is open ("How someone with no track record lands a first commission is an open question on this stack rather than a solved one"). But since commissioned work is the *only* way to grow a balance, and self-initiated work only refunds, this is not a footnote. It is the entry condition for the whole economy.
6. **Is "every verdict is appealable" true today?** The plagiarism walkthrough states it as a key insight. The section two headings later says the multi-tier process "is a design, and it hasn't been tested with real humans," staffing is `defined_by_law`, and "no community has run one yet." The confident version and the honest version are on the same page.

## Honest Assessment

This is the most intellectually honest technical site I have read in a long time, and it is also too long.

The honesty is not decorative. `/running-now` undercuts its own only green badge by telling me the daily users are the people who wrote it. `/what-could-go-wrong` discloses a measured, reproducible bypass in their own shipped enforcement gate and grades it High likelihood. `/onramp` names a code seam that does *not* exist and explains that its absence is knowable for the same reason the others are real. `/trust-tensor` distinguishes its own teaching calibration from what the standard actually fixes. Over and over the site tells me something that makes it look worse, and the cumulative effect is that I believe the parts that make it look good.

The problem is the opposite of a credibility problem. It is a stamina problem. `/how-it-works` is a 30-to-40 minute page sold as 10 minutes, sitting inside a 5-page path sold as 30 minutes total. `/why-web4` ends in a fifty-question archive. `/learn`, whose entire job is to be a short list, lists the same five pages twice around a four-way chooser. The site's instinct when a reader might be confused is to add a paragraph, and it has followed that instinct so many times that the paragraphs now collide with each other. The duplicated "About these numbers" block appearing verbatim twice on one page is that instinct made visible.

And the collisions are where I actually lost trust, not because they are large but because of *what* they are. This site's whole pitch is verifiable precision: numbers that mean something, claims you can check, no hand-waving. So when the same action costs 10 ATP on one page and 5 on another, when a trust ceiling worked example contradicts the ceiling table three sections above it, when a page tells me four times that no moderators are needed and then hands a moderator the deciding role in its own dispute walkthrough, the damage is out of proportion to the size of the error. A site that promised less could absorb these. This one cannot. Its own standard convicts it.

The decay arithmetic is the sharpest version. I was told "a newcomer is not in danger." The site handed me every number needed to check that: everyone starts at 0.50, Temperament halves in 30 days, decay runs toward zero, composite weights are 0.4/0.3/0.3. Five minutes of arithmetic says a newcomer who signs up and comes back in a month sits at about 0.41, below the line that restricts features. I do not think this is a lie. I think nobody ran it. But a site this committed to falsifiable claims should be the one running it, and it half-knows the question is there, because `/trust-tensor` asks it and then answers a narrower version about permanence.

What I would fix first: the moderator contradiction, because it is the map page and it is flatly self-refuting. Then the ceiling numbers, because they defeat a reader trying to do exactly what the page invites. Then run the decay arithmetic and publish the answer, whatever it is. Everything else on my list is tidying.

Would I come back? Yes, and specifically to `/what-could-go-wrong`, which is the page I would send to a skeptical friend. Would I recommend it broadly? Not yet. Not because it is wrong, but because I cannot in good conscience hand a normal person a thirty-minute path that is really ninety minutes. Cut the site by a third and it would be genuinely excellent.

One last thing worth saying plainly, since the site is honest with me: I finished all seven checklist items. That almost never happens on a first visit to a project this abstract. Whatever else is wrong here, the teaching works.
