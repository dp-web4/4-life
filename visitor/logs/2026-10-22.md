## Honest Assessment
This site is unusually honest. Nearly every page says what is not built, who the "Running" users actually are (the lab's own four machines), and which numbers are teaching calibrations rather than protocol constants. /tldr, /running-now and /onramp are genuinely good. By the end I trusted the authors' intentions more than I trust most product sites.

The problem is that the honesty is spread across caveats, and the *numbers and illustrations* don't agree with each other. Whenever I did arithmetic with the site's own rules, it broke. The Novice can't buy a dozen actions with 60 ATP. The quality-ramp table fits neither its stated shape nor its prose. Karma has opposite signs for two similar deaths. The site's best-trusted example falls below the survival line after a long vacation. Several basic claims also flip between pages: whether out-of-network people can see you, whether out-of-network actions cost energy, whether ATP is capped, whether CI decides life or death, and how many mechanisms there are. Some of these flip within a single page.

As a newcomer I can't tell which version is the real one, so I end up believing the caveats ("this is unsettled") more than the mechanics. That is the opposite of what a teaching site wants. Many of the caveats are "one modelling choice, not the rule" notes stapled onto examples that contradict each other. It would be clearer to pick one model for the examples and put the "this is unsettled" note in one place.

The deepest question I'm leaving with isn't a wording issue. By the site's own rules, newcomers start exactly on the death line. Self-started work can only lose energy. Software-only users (which today means every hestia user) can never rise above where they started. I came in thinking "earned trust" was the pitch. I leave unsure that a new, chip-less person can earn anything under these rules, and the site seems unsure too.
