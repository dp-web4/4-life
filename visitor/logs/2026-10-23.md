### /trust-tensor (~55:00)
- Talent / Training / Temperament per role is the most intuitive idea on the site. The surgeon/mechanic examples work, and the composite arithmetic checks out (89.5, 74.3, 27.5).
- The rule: `0.02 × (quality − 0.5)`. At the best possible quality (1.0) that's +0.01. **So Alice's +0.02 for one post and +0.03 for one mentoring session can't come from one action.** Then I found "each confirmer's V3 feeds the formula independently," so maybe several readers confirming one post adds up. But /what-could-go-wrong says trust moves by that amount "**per action**... not to who vouched for you. A founder and a newcomer doing equally good work move by the same amount." Per action or per confirmer? If it's per confirmer, the person with more readers climbs faster, and the "founder and newcomer move the same" promise breaks. If it's per action, First Contact's steps are impossible. Why Web4's "each quality contribution moves the needle by ~0.02" sides with neither.
- **Decay vs the death line.** Temperament has a 30-day half-life, and the example decays it toward zero ("0.80 settles at 0.40"), not back toward 0.5. I did the arithmetic for a brand-new member at 0.5/0.5/0.5 who takes one month off: Temperament ≈ 0.25, Training ≈ 0.445, Talent 0.5. Composite = 0.4(0.5) + 0.3(0.445) + 0.3(0.25) ≈ **0.41, under the survival line**, from one month away. The Trust Tensor page does raise this ("Can decay alone push you below the survival line?... not settled... filed with the standard's maintainers"). But the Why Web4 "month off" FAQ reassures me instead ("recovers quickly... A few days of active contribution restores confidence"). And "Temperament's 30-day half-life works in both directions" doesn't fit a rule that adds at most +0.006 per confirmation: going from 0.25 back to 0.5 at that rate is 40+ perfect confirmations, not "a few days."
- "A software-only identity is capped at that same midpoint." So a software-only person starts at 0.50, can never go above 0.50, and dies below 0.50.

## Honest Assessment
The first ten minutes are genuinely good. The landing page and /tldr gave me a crisp problem and a crisp pitch, and Web2/Web3/Web4 in three boxes is the best explanation on the site. Running Now is unusually honest; I trusted the project more after reading it.

The trouble starts when I tried to put numbers together. The site invites this: it shows formulas, worked examples and half-lives, and says "if you can do the arithmetic above, you can reach that question yourself." So I did, and a lot of it doesn't close. One action's trust step is either +0.01 max or +0.03. The quality ramp's table, prose and example each describe a different curve. The Novice's spend can't cover a dozen actions. One month off puts a newcomer under the death line, while the FAQ says a few days fixes it. Running out of energy earns +12 karma in one story and −15 in another. Each page I read added a new thing to keep straight, and some of them contradict each other.

The bigger pattern is that the same idea is stated in two directions on different pages, and sometimes on the same page. Can strangers see you? Does reaching them cost energy? Is ATP capped? Is CI 0.5 alive/dead? Are there four systems or five? Each one on its own is a small edit. Together they made me stop trusting the specifics and fall back on the gist.

Second, the caveat density. I can tell the authors care about not overclaiming, and the "this is not settled" notes are honest. But by How It Works, nearly every section had a box saying the example I just read isn't how it works, that another page models it differently, or that the cause shown doesn't exist. I started skipping the boxes, and that's exactly where the real answers were. A newcomer can't tell which numbers to hold onto. The ATP page's "settled vs still moving" list is the right idea; I wish every page did that instead of caveating inline.

Last, the thing I'll remember: if I only have a laptop, I start at 0.50, I'm capped at 0.50, and below 0.50 is fatal. The site says this is a known hole, and I respect that. But the one product badged "Running" is a software identity whose page advertises "trust that evolves," and nothing on that page connects the two. As a first-time visitor that's the gap I'd most want closed: tell me what Web4 does for me *today, with what I have*.
