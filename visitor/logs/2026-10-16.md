## Unanswered Questions
1. If I install hestia on a laptop with a TPM, is my identity at the 0.90 TPM tier (LCT page) or the 0.50 software tier (hestia says hardware binding is "trait contracts only")? I think the answer is 0.50 today, but no page says so in one sentence.
2. Who decides an action is "spam" before its cost is charged? Costs are charged at action time; quality is judged afterwards. First Contact charges 25 for spam and 8 for a quality post.
3. How long can raw trust sit below 0.50 before "stays below" is fatal? For a software-only identity the margin is zero, so this is the only question that matters at that tier.
4. Is federation built or not?
5. If the lab's fleet is "a live Web4 society", why is the hub not Running and why is there "no live network with real users"? Are machines not users?
6. Is a software-only user's rebirth even possible? Rebirth needs composite ≥ 0.5 and software-only caps at 0.50 exactly. Any decay at all makes them ineligible.
7. Is Alice's Trust Score panel the role-weighted match or the fixed composite?

## Honest Assessment
The site is unusually honest about what is real, and that honesty is what makes the contradictions stand out: when a page tells me "the present tense is the model talking", I believe it, and then I expect the model to be one model. It isn't, yet. The same four or five mechanisms are described with different visibility rules, different spam prices, different rebirth outcomes, and a different count on consecutive pages of the recommended path.

Three things I'd want fixed before sending a friend here, in order:

1. The MRH visibility and cost story. It flips on three pages, and twice within a single page. A new reader cannot tell whether strangers see them or not, or whether talking to a stranger is free, cheap, or very expensive.
2. The death-and-rebirth story. First Contact and the karma page teach opposite lessons about whether dying with a decent record is rewarded or punished. The "amount not settled" disclaimers are honest but they are covering a sign flip, not a magnitude.
3. What a software-only identity can actually do. Every concept page assumes a hardware anchor; the only shipping product has none; and the pages that admit this (hestia's caveat, the accessibility risk, the LCT "dead on arrival" box) are not where the headline claims ("Trust that evolves", "Bad behavior follows you") are.

What worked: the problem framing, the two-number panel on First Contact, "recharge refunds, payment earns", the LCT birth-certificate analogy, the 0.7-per-hop arithmetic, the badge key once it got its "because" clause, and every paragraph that named a missing seam or an open question. I left knowing what Web4 is, why someone would want it, and roughly what exists. I did not leave with one consistent model of how it works, because the site has more than one.
