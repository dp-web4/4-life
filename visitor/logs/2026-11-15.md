## Journey

### Landing Page (00:00)

- Clicked: "Web4 in 2 minutes", as told.

### Web4 in 2 Minutes (/tldr) (02:00)
- Very good. The Web2 / Web3 / Web4 contrast (platform *declares*, key holder *is assumed*, behavior is *witnessed*) is the clearest thing on the site.
- The badge key (Spec / Reference / Running) is useful. The note that "Running" means the lab's own daily use, not adoption, is honest and I appreciated it.

### Why Web4? (05:00 to ~40:00)
- The problem section is excellent: five problems, each with a "root cause". The "What it gets right / Failure" treatment of earlier solutions felt fair, not salesy.
- The four ideas (energy, device identity, multi-dimensional trust, neighborhood) arrive one at a time with plain-English names before the acronyms. Good.
- **First crack:** the "All four, in one moment" box says *"only people inside the asker's trust neighborhood see your reply at all - a stranger five hops away never sees the thread."* Two cards earlier the Neighborhood card said *"Everyone stays visible - what changes is weight. A stranger's message reaches you."* A few scrolls later the FAQ says *"Everyone can see them - nothing is hidden or censored."* Which one is it? The FAQ later gives a fourth version: "people with no connection path aren't shown it by default."
- The FAQ is huge (72 questions). Each answer is honest on its own, but the answers contradict each other when you read many of them:
  - **Developer FAQ:** "Is there an SDK? **Not yet.** … not the 'npm install web4' stage. There's no public SDK or API today." But the landing page, the 2-minute page and Running Now all tell me to `pip install web4-core` and `cargo add web4-core`, and call the primitives "installable now".
  - **Scarcity:** the FAQ "what backs ATP" lists **"Capped issuance: A community mints a finite ATP pool … ATP matters exactly because it's limited."** The ATP page says **"This is not a currency. No scarcity cap, no mining."**
  - **The 0.5 death line:** the teenager FAQ says "trust below 0.5 = permanent death … **But this threshold is high; ordinary teenage mistakes don't approach it.**" Everyone *starts* at 0.50 (stated about five times), so the line is not "high". It is exactly where I begin. And per the trust rule, one action below quality 0.5 moves me under it.
  - **Trust step size:** the bootstrap FAQ says "each quality contribution moves the needle by **~0.02**", and "Why these numbers" calls 0.02 "the trust update step". The actual rule on the Trust Tensor page is `0.02 × (quality − 0.5)`, so the biggest possible step is **+0.01** (at perfect quality), and +0.007 at a typical good 0.85.
  - **Tier numbering:** one answer says "Tiers 0-3 are reversible - only Tier 4 (full native) is permanent", which makes Wrapper Tier 0. Another says "What **Tier 1** actually looks like: … At the Wrapper tier" and "Tier 2 (Observable)", which makes Wrapper Tier 1. Five tiers, two numbering schemes.
  - **Anonymous vs published:** "Who builds this? Why anonymous? … a project about trust that doesn't tell you who's behind it." Running Now and Hub say "Which lab, and who is in it, is checkable … members and roles are published at 4-lab.io/fleet."
  - **Disposable identity:** the privacy table says "Starting fresh: disposable identity is **prevented by design**." Elsewhere on the same page: software-only "the record stays sheddable", and "an attacker can still mint thousands of identities cheaply".
  - "What would a Web4 app look like? … Right now, Web4 exists as a research prototype - **the site you're on**." The 2-minute page said this site is "explainers, nothing you install", and that hestia is *Running*. Is the prototype this site or hestia?
  - Minor: "After just **4 round trips**, they've lost nearly 19%". The example shows 4 *transfers* (2 round trips). The 19% is right for 4 transfers.
- Clicked: "Start First Contact".

### First Contact (~40:00)

### How It Works (~50:00 to ~75:00)
- The page says "Five interlocking systems", "five foundational mechanisms", "How All Five Systems Create Aliveness". Then, further down the same page, "How The Pieces Fit Together: **Web4 has four core systems**." Why Web4 said "four ideas" and called CI "not a fifth mechanism". I don't know whether it is four or five.
- The CI example in the overview: "suddenly starts posting crypto spam at 3am **from a new country**, their coherence drops." The VPN/Tor FAQ said "'Spatial' here means scope of activity … **not where your device is** … CI measures what you do, not where you connect from." The CI page then has "Vacation in Tokyo (CI dips)", "impossible travel", and pseudocode with `location: gps_coords`. So does CI read my location or not?
- **The Novice life cannot close:** "spent 60 ATP … T3 → 0.65 … the sum across a dozen+ actions". Posts cost 10-20 ATP, so 60 ATP buys 3 to 6 posts. The update rule gives at most +0.0082 composite per perfect-quality action (0.01 × (0.4 + 0.3·0.8 + 0.3·0.6)). Climbing 0.15 needs **at least 19 perfect actions**, or ~26 at quality 0.85, and the page says temperament did not rise, which makes it worse. Neither "a dozen+" nor "60 ATP" fits.
- Spam pricing: "Spam message: Cost 5 ATP" and "Send 20 spam messages = -100 ATP", while a post costs 10-20 ATP. Spam is the *cheapest* thing you can do that affects others, cheaper than an honest post. It still loses, but on a page whose thesis is "make bad behavior expensive" that reads backwards.
- "Actions outside your MRH don't cost energy and don't move trust" (diagram) vs. "reading costs about 1 ATP … so scraping a million posts is not free" and "Crossing boundaries costs ATP (spam costs real energy)" (MRH page). Do actions outside my neighborhood cost energy or not?
- The federation paragraph says "Federation across societies remains **Spec only**, specified but not yet built". The Why Web4 FAQ listed under "What's built and tested": "Reference implementations: **tested code covering** … federation", plus "federation consensus" under System integration.
- Positive: the agent-pipeline section with its explicit "two different numbers, not two rates for one thing" note was clear, and I didn't confuse 0.9^5 with the 0.7 hop decay.

## Honest Assessment
The site's voice is unusually honest. Almost every page tells me what is not built, which numbers are calibration, and which questions are still open upstream. The opening five-page path works well for the *why* and the *what's real*. I came away clearly able to explain the problem, the four pieces, and the maturity of each. Running Now and Hestia made me believe there is real software here.

The site fails me when I try to use it as a model, by doing the arithmetic or comparing two pages that describe the same mechanism. The honesty is applied per page and per answer, and the pages don't agree with each other. The biggest example is visibility: the site's central anti-spam story ("strangers can't reach you" vs. "everyone stays visible") changes from box to box, sometimes within one page. The second is the numbers. The trust update rule is stated precisely, and then the Novice life, the bootstrap FAQ and the "0.02 step" all imply steps twice as big or more. The quality ramp table fits neither of its own descriptions. Karma has opposite signs for the same death. CI is called an "alive/dead" line on the same page that says it isn't one.

Each page has hedges ("illustrative", "one modelling choice", "not settled") that partly cover these. But the hedges are about *amounts*. The contradictions I hit are about *direction*: visible or invisible, capped or uncapped, location or not, penalty or bonus, SDK or no SDK. A careful reader stops trusting the mechanics even while trusting the authors' intentions. For a site about verifiable, consistent records, the inconsistency between its own pages undercuts the message.

If I could fix three things: (1) one visibility story, (2) one ATP-scarcity story, and (3) make the worked lives (Novice, First Contact, karma Life 1) obey the stated rules.
